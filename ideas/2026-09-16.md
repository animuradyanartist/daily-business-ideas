# The Small Water Treatment Plant's "State-Ready" Monthly Operating Report (MOR) Generator

_2026-09-16 · conviction: high · score: 100/100_

## The idea
A software-as-a-service (SaaS) tool that automates the creation of state-mandated Monthly Operating Reports (MORs) for small public water systems. The product provides simple forms for operators to log daily process data (e.g., flow rates, chemical usage, turbidity). It then automatically performs all required calculations and generates a pixel-perfect, compliant PDF of the official state MOR form, ready for submission. It is sold as a monthly subscription starting at $299/month.

## Who pays and why
The buyer is the licensed operator or superintendent of a small community water system, typically one serving a population between 500 and 10,000 people. There are over 18,000 such systems in the US. These operators are personally responsible for compliance, and their professional license is at risk if reports are inaccurate or late. They gather and build trust through state-level Rural Water Associations. They pay to eliminate the tedious, error-prone manual work of spreadsheet-based reporting and, most importantly, to mitigate the catastrophic risk of non-compliance, which includes heavy fines and potential license revocation.

## Problem proof
The pain is the recurring, high-stakes, and time-consuming administrative burden of generating mandatory compliance reports using inadequate tools like spreadsheets.

*   **Direct buyer quotes:**
    1.  **Waterly Customer (2026):** "I don't love technology, but I love Waterly. Anyone can use it.” This highlights the need for simple, usable tools for a non-technical user base.
    2.  **Waterly Customer (2026):** “No more dozens of spreadsheets. With a single pane of glass for my water and wastewater data, my staff can now more easily trend data to explain patterns." This directly confirms the current use of spreadsheets and the pain of a fragmented, manual process.
    3.  **Waterly Founder describing customer feedback (2026):** "Everyone essentially said ‘it is nearly impossible to keep up with what I'm supposed to do/measure/report on’." This speaks to the overwhelming complexity and cognitive load of the compliance requirements.

*   **Prevalence:** 97% of the 145,000+ public water systems in the US are classified as "small," serving 10,000 or fewer people. These small systems account for 80% of Safe Drinking Water Act violations, often due to a lack of resources and technical expertise, not a lack of care. The existence of dedicated forums and blogs for operators to discuss compliance demonstrates the widespread nature of these challenges.

*   **Cost of Failure:** The cost is severe. The EPA can levy civil penalties of up to $25,000 per day for each violation. State agencies also issue significant fines; Oregon recently fined a small city over $10,000 for monitoring violations and failure to submit reports. A small city in Texas faced a $24,500 penalty for similar violations. The ultimate cost is license revocation, which is a career-ending event for the operator.

*   **Frequency:** This pain is acute and recurs every single month when the MOR is due. The process is described by software vendors as a move away from "dozens of siloed spreadsheets" and "days to produce reports."

## Demand signals
Money is actively flowing to software that solves this exact problem, and operators are spending significant sums on worse substitutes like manual labor and consultants.

1.  **Established Point Solutions:** Companies like Waterly and H2Ometrics have built businesses specifically to solve this data management and reporting pain for small-to-mid-sized utilities. Waterly explicitly targets rural and small systems and has over 1,000 systems using its platform. H2Ometrics markets itself as a way to "say goodbye to endless spreadsheets and number crunching."
2.  **Value-Based Pricing:** Waterly's pricing is based on the utility's flow rate, which is a direct proxy for the value and compliance burden. This confirms the market accepts value-based pricing tied to the scale of the operation, rather than per-seat pricing.
3.  **Spend on Worse Substitutes (Manual Labor):** The primary alternative is manual data entry and calculation in spreadsheets. A licensed operator's time is valuable, with average salaries ranging from $56,000 to over $90,000. At a conservative loaded rate of $40/hour, an operator spending just 10 hours a month on this report "costs" the utility $400/month in labor, justifying a $299/month software subscription.
4.  **Spend on Worse Substitutes (Consultants):** Environmental consultants are hired to prepare or review compliance reports. This service can cost between $1,000 and $15,000+ per report. Hourly rates for consultants average $135-$160/hr. This establishes a very high price anchor for a software alternative.

**Wallet verdict: PROVEN DEMAND**. The existence of multiple, successful SaaS competitors targeting this niche, combined with the high cost of manual labor and consultant alternatives, proves that small utilities are actively paying to solve this high-stakes, recurring compliance problem.

## Why now
1.  **Workforce Transition:** The water utility workforce is aging, with up to 3 million workers needing replacement in the next decade. A new generation of operators is less tolerant of manual, paper-based workflows and expects modern, digital tools.
2.  **Increased Regulatory Scrutiny:** State and federal regulators are increasing enforcement and demanding more granular data. Recent, highly-publicized fines for reporting violations are creating market-wide awareness and urgency to adopt more reliable compliance systems.
3.  **Digitization of Small Utilities:** Small utilities, historically underserved by technology, are now actively seeking affordable, simple digital tools to solve their most pressing operational challenges, a trend identified and served by companies like Waterly.

The market is a large, fragmented base of small, quasi-governmental entities with a mandatory, high-stakes operational requirement and a low tolerance for complex software.

*   **TAM/SAM/SOM:**
    *   **TAM:** The global water and wastewater management market is valued in the billions.
    *   **SAM:** There are ~18,300 community water systems in the US serving populations between 501 and 10,000. At an annual price of $3,600 ($300/month), the Serviceable Addressable Market is **~$65.9M annually**.
    *   **SOM (Year 3):** Capturing 1.5% of this market (275 systems) × $3,600/year = **~$990k ARR**.
*   **Fragmentation:** The buyer base of over 18,000 small systems is highly fragmented, making it difficult for large enterprise software companies to serve effectively. This creates an opening for a focused, product-led or inside-sales approach.
*   **Margin pool:** The value is captured by de-risking the operator's personal license and saving 10-20 hours of their valuable time per month. A $3,600/year tool that prevents a single $10,000+ fine and saves $4,800+ in annual labor has a clear ROI.
*   **Incumbents & Gaps:** Large asset management systems (e.g., from Oracle, IFS) are too complex and expensive for this segment. Newer players like Waterly are proving the model but cannot serve all 50 states' unique reporting formats simultaneously. The gap is for a product that focuses intensely on perfecting the compliance reporting workflow for a single state initially, then expanding.

*   **Payback:** ~$2,500 CAC / ($350 * 0.90) = ~8 months.
*   **Capital Intensity:** Low. A solo founder can build a single-state MVP in 4-6 months.
*   **Moat Thesis:** The primary moat is **high switching costs** created by data lock-in; once years of compliance data are in the system, migrating is risky and difficult. A secondary moat is **regulatory complexity**; mastering and maintaining the specific reporting formats and rules for 50 different states is a significant barrier to entry.

## Scope of work
The MVP is a "report generator," not a full data management system.

*   **IN for v1:**
    *   **Single-State Focus:** Supports only the MOR for one target state (e.g., Texas or Wisconsin).
    *   **Manual Data Entry:** Simple, mobile-friendly forms for daily data input.
    *   **Core Calculations:** Automatically performs all state-mandated monthly calculations (totals, averages, etc.).
    *   **Pixel-Perfect PDF Generation:** The core feature is a downloadable PDF that is an exact replica of the official state MOR form, filled and calculated.
    *   **Single-Plant, Single-User:** Built for one operator at one plant.

*   **OUT for v1:**
    *   **Multi-State Support:** Deferred. This is the expansion path.
    *   **Direct E-Filing:** User downloads the PDF and submits it themselves.
    *   **SCADA/LIMS Integration:** All data is entered manually.
    *   **Analytics & Alerting:** No dashboards or trend analysis. Focus is 100% on the report.

## Validation plan
The biggest assumption is that a small operator will go through a procurement process for a SaaS tool. The test must de-risk this by getting commitment.

1.  **Target State:** Select one state with a high number of small systems and an active Rural Water Association (e.g., Texas, Wisconsin, Pennsylvania).
2.  **Build:** Create a high-fidelity, interactive prototype of the single-state MOR generator using Figma or a similar tool.
3.  **Outreach:** Contact the executive director of the state's Rural Water Association. Offer to provide a free, 30-minute educational webinar for their members on "5 Common Errors in MOR Reporting and How to Avoid Them."
4.  **Offer:** At the end of the webinar, present the prototype and offer a "Founding Member" pilot program: the first 10 systems to sign up get the tool for free for 6 months in exchange for feedback, followed by a locked-in 50% discount ($149/mo) for life.
5.  **Thresholds:**
    *   **GO:** 5+ systems sign up for the pilot program with a signed letter of intent.
    *   **REFINE:** 1-4 systems sign up. Conduct deep interviews to understand hesitation (price, features, trust).
    *   **PAUSE:** 0 sign-ups. The pain is not strong enough to overcome inertia or the sales channel is wrong.

## Red-team and rebuttal
1.  **Attack: The market is too slow and cheap.** Small municipal sales cycles are notoriously long, and these entities have no money.
    *   **Rebuttal:** This is true for large, complex software. This tool is priced to fit within an operator's discretionary budget or be a simple line item. The sale is to the operator, not a committee. The slowness of the market is also a moat, deterring venture-backed competitors who need rapid growth.
2.  **Attack: Spreadsheets are good enough.** The risk of fines is low in reality, and a spreadsheet is free and familiar. This is a vitamin, not a painkiller.
    *   **Rebuttal:** This underestimates the operator's personal career risk. Their *license* is their livelihood. The pain is not just the fine, but the professional anxiety of non-compliance. The software is selling certainty and peace of mind to the individual responsible, a true painkiller for them.

## Risks and kill criteria
1.  **Channel Risk:** State Rural Water Associations are gatekeepers. If they are unwilling to partner or promote the solution, reaching operators will be slow and expensive.
2.  **Budget Authority Risk:** The operator may love the tool but lack the authority to purchase it, getting stuck in municipal procurement hell.
3.  **Inertia Risk:** The pain of switching from a familiar spreadsheet, even a bad one, may be greater than the perceived pain of the status quo.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If, after 3 months of focused effort in a single state, it is not possible to get 5 pilot users to sign a (non-binding) letter of intent to use the software, kill the project. This indicates a failure to overcome one of the core risks.

## What I learned today
- The most powerful B2B software opportunities often involve de-risking an individual's professional license, not just the company's finances.
- A market of many small, fragmented, non-technical buyers (like small utilities) is a feature, not a bug. It creates a natural barrier against large enterprise competitors and rewards simple, focused product design.
- Regulatory complexity that varies state-by-state can be a powerful moat. Building a business that masters this complexity creates a durable competitive advantage.

## Why this beats yesterday's idea
Yesterday's idea, the H-2A Visa Compliance Hub, was excellent because it addressed a critical labor input risk. Today's idea is slightly stronger because the consequence of failure is more direct and personal to the buyer. For the farm, H-2A failure means losing their workforce—a business catastrophe. For the water operator, reporting failure means fines *and* potentially losing their personal license to practice their profession—a career catastrophe. This personal, existential risk for the buyer creates an even more intense and less price-sensitive purchasing motivation.
