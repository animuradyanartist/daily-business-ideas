# The Small Government Contractor's "DCAA-Proof" Timesheet Hub

_2026-10-01 · conviction: high · score: 95/100_

## The idea
A simple, standalone software-as-a-service (SaaS) tool for small government contractors (5-50 employees) that provides an auditable, "DCAA-proof" timesheet system. The software replaces inadequate solutions like spreadsheets or generic time trackers with a purpose-built hub that enforces Defense Contract Audit Agency (DCAA) timekeeping rules. It ensures every time entry is compliant, auditable, and defensible, sold on a per-user, per-month subscription starting at $15/user/month.

## Who pays and why
The buyer is the owner, CEO, or CFO of a small business that has won, or is trying to win, a U.S. government contract. This includes contracts that are cost-reimbursable, Time & Materials (T&M), or subject to progress payments based on cost. There are at least 65,500 such small business contractors in the U.S.. They gather in subreddits like r/GovernmentContracting and are identifiable through public award databases.

This buyer pays to mitigate the existential risk of failing a DCAA audit. A failed audit can lead to termination of existing contracts, repayment of funds, financial penalties, and debarment from winning future government work. The pain is not inefficiency; it is catastrophic business failure.

## Problem proof
The pain is the non-negotiable, complex, and high-stakes requirement of maintaining a DCAA-compliant timekeeping system. Generic tools and manual processes are often insufficient and create significant audit risk.

*   **Direct buyer quotes:**
    1.  **Reddit User, r/GovernmentContracting (Apr 2025):** "Any recommendations on one that doesn't cost a fortune? We have a fixed monthly contract and we are sub to a prime so we just need a basic timekeeping software in case of an audit. Deltek/unanet seem like over kill since we just have four employees."
    2.  **Deltek Marketing Material (May 2025):** "Don't risk your firm's future using software that can't support DCAA... compliance requirements. Now is the time to make the move - delaying the decision until you've won a Cost-Plus or defense contract, or are in the midst of an audit, may be too late."
    3.  **Industry Consultant (Mar 2022):** "A failed DCAA audit can have disastrous consequences for your organization... This can lead to an inability to procure future government contracts, issues with receiving payments... [and] damaged organizational reputation."

*   **Prevalence:** The problem is universal for any contractor bidding on or holding flexibly priced contracts. A key step in this process is the SF-1408 Pre-Award Survey, which assesses the adequacy of a contractor's accounting system, including timekeeping. DCAA rules require daily time recording, supervisor approvals, and a complete, unalterable audit trail for any changes—features often missing from generic software.

*   **Cost of Failure:** The cost of non-compliance is catastrophic. A failed audit leads to disallowed costs, where the government refuses to pay for improperly documented work. This can trigger contract termination, repayment of previously awarded funds, and suspension or debarment from future federal work.

*   **Frequency:** The risk is constant. Timekeeping policies must be enforced daily. The threat of an audit—whether a pre-award survey, an in-process floor check, or a post-award incurred cost audit—is always present.

## Demand signals
Money and effort are actively flowing to mitigate this risk, primarily through expensive, complex ERPs or by trying to adapt cheaper, non-compliant tools.

1.  **Spend on Enterprise ERPs:** The market incumbents, Deltek and Unanet, serve tens of thousands of government contractors. Deltek Costpoint can cost a 10-user firm $800/month plus significant implementation fees. Unanet's pricing is also in the premium tier. This proves a very high willingness to pay for a compliant solution.
2.  **Spend on Point Solutions:** A market of smaller, focused timekeeping tools like TimeRewards and Hour Timesheet exists, with pricing from $9 to $19 per user per month. This validates the demand for unbundled, affordable solutions.
3.  **Adapting Generic Tools:** Generic trackers like Clockify have added "DCAA-compliant" features like audit logs to their highest-priced tiers ($12-15/user/mo) to capture this market segment, proving the demand extends beyond purpose-built tools.
4.  **Pre-award System Requirement:** The SF-1408 form requires contractors to prove they have an adequate accounting system *before* winning a contract. This creates a hard gate and forces a purchasing decision, turning the "insurance" into a prerequisite for revenue.

**Wallet verdict: PROVEN DEMAND**. The market spends hundreds of millions of dollars annually on expensive ERPs and dedicated point solutions to solve this exact, non-negotiable compliance problem.

## Why now
1.  **Flight to Quality:** The number of small businesses in federal contracting has declined by nearly 40% over the last decade, while the total contract value awarded to them has increased. This indicates market consolidation, where fewer, more serious firms are winning larger contracts, increasing the compliance burden and the need for professional tools.
2.  **Incumbent Complexity & Pricing:** The dominant ERPs like Deltek and Unanet are increasingly complex and expensive, creating a significant gap at the low end of the market for a simple, affordable, best-in-class point solution. Small businesses are actively seeking alternatives to these "overkill" solutions.
3.  **Remote Work:** The shift to remote and hybrid work makes manual or spreadsheet-based timekeeping systems even more fragile and difficult to audit, increasing the need for a robust, digital-native solution.

## Market structure
The market is a large, fragmented base of SMBs facing a high-consequence, legally mandated operational requirement, dominated by overpriced and overly complex incumbents.

*   **TAM/SAM/SOM:**
    *   **TAM:** The U.S. federal government contracting market is over $793 billion annually.
    *   **SAM:** There are at least 65,500 active small business federal contractors. Assuming an average of 15 employees per firm at $15/user/month, the Serviceable Addressable Market is **~$177M annually**.
    *   **SOM (Year 3):** Capturing 1% of this market (655 firms) would result in **~$1.77M ARR**.
*   **Fragmentation:** The market is highly fragmented, with tens of thousands of small contractors across all 50 states and numerous industries. This makes it ideal for a product-led or inside-sales motion and difficult for high-touch enterprise sales teams to serve efficiently.
*   **Margin pool:** The value is captured by de-risking the contractor from catastrophic business failure. The software's cost is a rounding error compared to a potential contract termination or debarment.
*   **Incumbents & Gaps:** Deltek and Unanet are the incumbents. Their strength is their all-in-one ERP offering for larger contractors. Their weakness is their high cost and complexity, which creates a classic unbundling opportunity for a simple, affordable tool that does one job perfectly for the underserved small business segment.

## Scope of work
*   **MVP (in):** A web app where an admin can add employees and projects/charge codes. Employees can log in daily to fill out a simple timesheet. The system must enforce daily entries, capture digital signatures, require a reason for any changes, and maintain a complete, unalterable audit trail. Supervisor approval workflow. Exportable reports for audit purposes.
*   **MVP (out):** Invoicing, expense tracking, payroll integration, mobile app, advanced reporting.
*   **Milestones:**
    *   Weeks 1-2: Customer discovery calls with small govcon owners to validate MVP scope.
    *   Weeks 3-10: Build and test the core application.
    *   Week 11: Onboard first 3 beta customers for free.
    *   Week 12: Launch.
*   **Resources:** One full-stack developer. Minimal capital required. Time-to-first-revenue: ~3 months.

## Validation plan
1.  **Target:** Find 50 small government contractors (CEOs, owners) on LinkedIn or from public award data.
2.  **Offer:** Create a simple landing page with the headline: "The DCAA-Compliant Timesheet for Small Contractors That Isn't Deltek." Detail the core features (audit trail, daily lockouts, supervisor approval) and a price point of $15/user/month.
3.  **Action:** Drive traffic via cold outreach and targeted ads. The call to action is to join a waitlist for the first 10 beta slots.
4.  **Thresholds:**
    *   **GO:** >5% waitlist conversion rate (3+ signups). This signals the pain and positioning resonate.
    *   **REFINE:** 1-2 signups. Re-interview them to understand what parts of the message worked and what didn't.
    *   **PAUSE:** 0 signups. The core assumption is wrong. Kill the idea.

## Sales playbook
1.  **Pricing strategy:** Launch at $15/user/month for the first 100 customers. Raise to $19/user/month after. Offer a simple annual discount.
2.  **Top 3 sales channels:**
    *   **Content Marketing:** Write definitive blog posts on "DCAA timekeeping requirements" and "How to pass an SF-1408 survey." Rank for these terms.
    *   **LinkedIn Ads:** Target users with job titles "CEO," "Owner," "President" at companies in NAICS codes common for government services (e.g., 541511, 541330) with <50 employees.
    *   **r/GovernmentContracting:** Participate authentically. When users ask for software recommendations, mention the product.
3.  **Hook copy:**
    *   **V1:** Headline: DCAA-compliant timesheets without the Deltek price tag. Sub-headline: Get the audit-proof timekeeping system you need to win government contracts, for a price that a small business can actually afford.
    *   **V2:** Headline: Stop dreading the DCAA audit. Sub-headline: The simple, standalone timesheet hub that makes compliance easy for small government contractors.
4.  **Pre-launch → launch → sustained:**
    *   **Pre-launch (Day -30):** Run validation test. Start writing first 5 SEO blog posts.
    *   **Launch (Day 0):** Announce on LinkedIn and relevant subreddits. Email the waitlist.
    *   **Sustained (Day 30+):** Publish one new SEO-focused article per week. Run continuous, low-budget LinkedIn ad campaign.
5.  **Top 2 buyer objections:**
    *   "We're too small, we just use spreadsheets." Rebuttal: "Spreadsheets work until your first audit or your first big contract bid. Our system is the cheapest insurance you can buy for your company's future."
    *   "How do I know this is *really* compliant?" Rebuttal: "The DCAA doesn't certify software, they audit your process. Our software is built from the ground up to enforce their rules—daily entries, audit trails for changes, and supervisor approvals—so your process is always defensible."
6.  **Social proof strategy:** The first 10 customers are "Founding Members." Get testimonials from them focused on "peace of mind" and "ease of setup" compared to alternatives they considered. Display these on the homepage.

## Red-team and rebuttal
1.  **Attack:** The market is shrinking; the number of small business contractors has fallen 40% in a decade.
    *   **Rebuttal:** This is a flight to quality. Fewer, more serious contractors are winning larger contracts, which increases their compliance burden and makes them *better* customers for this tool. The pain is concentrating, not disappearing.
2.  **Attack:** This is a feature, not a business. QuickBooks or Clockify will just add this and kill you.
    *   **Rebuttal:** They have, but compliance is a feature on their most expensive tiers and isn't their core focus. For a risk-averse buyer facing a catastrophic audit failure, a purpose-built, trusted solution will always be preferred over a generic tool where compliance is an afterthought. We win on trust and focus.
3.  **Attack:** The audit risk is a boogeyman; most small firms fly under the radar.
    *   **Rebuttal:** This misreads the pain. The pain isn't just the audit; it's the *inability to bid* on lucrative contracts without first having a compliant system, as certified on the SF-1408 pre-award survey. It's a gate to revenue, not just a hypothetical risk.

## Risks and kill criteria
1.  **Risk: Market Apathy.** The target customer is content with "good enough" spreadsheets and doesn't perceive the risk to be acute enough to pay for a solution pre-emptively.
    *   **Kill:** The validation test fails to get any signups.
2.  **Risk: Channel Failure.** The target buyer is not reachable through content or paid ads, making customer acquisition costs too high.
    *   **Kill:** After 3 months and $5k in ad spend, CAC is higher than 12-month LTV.
3.  **Risk: Under-built Product.** The minimum feature set for a "defensible" audit trail is more complex than anticipated, leading to product gaps and churn.
    *   **Kill:** The first 10 beta customers report that the tool, while simple, would not give them confidence in an audit.

## What I learned today
- A shrinking number of market participants is not always a negative signal; it can indicate a "flight to quality" where the remaining players are more professional, have larger budgets, and feel compliance pain more acutely.
- The most powerful B2B pain is not just a risk of loss, but a hard gate to future revenue (e.g., needing a compliant system to even bid for a contract).
- Incumbent marketing material is a powerful source for validating the customer's core pain points and fears. Deltek's copy about "risking your firm's future" is a direct signal of the emotional core of the problem.

This opportunity in government contracting is superior for three reasons: 1) The market is significantly larger, with over 65,000 small business contractors compared to ~12,000 clinical research sites. 2) The buyer is easier to identify through public databases of federal contract awardees (e.g., SAM.gov). 3) The pain is tied directly to winning contracts (a revenue-enabling function), not just post-award audit defense, making it an even more urgent purchase.
