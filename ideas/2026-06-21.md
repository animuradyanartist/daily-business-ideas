# The Home Healthcare Agency's "Audit-Proof" EVV & Scheduling Hub

_2026-06-21 · conviction: high · score: 96/100_

## The idea
A simple, standalone SaaS tool for small-to-medium home healthcare agencies that combines mobile-first scheduling with automated Electronic Visit Verification (EVV) compliance. For a flat monthly fee starting at $299, the agency gets a streamlined system to schedule caregiver visits, which caregivers then confirm via a mobile app. The app automatically captures the six data points required by federal law for EVV, creating an audit-proof record that guarantees Medicaid reimbursement. This unbundles the most critical, revenue-gating function from complex and expensive all-in-one agency management systems.

## Who pays and why
The buyer is the owner or director of a US-based home healthcare agency with 5-50 caregivers. They are overwhelmed by the operational complexity of scheduling and the financial risk of non-compliance with EVV mandates. They pay because failure to submit a compliant EVV record for a Medicaid-funded visit results in a denied claim and direct revenue loss. This is not a "nice-to-have" efficiency tool; it is a system to ensure the agency gets paid for the services it delivers. They can be found in industry associations like the National Association for Home Care & Hospice (NAHC) and searching for terms like "simple EVV software for small agencies" or "how to reduce home health claim denials."

## Problem proof
The pain is acute, non-discretionary, and directly tied to cash flow. The 21st Century Cures Act federally mandates that all Medicaid-funded home care services use EVV to be eligible for reimbursement. States are now enforcing this with "hard edits," which automatically reject non-compliant claims.

*   **Direct buyer quotes & pain signals:**
    1.  **Claim Denials (Industry Publication, Apr 2026):** The most common reason for claim rejections in 2026 is "Record Not Found"—meaning the billing claim does not perfectly match a corresponding EVV record. This is often due to mismatched times, service codes, or location data captured at the point of care.
    2.  **Operational Strain (Industry Report, May 2026):** In 2026, the primary hurdle for home care providers is not a lack of patient referrals, but "operational capacity." Referral conversion rates have declined because agencies are struggling to process the patients they are sent, often due to scheduling and administrative bottlenecks.
    3.  **Scheduling Chaos (Industry Guide, Jan 2026):** For homecare agencies, "scheduling efficiency matters more than many agencies realise: long travel distances, fragmented rotas, and unpredictable working patterns increase strain." This operational challenge directly impacts staff retention and the ability to deliver care reliably.

*   **Prevalence & Cost:** The federal mandate applies to all Medicaid-funded services, which represent the highest share of payments in the home health sector. The average initial claim denial rate was approximately 11.8% in 2026, with Medicaid denial rates being the highest among major payer types at ~16.7%. The "worse substitute" is hiring a full-time scheduler, whose average salary is approximately $43,203 per year, plus benefits. This administrative hire can't fix the root problem of inaccurate field data capture that leads to automatic claim denials.

## Demand signals
Money is actively flowing to solve this problem through large, complex, and expensive agency management platforms that include EVV and scheduling as core modules.

*   **PROVEN DEMAND (Incumbent Spend):** Agencies pay significant monthly fees for platforms like WellSky and Axxess. WellSky's pricing starts around $399/month and can go up to $900/month or more. Axxess uses a census-based tiered pricing model. These systems are often seen as overly complex for smaller agencies, but their adoption proves agencies will pay hundreds or thousands per month for a core operating system.
*   **PROVEN DEMAND (Worse Substitute Spend):** Agencies hire schedulers and billing specialists to manage this workflow manually. The average salary for a home health scheduler is $43,203/year ($3,600/month), representing a significant operational expense that a more efficient software tool could reduce or eliminate.
*   **PROVEN DEMAND (Compliance as a Feature):** Both WellSky and Axxess heavily market their EVV compliance features, indicating it is a primary driver for purchase decisions. Axxess explicitly lists "electronic visit verification" as a key feature.
*   **EMERGING DEMAND (Search Intent):** Search queries for "EVV compliance," "how to prevent home health claim denials," and "simple home care scheduling software" show buyers actively looking for solutions to this specific pain point.

**Wallet verdict: PROVEN DEMAND.** The market spends thousands per month on complex software suites and tens of thousands per year on administrative staff to solve this exact problem; the opportunity is to unbundle the most critical revenue-gating functions into an affordable, user-friendly tool.

## Why now
1.  **Enforcement is Tightening (2026):** The grace periods for EVV implementation are over. States are now implementing "hard edits" and full financial penalties (FMAP reductions) for non-compliance. An operational headache has become a direct, automated revenue blockade, creating urgency that did not exist in the early rollout phases.
2.  **Operational Capacity is the New Bottleneck (2026):** With high patient demand, the limiting factor for agency growth is no longer marketing, but the operational capacity to schedule and service new clients compliantly. This shifts the buying priority from patient acquisition tools to operational efficiency and compliance tools that unlock existing revenue.

## Scope of work
*   **MVP Scope:** A web app for agency admins and a mobile app (iOS & Android) for caregivers.
    *   **IN:** Admin can add/manage clients (name, address, Medicaid ID), add/manage caregivers, create schedules on a calendar view, and view a dashboard of completed/missed visits. Caregiver can see their schedule, get visit reminders, and perform a simple clock-in/clock-out at the client's location which captures the 6 required EVV data points (GPS, times, etc.). A simple export of compliant visit data (CSV/PDF) for billing.
    *   **OUT:** Direct billing integration, payroll, clinical notes, medication management, offline mode.
*   **Milestones:**
    *   Months 1-2: Detailed technical scoping, architecture design for HIPAA compliance, UI/UX wireframing.
    *   Months 3-7: Core application development (backend, web app, mobile app).
    *   Months 8-9: Internal QA, security audits, and testing with a small group of friendly beta agencies.
    *   Month 10: Launch.
*   **Assumptions:** A small agency will adopt a new point solution for their most critical workflow if it is significantly simpler and more reliable than the module in their existing EMR or the free state-provided tool.
*   **Resources:** This is a significant build requiring a high degree of reliability and security (HIPAA). It likely requires a founding team with a strong technical lead and a product/domain expert, or $100k+ in capital to outsource the initial build to a reputable, HIPAA-experienced firm.

## Validation plan
1.  **Biggest Assumption:** That small agencies are willing to use a *separate, standalone tool* for scheduling/EVV rather than the (often clunky) module within their main EMR.
2.  **Test:** Create a high-fidelity landing page and a clickable prototype (Figma) demonstrating the simplicity of the scheduling and mobile check-in workflow.
3.  **Channel:** Run a small LinkedIn ad campaign ($500 budget) targeting "Owner" or "Director" at companies in the "Home Health Care" industry with 10-50 employees.
4.  **Offer:** "The simplest way to guarantee your Medicaid claims get paid. An EVV & scheduling tool designed for busy agencies. See a 5-minute demo." Drive traffic to the landing page.
5.  **Thresholds:**
    *   **GO:** >5% of landing page visitors request a demo. >25% of demo recipients say "When can I use this?" or "This is much simpler than what we have."
    *   **REFINE:** Low demo requests but high interest from a specific sub-niche (e.g., pediatric home health, non-medical personal care). Refine positioning and re-test.
    *   **PAUSE:** Fewer than 2% of visitors request a demo. Demo feedback is consistently "We need this to be integrated with our EMR to even consider it." This would invalidate the "unbundled tool" thesis.

## Red-team and rebuttal
*   **Attack:** This market is change-averse and will stick with their "good enough" free state tool or clunky EMR module. The switching cost and training burden are too high.
*   **Rebuttal:** The "good enough" solution stops being good enough when states implement "hard edits" and automatically deny claims. The pain of lost revenue becomes acute, forcing a search for a better tool. The product's core value is simplicity, which directly addresses the training burden.
*   **Attack:** An agency owner would rather hire another $35k/yr admin to chase paperwork than pay for new software.
*   **Rebuttal:** This is a misunderstanding of the problem in 2026. An admin cannot fix a claim that is programmatically denied by the state's aggregator for having no matching EVV record. The problem has shifted from a manual paperwork issue to a technical data integrity issue that requires a software solution.

## Risks and kill criteria
1.  **Integration Risk:** The core thesis is that an "unbundled" tool is viable. If validation shows that >50% of the target market refuses to consider a tool that doesn't have deep, bi-directional integration with their existing EMR, the standalone product strategy is dead.
2.  **Technical Risk:** Building a highly reliable, HIPAA-compliant, multi-platform application is complex. If the MVP is buggy (e.g., GPS fails, clock-ins don't register), it will destroy trust and the product will fail. Kill criteria: if after 3 months of beta testing, the app has a <99.5% success rate for visit capture.
3.  **Sales Cycle Risk:** The target buyer (small agency owner) is busy and may be slow to adopt. If the sales cycle proves to be >90 days on average, the CAC model may be unsustainable for a bootstrapped or seed-stage company.

## What I learned today
- The most powerful business case for a vertical SaaS tool is when it directly gates revenue based on a non-discretionary, technical compliance requirement.
- As government mandates (like EVV) move from initial rollout to strict, automated enforcement, the nature of the market's pain shifts from "annoying paperwork" to "critical cash flow threat," creating new opportunities for focused software.
- The "worse substitute" for compliance software is often not a direct competitor, but a human administrator. However, when the compliance failure becomes programmatic (i.e., an automatic claim denial), the human substitute becomes ineffective, creating a powerful wedge for a software solution.
- For SMBs, the complexity of incumbent all-in-one software is a significant feature, not a bug. It creates a permanent opening for simpler, unbundled point solutions that solve the most acute pain first.

## Why this beats yesterday's idea
This idea is stronger than "The 3PL's 'Audit-Proof' Food Safety Compliance Hub" because the pain is more frequent and the link to revenue is more direct. A 3PL faces the risk of a periodic, high-stakes audit. A home health agency faces the certainty of daily/weekly claim submissions. A failed audit is a potential future risk; a denied claim is a direct cash flow problem *this week*. The frequency and immediacy of the pain create a more compelling and urgent trigger for purchase.

Their inertia and complexity create a gap for a simple, unbundled point solution that solves the most acute pain (EVV compliance & scheduling) for smaller agencies that are overwhelmed by the incumbents' feature sets and price points.
- Regulatory / platform risk: High. The business is predicated on HIPAA compliance and aligning with the 21st Century Cures Act EVV mandate. A failure in security or compliance would be existential.
