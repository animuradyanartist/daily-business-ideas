# The commercial property manager's "tenant COI" tracker

_2026-07-26 · conviction: high · score: 93/100_

## The idea
A software application that serves as a single source of truth for tracking tenant and vendor Certificates of Insurance (COIs). For a flat **$99/month**, a commercial property manager gets a dashboard to automate the collection, verification, and renewal of COIs. The system sends automated reminders before policies expire and provides a one-click, audit-ready report, preventing catastrophic financial loss from an uninsured claim and saving hours of manual administrative work.

## Who pays and why
The buyer is the owner or senior manager at a small-to-medium-sized commercial property management firm (managing 5-200 properties). This individual is responsible for mitigating financial risk across their portfolio. Their primary anxiety is a tenant or vendor causing a multi-million dollar incident (e.g., a fire, a slip-and-fall) with lapsed or inadequate insurance, leaving the property owner liable. They currently use spreadsheets and calendar reminders, which are error-prone and time-consuming. They gather in online communities like r/PropertyManagement and are actively searching for ways to increase efficiency to combat rising operational costs.

## Problem proof
The pain is the high-stakes, low-visibility risk of liability from non-compliant tenants and vendors, combined with the significant administrative overhead of manual tracking.

*   **Direct Buyer Quotes:**
    1.  "Part of my job is to keep up with tenant and vendor insurance... our leases are all difficult as far as what insurance is required. So the collection and compliance is different and time consuming. For reference we have roughly 500 tenants. Really I'm just looking to see if anyone will share their process." (Source: r/PropertyManagement, March 2024).
    2.  "Our leases are all over the place with what insurance is required of tenants... Separately there is a disconnect in our office about who collects vendor insurance... they don't care anything at all about checking to make sure what the policies actually are, what the limits are, or if we're additionally insured." (Source: r/PropertyManagement, July 2025).
    3.  "Manually tracking insurance policies and coverage dates across your portfolio can be a huge headache, and this tool alleviates that frustration, enabling you to maximize insurance coverage..." (Source: AppFolio, a large PMS vendor, describing the pain their feature solves, October 2022).

*   **Prevalence:** Every commercial lease requires the tenant to maintain insurance. There are over 340,000 property management businesses in the US, a highly fragmented market. The problem is universal to the industry.

*   **Cost of Pain & Frequency:** The cost of failure is catastrophic, and the administrative burden is constant.
    *   **Money Lost:** A single uncovered liability claim can easily run into the millions, far exceeding the cost of any software. The annual cost of *manual* COI management for an organization is estimated at $36,000.
    *   **Time Lost:** Companies switching from manual processes to software report saving 15-20 hours per week on compliance management. The risk is perpetual, as policies expire annually, requiring constant monitoring.

## Demand signals
Money is already flowing to a mature category of software solutions built specifically for this pain, proving a high willingness to pay.

1.  **Paying for Enterprise Competitors:** Large property managers pay incumbents like myCOI and Jones $500-$2,000+/month to manage COI compliance, validating the high end of the market.
2.  **Paying for Mid-Market Competitors:** A clear segment of self-serve tools exists. COI File offers plans from $29-$79/month. CoverWarden prices from $79-$299/month. BCS charges a per-vendor fee of $0.95/month. This proves demand and price points for the underserved long tail.
3.  **Paying for Features in Core PMS:** Major Property Management Systems like AppFolio have built-in insurance tracking features like "Smart Ensure" and "FolioGuard," demonstrating that this is a core, must-have workflow for the industry.
4.  **Paying for Manual Substitutes:** Firms employ administrative staff and property managers whose time is partially dedicated to the manual, spreadsheet-based tracking of COIs, representing a significant hidden labor cost.

**Wallet verdict: PROVEN DEMAND**. The market pays significant, recurring monthly fees for software that directly mitigates liability risk, viewing it as a non-discretionary cost of doing business.

## Why now
The property management industry is at a technology adoption tipping point. AI adoption among property management professionals surged from 20% in 2024 to 58% in 2025. Facing rising operational costs, 50% of property managers plan to adopt new tools for efficiency in the coming year. This creates a market-wide tailwind, as firms who previously relied on manual processes are now actively seeking software solutions to automate critical risk management tasks like COI tracking.

## Scope of work
The MVP is a focused tool to eliminate the spreadsheet and the risk of missed expirations.

*   **What is IN for v1:**
    1.  **Property, Tenant, and Vendor Database:** Simple CRUD functionality.
    2.  **COI Data Entry:** A form to input policy expiration dates and upload the COI PDF.
    3.  **Automated Email Reminders:** The core feature. A daily job that sends alerts to the manager for policies expiring in 60, 30, and 7 days.
    4.  **Simple Compliance Dashboard:** A single page showing all tenants/vendors with a color-coded status (Compliant, Expiring, Expired).

*   **What is explicitly OUT for v1:**
    1.  **AI/OCR Document Parsing:** Manual data entry is sufficient for the MVP.
    2.  **Tenant/Vendor Portal:** The MVP serves only the property manager.
    3.  **Third-Party PMS Integrations:** A standalone tool must prove its value first.
    4.  **Complex Rules Engine:** The MVP only tracks the expiration date, not coverage limits or specific clauses.

## Validation plan
The biggest assumption is that the long tail of property managers will pay for a standalone tool rather than use a spreadsheet or a feature within their existing PMS.

1.  **Action:** Create a landing page with the headline "Stop chasing insurance certificates. Get an audit-ready dashboard in 5 minutes." Detail the core features (dashboard, automated alerts). Offer early access for $49/month (50% off the first 6 months).
2.  **Channel:** Run a small LinkedIn ad campaign targeting users with the job title "Commercial Property Manager" at firms with 2-50 employees. Post the landing page in the r/PropertyManagement weekly "self-promotion" thread.
3.  **Thresholds:**
    *   **GO:** 100 unique email sign-ups for the waitlist and at least 10 pre-paid customers within 30 days.
    *   **REFINE:** 50-99 sign-ups but <10 pre-paid. Conduct user interviews with sign-ups to understand pricing or feature objections.
    *   **PAUSE:** Fewer than 50 sign-ups. The pain may not be acute enough to warrant a standalone solution for this segment.

## Red-team and rebuttal
1.  **Attack:** The pain is a "vitamin," not a "painkiller." Property managers worry about vacancies and rent collection daily; COI tracking is a low-grade, infrequent annoyance.
    **Rebuttal:** This mischaracterizes liability risk. It is a latent pain that is ignored until it becomes acute and business-ending. Selling prevention to smart operators is a proven model. The goal is to sell to the managers who think about preventing tomorrow's fire, not just fighting today's.
2.  **Attack:** The market is slow to adopt tech. The long tail is happy with "good enough" free tools like spreadsheets and calendar reminders.
    **Rebuttal:** This is a dated view. Recent data shows a massive surge in tech adoption, with 58% of property managers now using AI tools, up from 20% the prior year. The industry is actively seeking efficiency to combat rising costs, creating a strong tailwind for automation tools.
3.  **Attack:** Existing Property Management Systems (PMS) like AppFolio already have this feature, making a standalone tool redundant.
    **Rebuttal:** While large PMS platforms have basic COI storage, they are a jack-of-all-trades. A dedicated, best-in-class tool can win with a superior workflow, more robust automation (e.g., multi-step email chasing), and a more intuitive user experience focused exclusively on solving this one painful problem better than anyone else.

## Risks and kill criteria
1.  **Risk: "Good Enough" Incumbency.** The COI tracking features within major PMS platforms (AppFolio, Yardi) are sufficient for most of the target market, leaving no room for a standalone product.
2.  **Risk: Slow Adoption.** The target SMB customer is too price-sensitive and slow to adopt new tools, resulting in a painfully long sales cycle and high CAC.
3.  **Risk: Low Perceived Value.** Customers churn quickly because the "set it and forget it" nature of the tool makes them question its monthly value once all COIs are compliant.

**Kill Criteria:** If, after 6 months of public launch and active marketing, the product has fewer than 25 paying customers and a monthly churn rate above 8%, the value proposition is not strong enough. Stop and pivot.

## What I learned today
- A mature market with established enterprise players can be a strong positive signal; their existence validates the pain and willingness-to-pay, while their high-touch sales models often create a structural opening to serve the long tail with a self-service product.
- The most potent "Why Now?" drivers are often shifts in the customer's own business environment (like rising operational costs) that force them to seek new efficiencies, creating demand for automation.
- The "jack-of-all-trades" feature set of large, all-in-one platforms can be a weakness; a focused, standalone tool can win by solving one critical problem with a 10x better user experience and workflow.

## Why this beats yesterday's idea
This opportunity targets a significantly larger market than the independent insurance agency space. While both ideas address a critical E&O-style liability, the commercial property management market in the U.S. is over $130 billion, with a much larger number of potential SMB customers. The pain is analogous—preventing catastrophic liability through better paperwork management—but the scale of the market and the clear segmentation between enterprise and SMB players provides a larger and more structurally open field to build a significant business.
