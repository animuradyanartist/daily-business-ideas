# The DME supplier's "get paid by Medicare" evidence locker

_2026-08-09 · conviction: high · score: 98/100_

## The idea
A SaaS tool for independent Durable Medical Equipment (DME) suppliers that acts as an evidence locker to prevent and fight Medicare claim denials. The product is a simple, mobile-first application that ensures every order has the required documentation—such as the physician's order, proof of delivery (POD), and evidence of medical necessity—collated and validated *before* the claim is submitted. It is priced from $199 to $499 per month based on claim volume.

## Who pays and why
The buyer is the owner or billing manager of an independent DME supply company. These are businesses providing patients with home-use equipment like wheelchairs, oxygen concentrators, and hospital beds. They are typically small, local operators under intense financial pressure from low reimbursement rates and complex regulations. They gather in communities run by industry associations like AAHomecare and read trade publications like HME News.

They pay because Medicare denies a staggering number of their claims due to missing or insufficient documentation, directly threatening their cash flow and survival. The pain is not abstract; it is a government-quantified, line-item risk to revenue.

## Problem proof
The pain is the direct, frequent, and high-cost denial of revenue from Medicare due to preventable documentation errors.

*   **Direct buyer quotes:**
    1.  "The number one reason DME claims get denied isn't the billing itself, but it's the documentation behind the claim... A lot of providers assume that if the doctor ordered the equipment, they're covered. That's not how Medicare or most commercial payers see it." (Aayur Solutions, Jul 2026)
    2.  "Most denials happen not because equipment isn't needed, but because Medicare's documentation and coverage rules weren't fully met." (Complete Care, Inc, May 2026)
    3.  "Durable medical equipment (DME) billing continues to be one of the most intricate and regulated areas and DME providers end up facing a lot of complications... popular friction points that DME providers need to be careful of include: Strict documentation standards." (SunKnowledge, Jan 2026)

*   **Prevalence signals:** The U.S. government's 2025 Comprehensive Error Rate Testing (CERT) report found the improper payment rate for DME was 24.1%, an increase from the prior year. This means nearly a quarter of all claims are initially paid in error, primarily due to documentation issues. The leading causes are consistently cited as "insufficient documentation," "missing physician orders," and lack of "medical necessity" proof.

*   **Cost of pain:** Reworking a single denied claim costs between $25 and $118 in administrative time. Critically, an estimated 65% of denied claims are never resubmitted, resulting in a permanent revenue loss. For a small supplier, a 24.1% error rate on their primary payer is an existential threat.

## Demand signals
Money is already flowing to expensive, high-friction alternatives (billing services and complex all-in-one software) to manage this specific pain.

1.  **Spend on All-in-One Software:** Suppliers pay significant amounts for complex DME management platforms like Brightree and WellSky's Bonafide. Brightree can cost a 15-person team $2,000–$3,000 per month plus implementation fees. WellSky's solutions start at approximately $1,500/month or involve setup costs from $20,000 to over $120,000. This proves a high willingness to pay for software that promises to solve billing and documentation challenges.
2.  **Spend on Outsourced Billing Services:** A large market of specialized DME billing companies exists (e.g., Medbill, Aayur Solutions) that charge a percentage of collections. This model directly ties their fee to solving the "get paid" problem, proving suppliers will pay a premium for expertise in navigating documentation and submission rules.
3.  **Emergence of Focused Software Tools:** Newer, cloud-based competitors like NikoHealth are gaining traction by offering more streamlined, all-in-one platforms focused on automating workflows from intake to payment. Their existence validates the market's demand for modern, focused solutions over legacy systems.
4.  **High Cost of Manual Substitutes:** The primary alternative is hiring more billing staff. With the average accounts receivable cycle for manual DME providers at 65 days versus under 40 for automated ones, the cost of labor and delayed cash flow is a significant, ongoing expense that justifies software investment.

**Wallet verdict: PROVEN DEMAND**. The market spends tens of thousands of dollars per year per company on oversized software suites and outsourced services to solve this exact revenue-denial problem, creating a clear opening for a more focused and affordable SaaS tool.

## Why now
The pressure on independent DME suppliers is intensifying. The 2025 CERT report showed an *increase* in the DME improper payment rate for the first time since 2015, to 24.1%. This heightened scrutiny from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) is a powerful forcing function. In response to the high error rates, CMS is directing its contractors to conduct more widespread pre-payment reviews, meaning more suppliers will have their cash flow delayed while awaiting documentation approval. Furthermore, in February 2026, CMS announced a temporary moratorium on the enrollment of new DME suppliers, citing widespread fraud and abuse. This increases the operational burden and compliance risk for existing, legitimate suppliers who are now under a microscope.

## Market structure
The market is a large, fragmented base of independent operators being squeezed by powerful external forces (government regulation and reimbursement cuts), making them ideal customers for a tool that directly protects revenue.

*   **TAM/SAM/SOM:**
    *   **TAM:** The U.S. DME market is valued at $74.62 billion in 2026. All participants are subject to Medicare billing rules.
    *   **SAM:** As of January 2026, there are approximately 8,078 traditional DMEPOS supplier locations in the U.S. While this number has been consolidating, thousands of independent operators remain. Targeting 4,000 of these independent suppliers who feel the pain of denials most acutely, at an average price of $3,600/year ($300/month), yields a serviceable addressable market of **$14.4 million annually**.
    *   **SOM:** A 2-year goal is to capture 2.5% of this market (100 suppliers), generating **$360,000 in ARR**.
*   **Fragmentation:** The market consists of thousands of small, local businesses. This structure makes it difficult for expensive, high-touch enterprise software to serve them effectively, opening a gap for a low-touch SaaS product.
*   **Incumbent Gap:** Incumbents are large, complex, and expensive all-in-one platforms like Brightree and WellSky. The substitute for many small suppliers is manual labor. The opportunity is a simple, standalone tool hyper-focused on solving the single most expensive part of the workflow: claim-evidence readiness.

*   **Pricing:** Three tiers: $199/month (Starter, <100 claims/mo), $349/month (Pro, <300 claims/mo), $499/month (Growth, >300 claims/mo). This is priced as a utility, significantly below the cost of one reworked claim per day or the price of an all-in-one suite.
*   **Gross Margin:** ~90%, standard for B2B SaaS.
*   **CAC/LTV:** A target CAC of $1,200 (via targeted content marketing, trade publication ads, and partnerships with billing consultants) and a 4-year LTV of $16,752 (based on $349/mo ARPA) yields an LTV/CAC ratio of ~14.
*   **Moat:** The initial moat is focus and simplicity. The long-term moat is built by becoming the system of record for compliance evidence. Expansion comes from analyzing denial data across the customer base to provide predictive insights ("claims with this missing document from Dr. X have a 90% denial rate") and then expanding into adjacent evidence-management workflows (e.g., audit defense).

## Scope of work
The MVP is a focused "evidence locker" web and mobile application.

*   **In:**
    *   Create a new order/patient record.
    *   Checklist of required documents for common DME items (e.g., Oxygen, CPAP, Wheelchairs) based on Medicare rules.
    *   Upload functionality for each required document (e.g., scan/photo of Signed Physician Order, Proof of Delivery, Certificate of Medical Necessity).
    *   A simple dashboard showing all orders and their documentation status (Incomplete, Ready for Billing).
    *   Mobile app for delivery drivers to capture POD signatures and photos in the field.
*   **Out:**
    *   Direct integration with billing software or clearinghouses.
    *   Inventory management.
    *   Patient scheduling.
    *   Full EMR/EHR functionality.
*   **Milestones:**
    *   **Month 1:** Detailed customer interviews with 10 DME billing managers to map the precise evidence workflow. Build clickable prototypes.
    *   **Months 2-4:** Build and test the core web app and mobile POD capture app with a small group of beta users.
    *   **Month 5:** Launch with first paying customers.
*   **Resources:** Requires one full-stack developer with mobile experience and deep product/domain research by the founder. Initial capital is low, focused on development.

## Validation plan
The biggest assumption is that DME suppliers will use a *standalone* tool instead of the features within their existing (likely clunky) system.

1.  **Test:** Create a landing page with the core value proposition: "The $199/mo tool that makes sure you have every document Medicare requires *before* you bill. Stop writing off denied claims."
2.  **Channel:** Run a small LinkedIn ad campaign targeting job titles like "Billing Manager" and "Owner" at companies with "Durable Medical Equipment" in their profile.
3.  **Thresholds:**
    *   **GO:** 10+ demo requests or email sign-ups within a $500 ad spend. This signals the pain is acute enough to seek a solution outside their current workflow.
    *   **REFINE:** 3-9 sign-ups. The message is resonating but may need tweaking. Conduct interviews with the sign-ups to understand their current process and where the friction is.
    *   **PAUSE:** <3 sign-ups. The pain is not strong enough to overcome the inertia of existing tools.

1.  **Pricing strategy:** Launch at $199/mo (Starter), $349/mo (Pro), $499/mo (Growth). Lock in early customers with a lifetime discount. Tiers are based on claim volume. Justified by being a fraction of the cost of legacy software or a single lost claim.

    *   **B:** Headline: "Your 24.1% Medicare denial rate is not a cost of doing business." Sub-headline: "Our simple tool validates that you have every required document—from SWO to POD—*before* you submit the claim. Fight denials before they happen."
4.  **Pre-launch → launch → sustained:**
    *   **Pre-launch (Day -30):** Publish 3 deep-dive articles on DME documentation errors. Start the validation ad campaign.
    *   **Launch (Day 0):** Announce on LinkedIn and to the pre-launch email list. Offer a 30-day money-back guarantee.

## Red-team and rebuttal
*   **Attack 1: The market of small, independent suppliers is shrinking.**
    *   The number of DMEPOS locations has declined 37.4% since 2013. This is a contracting market, making customer acquisition a fight over a shrinking pie.
    *   **Rebuttal:** The market is consolidating *because* of the intense margin pressure this tool solves. The decline of small operators is the business case. This tool is a lifeline for the thousands of remaining independents to compete and survive. The pressure makes the pain more acute for those still in business.
*   **Attack 2: This is a feature, not a product. It will be copied by incumbents.**
    *   Brightree or WellSky could easily add a more robust document checklist feature to their platform, killing this product overnight.
    *   **Rebuttal:** Incumbents are slow to innovate and are managing a massive, complex codebase. Their incentive is to build broad enterprise features, not perfect a single, niche workflow. By the time they successfully copy it, this product will have established a brand around being the best-in-class solution for this specific, high-value problem and will be moving on to adjacent data-driven features.

## Risks and kill criteria
1.  **Workflow Inertia:** The biggest risk is that suppliers, despite the pain, refuse to adopt a tool that lives outside their primary billing system. **Kill criteria:** If after 20 sales conversations, more than 75% say "I'll only use this if it's integrated into Brightree," the standalone product thesis is invalid.
2.  **High CAC:** The target buyers are hard to reach via traditional SaaS marketing channels. **Kill criteria:** If after 6 months, the cost to acquire a customer exceeds $2,500 (a 1.5-year payback on the lowest tier), the go-to-market is not viable.
3.  **Incumbent Response:** Brightree or another major player releases a surprisingly effective and well-marketed "Claim Readiness" feature. **Kill criteria:** If a top-3 competitor launches a directly comparable feature and our churn rate doubles in the following quarter, the window of opportunity has closed.

## What I learned today
- A government agency publishing a report that quantifies a market's "improper payment rate" is one of the strongest possible signals for problem proof. It is an unimpeachable, third-party validation of the customer's pain.
- A market that is consolidating or shrinking isn't an automatic "no go." If the consolidation is driven by the exact pain your product solves (e.g., margin compression), it can create intense demand from the survivors who need tools to compete.
- For hard-to-reach SMBs, the go-to-market strategy must often be indirect. The most effective channel may be partnering with the consultants and service providers (like billing companies) who already have the trust of the end customer.
- A regulatory moratorium on new entrants can be a positive signal for a tool serving existing businesses, as it increases the compliance burden and scrutiny on the established players.

## Why this beats yesterday's idea
This idea targets a pain point—revenue denial—that is more acute and directly tied to daily cash flow than yesterday's focus on NEMT claim preparation. While both are "get paid" problems, the DME market's 24.1% improper payment rate is a government-quantified, five-alarm fire. This provides a more powerful and undeniable hook for sales and marketing than the more general administrative burden of NEMT billing. The evidence is stronger, the financial impact is clearer, and the regulatory pressure is a more immediate forcing function.
