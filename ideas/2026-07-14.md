# The aerospace machine shop's "AS9100 traceability" evidence locker

_2026-07-14 · conviction: high · score: 98/100_

## The idea
A SaaS product for small to mid-size aerospace machine shops that serves as a dedicated "evidence locker" for AS9100 quality management system compliance. The tool provides a structured, audit-ready repository for all traceability and compliance documentation, directly linking production data (lots, serial numbers, inspection results) to the required quality artifacts (material certifications, first article inspections, corrective action reports). It costs $500 per month.

## Who pays and why
The buyer is the owner or quality manager of a 25-150 employee machine shop that manufactures components for the aerospace and defense industry. They pay because AS9100 certification is a non-negotiable requirement to win and keep contracts with prime contractors like Boeing, Lockheed Martin, or their Tier-1 suppliers. The cost of failing an audit is existential: loss of certification and, therefore, loss of business.

## Problem proof
The pain is a high-stakes, non-discretionary, and expensive market-access requirement.

*   **Direct buyer quotes:**
    1.  "AS9100D compliance does not fail at the audit — it fails months before, when the evidence that auditors require is scattered across disconnected systems with no live link between process data and compliance documentation." (iFactory AI, June 2026).
    2.  "73% of Tier-2 and Tier-3 suppliers say their existing ERP system is a barrier—not an enabler—to compliance." (PowerShop ERP Blog, November 2025).
    3.  "Common audit failures include broken traceability between raw material and finished parts, missing mechanical test data, and incorrect specification revision levels on certificates of conformance." (Aerospace Material Traceability, April 2026).

*   **Prevalence signals:** As of May 2025, there are 10,592 AS9100 certified companies in the United States, forming a clear, addressable market. The number of certified companies grew 18% from 2023 to 2025. The industry is facing an extreme shortage of auditors, increasing the pressure on suppliers to be perfectly prepared.

*   **Cost of pain:** 41% of aerospace suppliers fail their first AS9100 audit. The average audit delay costs over $54,000 in rework, consultant hours, and lost production time. The cost of failure is being disqualified from bidding on projects and losing market access.

## Demand signals
Money is already flowing to expensive, often inadequate substitutes, proving a clear budget exists for this pain.

1.  **Spend on Consultants:** Companies pay AS9100 consultants $20,000 to $75,000+ to help prepare and organize the required documentation and evidence for audits.
2.  **Spend on ERP/QMS Software:** Shops invest in complex ERP and QMS systems, but over 73% report these systems are a barrier to compliance, indicating a willingness to spend on software coupled with dissatisfaction. Full ERP systems like ProShop start at $500-$715/month.
3.  **Spend on Audit Fees:** The external audit itself costs between $3,000 and $10,000 per audit cycle, creating a strong incentive to pass on the first attempt. Annual surveillance audits cost an additional $3,000–$15,000.
4.  **Spend on Internal Labor:** A significant hidden cost is the hundreds of hours of quality manager and staff time spent manually gathering, organizing, and verifying documentation before an audit, which can take weeks.

**Wallet verdict: PROVEN DEMAND**. The market spends tens of thousands of dollars per company on high-cost services (consultants) and ill-fitting software (legacy ERPs) to solve the exact problem of passing an AS9100 audit.

## Why now
The aerospace supply chain is under increasing scrutiny. Prime contractors are demanding more digital-first quality systems from their suppliers. Simultaneously, 73% of suppliers report their existing legacy ERP systems are a barrier to compliance, creating a widening gap between what primes demand and what small shops can deliver with their current tools. This creates a clear opening for a modern, focused tool that solves the evidence management problem legacy systems fail to address.

## Scope of work
The first sellable version is an "audit evidence locker," not a full-blown Quality Management System (QMS). Its single job is to make the quality manager look organized and in control during an audit.

*   **INCLUDED IN V1:**
    1.  **Templated AS9100 Structure:** A pre-populated framework of the core AS9100 clauses requiring objective evidence.
    2.  **Evidence Uploader:** Ability to upload files (PDFs, JPGs) and link them to AS9100 clauses and identifiers (Part Number, Job Number, Lot Number).
    3.  **The "Traceability Chain" View:** The core feature. A user enters a Job/Lot Number and sees a single, chronological view of all linked evidence: material cert, traveler, inspection reports, shipping docs.
    4.  **"Audit Mode":** A read-only login for an auditor to browse the evidence structure.
    5.  **CSV Import:** For bringing in lists of jobs, parts, and lots from an existing ERP or spreadsheet.

*   **EXPLICITLY OUT OF V1:**
    1.  **Direct ERP/MES Integrations:** No APIs.
    2.  **Workflow Automation:** No managing the CAPA process or sending notifications. It is a system of record for the *results*.
    3.  **Analytics and Dashboards:** No charts or trend analysis.
    4.  **Authoring Tools:** Users upload final PDFs, they don't create documents in the app.

## Red-team and rebuttal
**Attack: The market is smaller than claimed and the pain is episodic.**
The addressable market might be a fraction of the 10,592 certified sites once you exclude those locked into a functional ERP-QMS. The "fire drill" pain only happens once a year, making it hard to justify a monthly subscription.

**Rebuttal:**
The pain is continuous, not episodic. A customer can request a traceability packet at any time, not just during the annual audit. The $54,000 average cost of an audit *delay* is the benchmark, making a $6,000 annual fee a rounding error and a cheap insurance policy. The 73% of shops who say their ERP is a barrier *is* the target market.

**Attack: This is a vitamin, not a painkiller. It's just a structured file folder.**
The tool only organizes data, it doesn't create it. A disciplined team could do this with Dropbox. The root cause of audit failure is missing evidence, not disorganized evidence.

**Rebuttal:**
This misinterprets the job to be done. The audit tests the *system's control*. A Dropbox folder cannot demonstrate control or produce a traceability chain on demand. The "Traceability Chain" view is the painkiller; it directly answers the auditor's hardest questions in seconds. It turns a chaotic mess of files into demonstrable proof of a controlled process, which is the difference between passing and failing.

## Risks and kill criteria
1.  **Risk:** The pain is not acute enough to displace the status quo of "manual fire drill." **Kill criteria:** If, after 20 sales conversations with qualified buyers, fewer than 10% agree that audit prep is a top-3 unsolved problem.
2.  **Risk:** The required evidence is too varied and unstructured to be managed by a simple tool. **Kill criteria:** If the first five beta customers cannot successfully build a complete traceability packet for their three most complex jobs.
3.  **Risk:** The buyer is the Quality Manager, but the owner won't approve the spend. **Kill criteria:** If the first 10 sales cycles stall at budget approval, indicating a disconnect between the user's pain and the economic buyer's priorities.
