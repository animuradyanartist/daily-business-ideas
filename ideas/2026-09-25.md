# The Independent Auto Dealer's "10-Year Audit-Proof" OFAC Compliance Hub

_2026-09-25 · conviction: high · score: 85/100_

## The idea
A standalone SaaS tool for independent US auto dealers that automates the mandatory Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) screening for every customer and, critically, archives the proof in a permanent, auditable digital vault for the newly required 10-year retention period. For **$60 per month**, the dealer gets a "fire-and-forget" utility that neutralizes the risk of catastrophic fines from a compliance audit.

## Who pays and why
The buyer is the owner of one of the ~52,000 active independent used car dealerships in the US. They are small business owners, often non-technical, who operate in a highly regulated environment. They gather in communities run by the National Independent Automobile Dealers Association (NIADA) and its state-level affiliates. They pay to avoid the severe penalties for non-compliance, which can include fines from $356,579 to over $1 million per violation and potential prison time.

## Problem proof
The pain is a mandatory, per-transaction federal compliance check where the evidence must now be retained for a decade, and the cost of failure is a business-ending penalty.

*   **Direct Buyer Quotes:**
    1.  "Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC): This is the grand poobah of all potential fines... Depending on the program, criminal penalties can include fines ranging from $50,000 to $10 million and imprisonment ranging from 10 to 30 years for willful violations. Civil penalties range from $250,000 or twice the amount of each underlying transaction to $1.075 million for each violation, according to the Treasury." (F&I Showroom Magazine, Mar 2018).
    2.  "The Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) doesn't care if you're a small Buy Here Pay Here lot or a high-line franchise. They enforce strict liability. If you sell a vehicle to someone on the Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list—even by accident—the fines are astronomical." (MoveMetal, 2026).
    3.  "Because the statute of limitations is now 10 years, the Treasury Department can walk into your showroom in 2034 and demand proof that you ran an OFAC check on a deal you desked in 2024. If that paper has faded, been lost in a fire, or was tossed out in a move, you have no legal defense." (MoveMetal, 2026).

*   **Prevalence:** The requirement to screen every customer (both cash and finance) against the OFAC SDN list is universal for every auto dealer in the US. The recent extension of the record retention period to 10 years makes this a long-term liability for every single deal.

*   **Cost of Pain & Frequency:** The check must be performed for **every transaction**. The cost of failure is not just a fine but an existential threat. Civil penalties can exceed $350,000 per violation, and willful violations carry criminal penalties including multi-million dollar fines and up to 30 years in prison.

## Demand signals
Money already flows to Dealer Management Systems (DMS) and credit bureaus that bundle OFAC checks. The opportunity is to unbundle this single, high-stakes compliance function and solve the newly created 10-year archiving problem, which incumbents handle poorly.

1.  **Spend on "Worse" Solutions:** Dealers already pay **$99 to $300+ per month** for DMS platforms where OFAC is a bundled feature. This proves the budget exists for operational software.
2.  **Credit Bureau Bundling:** Major credit bureaus like 700Credit offer OFAC screening as part of their compliance suites, confirming dealers pay for this function. However, this often doesn't provide a dedicated, 10-year, easily accessible archive.
3.  **Regulatory-Driven Market:** The existence of a whole category of "compliance solutions" for dealers shows a clear market for tools that mitigate regulatory risk.
4.  **Pain of Paper Records:** The extension to a 10-year retention period makes paper or non-archived digital records a significant liability due to fading, loss, or system migrations. This creates a new, specific pain point around long-term proof.

**Wallet verdict: EMERGING**. While demand for the OFAC *check* is proven (it's paid for inside bundles), the demand for a standalone *archive* solution is not yet explicitly proven with flowing money. The 10-year rule creates the catalyst for this emerging demand.

## Why now
In April 2024, the "21st Century Peace through Strength Act" extended the statute of limitations for OFAC violations from five to ten years. This regulatory change, finalized in early 2025, means that as of today, dealers are liable for a full decade for every transaction. An auditor in 2034 can demand proof for a deal made in 2024. This turns flimsy, paper-based, or non-archived digital records from a minor inconvenience into a major liability, creating a clear market need for a dedicated, permanent digital archive.

## Competitive landscape
1.  **Dealer Management Systems (DMS) (e.g., DealerCenter, Auto/Mate):** These platforms often include an OFAC check. Their weakness is that it's a bundled feature, not a core competency. The archiving is often weak and not marketed as a primary benefit.
2.  **Credit Bureaus (e.g., 700Credit):** They provide OFAC checks as part of the credit pull process. Their weakness is the same as the DMS: the check is transactional, and a permanent, easily searchable 10-year archive is not their core product.
3.  **Standalone Screening Tools (e.g., Visual OFAC, Compliancely):** These are often enterprise-focused and priced too high for a small independent dealer. Their weakness is a lack of focus on the specific workflow and price sensitivity of the independent auto dealer.

## Scope of work
The MVP is a simple web application.
*   **In:** User accounts for dealerships. A simple form to input customer name and identifying info. An API integration with a government or commercial sanctions list provider. A results page showing "Clear" or "Potential Match." A permanent, non-editable PDF generation of the search result with a timestamp. A searchable, exportable archive of all past searches.
*   **Out:** Complex case management, workflow tools, integration with DMS platforms, CRM features.
*   **Timeline:** 6-8 weeks for a solo developer to build the MVP.
*   **Assumptions:** A reliable, affordable API for the OFAC SDN list is available. Dealers will trust a new, standalone vendor with critical compliance data.
*   **Resources:** One full-stack developer. Initial capital for hosting and API costs.

## Validation plan
The biggest assumption is whether dealers will pay for a *standalone* tool when they perceive the problem is already "handled" by their DMS or credit bureau.

1.  **Action:** Create a simple landing page with the headline: "Your DMS Checks OFAC. But Where Is the Proof from 2024 When You're Audited in 2034?"
2.  **Content:** Explain the new 10-year rule and the risk of lost paper records. Offer a simple, permanent digital vault for $60/month. Include a "Request Early Access" button that collects an email address.
3.  **Channel:** Run a small ad campaign ($500) targeting members of independent auto dealer groups on Facebook and LinkedIn. Post the link in relevant forums.
4.  **Thresholds:**
    *   **GO:** >50 qualified email sign-ups within 2 weeks. This signals the messaging around the 10-year risk resonates enough to warrant building the MVP.
    *   **REFINE:** 15-50 sign-ups. The pain is present but the offer or messaging isn't strong enough. Interview sign-ups to understand their current process and fears.
    *   **PAUSE:** <15 sign-ups. The market does not perceive this as a distinct, urgent problem worth solving with a new tool.

## Red-team and rebuttal
*   **Attack:** This is a vitamin, not a painkiller. The dealer's immediate pain is solved by the check-box in their DMS. The 10-year risk is a future problem they won't pay to solve today.
    *   **Rebuttal:** The 2024 legislative change is the catalyst that makes the future pain feel present. An audit can happen at any time and reach back years. This product sells peace of mind against a catastrophic, business-ending event, which is a painkiller. The marketing must educate the buyer on why the risk is now acute.
*   **Attack:** This is a feature, not a company. The moment it gets traction, DMS providers will simply add a robust 10-year archive and give it away for free, killing the business.
    *   **Rebuttal:** The moat is focus. Incumbents are slow and not incentivized to build and market a best-in-class solution for one compliance feature. By winning on simplicity, ease of use, and a clear message tailored to this specific pain, a new entrant can build a trusted brand that becomes the de facto standard for OFAC archiving, making it hard to displace.

## Risks and kill criteria
1.  **Apathy Risk:** Dealers understand the risk but are not motivated enough to adopt a new, separate tool and subscription.
2.  **Incumbent Risk:** A major DMS or credit bureau provider sees the opportunity and quickly launches a competing feature that is "good enough" and bundled for free.
3.  **Distribution Risk:** Reaching the fragmented market of 52,000 independent dealers proves too slow or expensive.

**Kill Criteria:** If we cannot sign up 100 paying customers in the first 6 months post-launch, it indicates the market apathy is too high to build a venture-scale business.

## What I learned today
*   A change in a seemingly minor administrative rule, like extending a record retention period, can be a powerful "why now" catalyst that creates an entirely new software category.
*   The pain is not always the *task* itself, but the long-term *liability* associated with the proof of that task.
*   A defensible wedge against large, bundled incumbents can be an obsessive focus on doing one critical, unglamorous job better than anyone else, especially when that job is tied to a high-stakes compliance risk.
