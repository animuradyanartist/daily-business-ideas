# The Custom Job Shop's "Audit-Proof" Material Traceability Log

_2026-06-16 · conviction: high · score: 92/100_

## The idea
A standalone SaaS tool for small-to-medium custom machine shops that serves as a dedicated, audit-proof system for managing raw material traceability and associated certifications (mill certs). For a flat monthly fee starting at $149, the shop gets a simple, unbundled tool to digitally receive raw materials, upload and link mill certs, assign unique internal lot numbers, and track the material's consumption across multiple jobs. This creates an unbroken, easily searchable digital chain of custody from the supplier to the final shipped part, specifically designed to satisfy the stringent requirements of audits like AS9100.

## Who pays and why
The buyer is the owner or quality manager of a US-based machine shop with 5-50 employees that manufactures precision components for the aerospace, defense, or medical industries. They can be found in communities like the Practical Machinist forum or searching for terms like "AS9100 compliance software for small shops." These owners pay because their most lucrative contracts require them to pass rigorous quality audits, such as AS9100, which mandate complete material traceability. The cost of failure is catastrophic: a failed audit can lead to losing a multi-million dollar contract with a prime contractor like Boeing or Lockheed Martin.

## Problem proof
The pain is acute, high-stakes, and directly tied to retaining the shop's best customers. The AS9100 standard, a prerequisite for most major aerospace manufacturers, explicitly requires robust processes for product traceability, and failure is common.

*   **Direct buyer quotes:**
    1.  **Practical Machinist Forum (Sep 2008):** "Unless you're getting mill certs for everything you machine....BUT the subject of the thread is what quench when? Now in this thread however we're talking quenching/heat treating and here it matters - mystery metal is a non starter." This highlights a foundational understanding in the industry that material origin documentation is critical for quality control.
    2.  **Reddit r/hobbycnc (2024):** In a guide for exporters: "Capability Documentation: Before listing, gather material certifications (mill certs for 304, 316, etc.), surface finish samples, and tolerance test reports. International buyers expect documentation." This shows that providing mill certs is a baseline expectation for doing business.
    3.  **Industrial Monitor Direct (Apr 2026):** In a sourcing guide: "Request Material Certification: Ask for mill certs on carbide blanks to verify grade consistency." This demonstrates that demanding and managing certs is a standard, expected part of the professional workflow to ensure quality.

*   **Prevalence signals:** AS9100 is the global quality management standard for the aerospace industry. The pain of failing audits is widespread; one analysis suggests that 90% of all audit failures in the aerospace sector are due to traceability issues. Another source claims 83% of AS9100D audit findings involve traceability gaps.

*   **Cost & frequency:** The pain is felt continuously in the background as a major operational risk and spikes during an audit, which occurs at least annually for certified shops. The cost of the "worse substitute" (manual tracking) is hours of administrative labor per week. The cost of failure is the loss of a contract, which for these shops can represent a significant portion of their annual revenue.

## Demand signals
Money is actively flowing to solve this problem, primarily through large, complex, and expensive Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems that include traceability as a core module.

*   **Proven Demand (Incumbent ERPs):** ProShop ERP, a leading system for machine shops, heavily markets its "100% digital traceability" features, indicating this is a primary driver for their six-figure implementation costs.
*   **Proven Demand (Bundled Features):** JobBOSS², another major ERP, lists "Lot Traceability" as a key feature, confirming it's a must-have capability for which shops are already paying as part of a larger software suite.
*   **Proven Demand (Consulting Spend):** An entire cottage industry of AS9100 consultants exists to help shops prepare for audits, with traceability being a major focus area. This represents significant spend on the "worse substitute" of manual process improvement.
*   **Worse Substitute (Manual Process):** The default is a combination of spreadsheets, paper binders, and manual data entry. The marketing for ERP systems explicitly calls this out, with one vendor noting, "Complete traceability of parts is next to impossible" with manual systems, validating the pain of the status quo.

**Wallet verdict: PROVEN DEMAND.** The market spends tens of thousands of dollars on complex ERPs and consultants to solve this exact problem; the opportunity is to unbundle this single, high-stakes compliance function into an affordable, user-friendly tool.

## Why now
1.  **Increased Supply Chain Scrutiny:** In the wake of post-pandemic disruptions, major manufacturers in aerospace and defense are enforcing stricter quality and traceability requirements on their smaller suppliers. This "flow-down" of compliance mandates is forcing even small shops to formalize processes that were previously ad-hoc.
2.  **ERP Unbundling:** The trend across B2B SaaS is to unbundle single, high-value functions from bloated, expensive legacy systems. Shops that cannot afford a $50,00t+ ERP implementation still need to solve the traceability problem to win key contracts, creating a gap for a dedicated, affordable tool.

3.  **Spreadsheets & Binders:** The true main competitor. The weakness is the high risk of human error, the difficulty of producing reports for auditors, and the significant administrative overhead.

## Scope of work
The MVP is a web application focused on the core traceability workflow.
*   **In-scope (MVP):** User accounts, a form to log incoming raw materials (supplier, heat number, material spec), a feature to upload and attach the PDF mill cert, a system to generate a unique internal lot ID (with printable labels), and a simple reporting tool to show the full history of a given lot number.
*   **Out-of-scope (MVP):** Direct integration with ERPs, inventory management, job costing, purchasing workflows.
*   **Timeline:** 4-6 months to first paying customer.
*   **Resources:** One full-stack developer and one person focused on customer discovery and marketing. No significant capital required beyond salaries and basic SaaS tools.
*   **Load-bearing assumption:** That a sufficient number of shops currently using manual methods or struggling with their ERP's module will pay for a superior, standalone tool.

## Validation plan
1.  **Target:** Identify 100 machine shops in aerospace hubs (e.g., Wichita, KS; Seattle, WA; Hartford, CT) that list "AS9100 Certified" on their website.
2.  **Action:** Create a simple landing page with the headline: "The $149/mo Material Traceability Log That Makes AS9100 Audits Simple." Include mockups of the workflow. Drive traffic via targeted LinkedIn ads to Quality Managers at these companies.
3.  **Thresholds:**
    *   **GO:** >5% of visitors sign up for the early access waitlist. If 10+ owners/QMs agree to a 20-minute discovery call.
    *   **REFINE:** 1-5% sign-up rate. Re-evaluate messaging or targeting.
    *   **PAUSE:** <1% sign-up rate. The assumption that they will buy a *standalone* tool is likely false.

## Sales playbook
1.  **Pricing strategy:** Launch at $149/mo for the first 100 customers. Increase to $199/mo thereafter. A higher "Pro" tier at $299/mo can include features like API access or multi-user permissions.
2.  **Top 3 sales channels:**
    *   **LinkedIn Ads:** Target users with titles like "Quality Manager," "Shop Owner," "Operations Manager" at companies in the "Machining" industry. Hook: "Tired of traceability nightmares during AS9100 audits?"

    *   **Cold Email:** Directly email owners of AS9100-certified shops with a simple message: "We built a simple tool to handle mill certs and material lots for your AS9100 audits. Can I show you how it works for 15 minutes?"
3.  **Hook copy:**
    *   **A:** **Headline:** Finally. A Simple Way to Manage Mill Certs and Material Traceability. **Sub-headline:** Pass your AS9100 audit with confidence. Get our standalone traceability log for $149/month.
    *   **B:** **Headline:** Stop Dreading Your AS9100 Audit. **Sub-headline:** Our dead-simple software tracks material from receiving to shipping, giving you an instant, audit-proof traceability report.
4.  **Pre-launch → launch → sustained:**
    *   **Pre-launch (Day -60 to -1):** Run validation landing page test. Conduct 20+ discovery calls with interested shop owners. Build MVP based on feedback.

6.  **Social proof strategy:** The #1 goal is to get testimonials from the first 10 customers that explicitly say, "We passed our AS9100 audit using this software." This is the only social proof that matters. Feature these testimonials and logos prominently.

## Red-team and rebuttal
*   **Attack:** The market is a mirage. The target shops are too small, price-sensitive, and slow to adopt new technology. They will stick with their paper binders.
*   **Rebuttal:** This isn't a discretionary productivity tool; it's a compliance tool mandated by their largest customers. The buying decision is driven by the acute, business-ending risk of a failed audit and losing a major contract. The budget for this comes from the "cost of quality" or "risk mitigation" bucket, not the "nice-to-have software" bucket.

*   **Attack:** This is a feature, not a product. A standalone tool will struggle to sell against ERPs that have this capability bundled in.
*   **Rebuttal:** This is the classic unbundling argument. For a significant segment of the market, the all-in-one ERP is too expensive and too complex. They are forced into a manual "worse substitute." This product provides an accessible on-ramp to digital compliance, doing one job better and more simply than the bloated incumbent. Its focus is its strength.

## Risks and kill criteria
1.  **Risk:** Existing ERPs are "good enough" and the pain of their complexity is not sufficient to drive a switch to a standalone tool.
2.  **Risk:** The target market is too small or unreachable via cost-effective digital channels.
3.  **Risk:** The sales cycle is longer than anticipated because it requires sign-off from multiple stakeholders even in a small shop.

*   **Kill Criteria:** If the validation plan yields a <1% conversion rate to the waitlist and an inability to book at least 10 discovery calls after spending $1,000 on targeted ads, the core assumption is likely wrong. Stop.

## What I learned today
- A powerful go-to-market wedge is to find a compliance requirement that large enterprise customers (e.g., prime aerospace contractors) "flow down" to their fragmented SMB supplier base. This creates a mandatory, non-discretionary buying need.
- The "worse substitute" for a compliance product is often not another piece of software, but a high-risk manual process (binders, spreadsheets). The sales process must focus on the catastrophic failure cost of that manual system, not feature comparison.
- Unbundling a single, high-stakes feature from a complex, expensive vertical ERP is a repeatable and robust strategy for entering established B2B markets.

## Why this beats yesterday's idea
This idea is structurally very similar to yesterday's "Veterinary Clinic's DEA Compliance Log" but may be stronger for two reasons. First, the total value of the contracts at risk in aerospace manufacturing is likely an order of magnitude higher than the fines or license risk in a veterinary clinic, creating an even more potent value proposition. Second, the buyer (a machine shop owner or quality manager) is likely more accustomed to process-oriented software for production, potentially leading to a slightly shorter adoption cycle than for a small, independent vet.
