# The small-scale cosmetics maker's "MoCRA-ready" compliance system

_2026-08-03 · conviction: high · score: 98/100_

## The idea
A SaaS tool that provides a complete compliance system for small-to-mid-sized cosmetics brands to meet the requirements of the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 (MoCRA). The product is an "evidence locker" that helps brands manage facility registration, product listings, Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) documentation, and mandatory adverse event reporting. It is priced in tiers from $79 to $299 per month based on the number of products managed.

## Who pays and why
The buyer is the founder or owner-operator of an independent cosmetics brand. This buyer is an expert in formulation and marketing, not regulatory affairs. They are typically active in Shopify and Etsy seller communities. They pay to avoid the existential risk of their business being shut down by the FDA for non-compliance with MoCRA. The regulation is the most significant update to US cosmetics law in over 80 years, and failure to comply can result in mandatory recalls, facility registration suspension, and product seizure.

## Problem proof
The pain is the direct, high-stakes, and complex legal requirement for all non-exempt US cosmetic brands to comply with MoCRA. This is a new and non-negotiable cost of doing business.

*   **Direct buyer quotes:**
    1.  "A customer DMs you a photo of a bad reaction to your new serum. What's your next move? Under the new MoCRA regulations, this isn't just a customer service issue anymore—it's a legal one. The clock is ticking. You now have just 15 business days for FDA MoCRA serious adverse event reporting." (JJCC Group, Nov 2025)
    2.  "If you make lip balm, lotion, soap, or any cosmetic product to sell in the US, the Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 (MoCRA) probably applies to you. It's the biggest expansion of FDA's authority over cosmetics since 1938, and understanding what it requires is less complicated than the legislation makes it sound." (Stocksmith, Mar 2026)
    3.  "Under MOCRA, the FDA can suspend a facility's registration, making it illegal to sell or distribute cosmetics from that site." (Respect Manufacturing, Feb 2026)

*   **Prevalence signals:** The problem is universal for every cosmetic brand selling in the US not explicitly exempt. MoCRA mandates facility registration, product listing, safety substantiation, and adverse event reporting. The FDA reports over 15,000 active cosmetic facility registrations and over 1 million product listings since MoCRA's implementation, a dramatic increase from the prior voluntary system, confirming widespread market engagement with these new rules.

*   **Cost of pain:** The cost of non-compliance is business closure. The FDA has mandatory recall authority and can suspend a facility's registration, making it illegal to distribute products. The proactive cost is engaging consultants, which can cost $150-$450 per hour, with a single product review running $1,500-$9,000.

## Demand signals
Money is already flowing to high-cost, high-friction manual alternatives (consultants and service firms) to solve this new, mandatory compliance problem.

1.  **Spend on Compliance-as-a-Service:** Firms like Registrar Corp and FDA Registration Assistance charge significant fees for MoCRA compliance services. One firm charges $849/year for facility registration and $200 per product listing. Another firm charges a base of $450 for facility registration and a tiered fee of $300-$450 per SKU for product listings.
2.  **Spend on High-Ticket Consulting:** Regulatory consultants charge $150-$450 per hour. A single product safety review can cost thousands, and a toxicological risk assessment can cost $500 to $3,000+ per product.
3.  **Adoption of Adjacent Software:** Small brands already pay for software to manage other parts of their business. Cosmetic formulation software ranges from $39-$89/month for small teams. MRP software for cosmetics manufacturers can cost $199-$697/month. This demonstrates a willingness to pay for specialized software to professionalize operations.
4.  **Emergence of Niche MoCRA Tools:** New, focused software solutions are appearing to solve specific pieces of the MoCRA puzzle. For example, CosmeticSafetyRecord.com offers a dedicated adverse event logging tool for $19-$49/month.

**Wallet verdict: PROVEN DEMAND**. The market is spending thousands of dollars per company on high-cost services to solve this exact, legally-mandated problem, creating a clear opening for a more efficient and affordable SaaS alternative.

## Why now
The Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022 (MoCRA) is the first major overhaul of US cosmetics law in over 80 years. Key compliance deadlines for facility registration and product listing have recently passed (July 2024), and the FDA is now in an active enforcement phase. In May 2026, the FDA highlighted its achievements in implementing MoCRA, signaling a shift from awareness to active oversight. This transition creates a powerful, time-bound forcing function for the entire industry to find and implement compliance solutions.

## Market structure
The market consists of a large, fragmented base of independent brand owners being subjected to a new, uniform, and complex federal regulation.

*   **TAM/SAM/SOM:**
    *   **TAM:** The U.S. cosmetics market is valued at over $100 billion. The entire market is affected by MoCRA.
    *   **SAM:** MoCRA exempts businesses with under $1M in average annual sales, unless they produce high-risk products (e.g., for eye contact, like mascara or eyeliner). The target market is the thousands of brands just above this threshold or producing non-exempt products. Estimating a conservative 10,000 such businesses paying an average of $1,500/year ($125/month) yields a SAM of **$15 million annually**.
    *   **SOM:** A 2-year goal is to capture 2.5% of this market (250 businesses), generating **$375,000 in ARR**.
*   **Fragmentation:** The market is characterized by a large, growing, and fragmented base of "indie brands" selling through Shopify, Etsy, and social media. This structure is ideal for a low-touch SaaS model.
*   **Incumbent Gap:** Incumbents are either complex, expensive enterprise QMS/ERP systems (starting at $2,000-$3,000/month) or high-cost manual service providers (consultants). The gap is for an affordable, purpose-built SaaS tool designed for the non-expert founder of a small-but-growing brand.

## Unit economics and moat
*   **Pricing:** Three tiers: $79/month (Starter, <10 SKUs), $149/month (Pro, <50 SKUs), $299/month (Growth, >50 SKUs). This is priced significantly below the cost of consultants ($1,500+ per product) and service firms ($849/year + $200/product).
*   **Gross Margin:** ~90%, standard for B2B SaaS.
*   **CAC/LTV:** Assuming a blended CAC of $750 (via content marketing and targeted ads) and a 4-year LTV of $7,152 (based on $149/mo ARPA), the LTV/CAC ratio is ~9.5.
*   **Payback Period:** $750 CAC / ($149 ARPA * 0.90 Gross Margin) = **5.6 months**. This is a strong, financeable model.
*   **Moat thesis:** The primary moat is **high switching costs**. Once a brand's entire compliance history—product listings, GMP documents, supplier records, safety substantiation, and adverse event logs—is centralized, migrating becomes a significant compliance risk. The software becomes the auditable "single source of truth" for the FDA.

## Competitive landscape
1.  **Compliance Consultants/Service Firms (e.g., Registrar Corp):** They do the work for you but are expensive and slow. They prove willingness-to-pay but are vulnerable to a faster, cheaper, self-serve software alternative.
2.  **Enterprise QMS/ERP Software (e.g., BatchMaster, QT9 ERP):** These are powerful but overly complex and expensive for small brands. They are not designed for the specific, streamlined workflows required by MoCRA for a non-expert user.
3.  **Horizontal Tools (Spreadsheets, Google Drive):** The default "free" option. This is disorganized, not auditable, prone to error, and lacks the guided workflows and deadline tracking needed for serious compliance, making it a weak long-term solution.

## Scope of work
The MVP is a guided "evidence locker" focused on the most acute MoCRA requirements.
*   **MVP Cut (In):** 1) Adverse Event Log with 15-day countdown for serious events. 2) Product & Facility Listing data preparer (generates a submittable file, no direct API integration). 3) GMP template library and document uploader for SOPs and supplier records.
*   **MVP Cut (Out):** Direct FDA API integration, full eQMS features (batch records, CAPA), inventory management, formulation tools.
*   **Milestones:** Month 1-2: Build AE Log MVP. Month 3: Build Listing Preparer. Month 4: Build GMP document vault. Month 5: Onboard first 10 beta users.
*   **Resources:** 1-2 software engineers. 4-6 months to first revenue. Low capital intensity.

## Validation plan
The biggest assumption is that brands will choose a self-serve SaaS tool over a "done-for-you" consultant.
1.  **Test:** Create a landing page detailing the three MVP features and pricing ($79/mo). Headline: "Your MoCRA Compliance System. Get FDA-ready in a weekend."
2.  **Channel:** Run a $500 targeted ad campaign on Instagram and Facebook to audiences interested in "indie beauty," "cosmetic chemistry," and followers of small-batch cosmetics business influencers.
3.  **Thresholds:**
    *   **GO:** >2% click-through rate and >5% of visitors enter an email for the waitlist (implies ~25+ qualified leads). If 3+ leads pre-pay for a lifetime deal, begin building immediately.
    *   **REFINE:** Low CTR but high email conversion. The audience is right, but the message is wrong. Interview leads to refine the value proposition.
    *   **PAUSE:** <1% CTR and low conversions. The channel/message is wrong, or the desire for a self-serve tool is lower than hypothesized.

## Sales playbook
1.  **Pricing strategy:** Launch at $79/$149/$299. Grandfather first 100 customers at that price. Raise prices by 20% after the first year as features are added. Tiers are based on SKU count.
2.  **Top 3 sales channels:**

## Red-team and rebuttal
*   **Attack:** The market is a fraction of the size claimed and will adopt slowly. Most indie brands are under the $1M exemption and will ignore this until the FDA makes an example of someone.
    *   **Rebuttal:** The attack ignores that the exemption is voided for any brand making high-risk products like eye makeup, a huge part of the indie market. The target customer is the professionalizing brand for whom this is their livelihood, not a hobby. For them, the risk of shutdown is a powerful motivator, making this a "business insurance" purchase, not an optional tool.
*   **Attack:** This is a vitamin, not a painkiller. Brands will use Google Docs and spreadsheets because it's free. They won't pay $149/month for a slightly better spreadsheet.
    *   **Rebuttal:** This mischaracterizes the job-to-be-done. The product isn't just storage; it's a guided, auditable system. Its value is in preventing catastrophic error, like missing the 15-day reporting deadline for a serious adverse event. A spreadsheet provides no active safeguards. The cost of failure is so high that a purpose-built tool is a rational purchase.

## Risks and kill criteria
1.  **Regulatory Delay:** The FDA further delays finalizing GMP rules, reducing the urgency for one key module.
2.  **Slow Adoption:** Brands prove unwilling to pay for a self-serve tool, preferring the hand-holding of expensive consultants despite the cost.
3.  **Low Perceived Risk:** Widespread non-compliance occurs with no visible FDA enforcement action for a year or more, eroding the purchasing trigger.

**Kill Criteria:** If after 3 months of a live, functioning MVP, we cannot convert 10 paying customers from organic traffic and paid ads, it signals the demand for a *self-serve tool* is too weak to sustain the business.

## Candidates I considered and killed today
- **The Small Food Truck Operator's "Health Department-Ready" Commissary Log** — Killed. Strong pain, but the cosmetics market is more digitally native, has higher gross margins, and the forcing function is a new, national federal law, which is stronger than varied local health codes.
- **The Independent Landscaper's "Pesticide Application" Compliance Log** — Killed. A very large market, but the buyer is less digitally concentrated and likely has lower software spend per-customer than a growing e-commerce cosmetics brand.
- **The Short-Term Rental (STR) Owner's "Local Ordinance" Compliance Dashboard** — Killed. The hyper-local and constantly changing nature of STR regulations makes building and maintaining the software core significantly more complex and costly than a single, national standard like MoCRA.

## What I learned today
- A new, sweeping, national-level regulation is one of the most powerful forcing functions for creating a B2B SaaS market overnight.
- The most valuable compliance software is not just a records database, but a system that actively prevents deadline-driven, high-consequence failures (e.g., a 15-day reporting window).
- The exemption criteria for a regulation are often where the true market opportunity lies; the businesses that fall just outside the exemption are the most motivated buyers.
- A viable wedge against expensive "done-for-you" service firms is a "do-it-yourself-with-guardrails" software product that is 10x cheaper and 10x faster.

## Why this beats yesterday's idea
This idea is stronger than the restaurant food safety log because the forcing function is a new, national federal law (MoCRA) versus a patchwork of existing state and local health codes. The cosmetics market is arguably more digitally concentrated and accessible through online channels than independent restaurants. Finally, the "indie brand" buyer is often a solo founder for whom the business is their entire livelihood, making the existential threat of an FDA shutdown an even more acute pain point.
