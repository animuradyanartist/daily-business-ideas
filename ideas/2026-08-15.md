# The Home Health Agency's "Get Paid by Medicaid" Evidence Locker

_2026-08-15 · conviction: high · score: 98/100_

## The idea
A flat-rate SaaS tool for small-to-midsize home health agencies that acts as a pre-submission audit and evidence locker for Medicaid claims. For ~$299/month, an agency's billing manager can connect their state's Electronic Visit Verification (EVV) data and automatically run each potential claim against a checklist of common denial triggers. The tool flags EVV mismatches, missing physician signatures, and incomplete documentation *before* the claim is submitted, packaging all necessary proof into an "audit-ready" file. This directly prevents payment denials, reduces manual rework, and protects the agency from catastrophic post-payment clawbacks.

## Who pays and why
The buyer is the owner or billing manager of a home health agency that relies on Medicaid for a significant portion of its revenue. Their primary operational risk is not a lack of patients, but the administrative friction that delays or denies payment for services already rendered. They can be found in industry forums discussing the challenges of EVV and billing. The federal 21st Century Cures Act mandates that all Medicaid-funded home health services be verified by an EVV system. A mismatch between the EVV record and the submitted claim is a primary reason for denial and a major trigger for audits. They pay for this tool because a denied claim is a direct threat to cash flow, and the risk of post-payment audits and recoupments (clawbacks) is an existential threat.

## Scope of work
The MVP is a web application for agencies in a single, large state (e.g., New York, Texas).
*   **In-scope (MVP):**
    *   Secure user authentication for agency billing managers.
    *   API integration with the state's chosen EVV data aggregator.
    *   A rules engine that flags claims against the top 5-10 state-specific denial reasons (e.g., EVV time mismatch, missing service codes, no physician order).
    *   A simple dashboard showing "Ready to Bill," "Needs Review," and "Error" queues.
    *   Ability to export a clean data file for submission and a PDF "evidence package" for each claim.
*   **Out-of-scope (MVP):** Direct claim submission, full EMR features, scheduling, payroll.
*   **Milestones:** Month 1-2: Deep customer discovery with 10 NY-based agencies. Month 3-4: Build core EVV data ingestion and rules engine. Month 5-6: Build UI/dashboard and launch with 3 beta customers.
*   **Resources:** One full-stack developer, one domain expert (can be a consultant). Low capital requirement.

    *   **GO:** 50+ qualified waitlist signups and 10+ completed discovery calls within 30 days. This signals the pain is acute enough to seek a new solution.

    *   **PAUSE:** Fewer than 20 signups. The assumption is likely wrong; agencies may be too locked into their EMRs to consider a point solution.

## What I learned today
- Federally mandated compliance workflows (like EVV) create the most durable and non-negotiable pain points for SMBs. The government forcing a change is a powerful market-creation event.
- In a market with established all-in-one incumbents, the most effective wedge is not to build a better all-in-one, but to build a best-in-class point solution for the single most expensive failure point in the incumbent's workflow.
- A government audit that quantifies the dollar amount of non-compliance (e.g., the NY Comptroller's $97.6M report) is the most powerful piece of marketing collateral possible for a compliance-driven product.
- Reimbursement pressure from a central payer (like CMS) can increase, not decrease, the demand for tools that prevent revenue leakage, as businesses can no longer afford operational sloppiness.

## Why this beats yesterday's idea
This opportunity targets a more acute and frequent pain than the University Lab Manager's chemical inventory system. While both are compliance-driven, the home health agency's pain is tied directly to monthly cash flow and revenue—the lifeblood of the business. A lab manager might delay fixing their inventory system, but an agency owner cannot ignore claim denials that threaten their ability to make payroll next week. The cost of failure is more immediate and existential.
