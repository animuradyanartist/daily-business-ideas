# The "Audit-Ready" Unclaimed Property (Escheatment) Compliance System

_2026-08-26 · conviction: high · score: 99/100_

## The idea
A SaaS tool for U.S. mid-market companies to manage unclaimed property (escheatment) compliance. For an annual subscription starting at **$3,000/year**, the software provides a system of record to track potential unclaimed property, automate mandatory owner outreach (due diligence), and generate audit-ready reporting files in the required format for all 50+ U.S. jurisdictions. It replaces error-prone spreadsheets and expensive, manual consulting engagements.

## Who pays and why
The buyer is the CFO or Controller of a U.S. mid-market company (defined as having $30 million to $10 billion in revenue). There are approximately 125,000 such companies in the U.S. They pay because failing to comply with unclaimed property laws is a significant financial and operational risk.

This is a mandatory, non-discretionary business function. Every state has laws requiring businesses to report and remit property (e.g., uncashed payroll checks, customer credits, dormant accounts) after a specified "dormancy period," typically 2-5 years.

The pain of non-compliance is severe:
*   **Financial Penalties:** States impose interest penalties of 10-25% on the value of the property, plus daily fines ranging from $100 to $200 (up to $10,000), and additional fines from $1,000 to $25,000. Some states add a civil penalty of 25% or more of the property's value. Willful failure can even lead to criminal charges.
*   **Audit Costs:** State audits are aggressive, expensive, and can have a lookback period of 10-15 years. Many companies lack complete records for that period, leading to costly estimations of liability.
*   **Operational Drain:** The process is complex, with over 50 different jurisdictions, each with its own rules, dormancy periods, and reporting formats. Managing this via spreadsheets is a high-risk administrative burden.

## Problem proof
The pain is a direct, recurring, and high-stakes compliance liability. States are increasing enforcement as they see unclaimed property as a significant source of revenue.

*   **Direct Buyer Quotes:**
    1.  "Unclaimed property compliance is a regulatory obligation that affects virtually every business... yet it remains one of the most overlooked and under-resourced areas of compliance. As companies face increasing scrutiny from state governments and a growing risk of unclaimed property audits, many are reevaluating how they manage this complex process." (Source: DMA, an industry vendor, describing the customer problem).
    2.  "Manual processes and unclear policy ownership commonly cause missed regulatory deadlines and inaccurate filings—the top triggers for state audits." (Source: Guidehouse, a consulting firm, describing the "before" state).
    3.  "Even the most sophisticated companies in the country that have a long history of filing unclaimed property reports... find themselves encumbered with long and costly audits that can extend three-to-five years and result in $10s of millions in liability." (Source: FEI Daily, describing the pain for large companies, which trickles down).

*   **Prevalence:** The problem affects virtually every organization operating in the U.S. States are intensifying audit activity, often using third-party auditors on a contingency-fee basis. Key audit triggers include a history of inconsistent or non-existent filings, M&A activity, or simply operating in a targeted industry.

*   **Cost of Pain & Frequency:** The pain is annual, with most states having a filing deadline around November 1st. A single audit can result in multi-million-dollar liabilities. The threat of these costs forces companies to spend on software, consulting, or dedicated internal resources to manage the process.

## Demand signals
The market has a mature ecosystem of software and service providers generating significant revenue by solving this exact problem.

1.  **Established Enterprise Vendors:** Large firms like Sovos and Ryan LLC have multi-million dollar practices dedicated to unclaimed property compliance, serving thousands of enterprise clients. This proves large-scale willingness to pay.
2.  **Emerging Self-Serve SaaS:** Newer, self-serve tools like ReportMyUP and Simple Escheat have entered the market with transparent, subscription-based pricing, validating the SaaS model for smaller filers. ReportMyUP starts at $299/year, while Simple Escheat starts at $1,200/year.
3.  **Active Government Enforcement:** States are becoming more aggressive. Delaware considers unclaimed property its third-largest source of revenue. California recently added questions about unclaimed property compliance directly to its business tax returns, forcing the issue. This state-driven pressure creates continuous demand.
4.  **Spend on Worse Solutions:** The default "worse solution" is a combination of manual spreadsheets and expensive ad-hoc advice from accounting firms. The risk and inefficiency of this manual approach are primary sales drivers for software vendors.

**Wallet verdict: PROVEN DEMAND**. A multi-tiered market of software and service providers already exists and is growing, fueled by non-discretionary, high-stakes government compliance requirements.

## Why now
State governments, facing budget pressures, are increasing their focus on unclaimed property as a significant source of non-tax revenue. They are using data analytics to identify non-compliant companies and are empowering third-party audit firms to pursue them aggressively on a contingency basis. This shift from passive collection to active enforcement elevates the risk for all businesses and transforms escheatment compliance from a low-priority accounting task into a high-visibility issue for CFOs.

## Market structure
The market is a mature compliance sector with a clear structural gap between high-end, service-heavy incumbents and low-end, feature-light SaaS tools.

*   **TAM/SAM/SOM:**
    *   **TAM:** The total value of unclaimed property is in the tens of billions. The addressable market is the budget allocated to mitigate the multi-billion dollar risk pool of penalties and audit costs.
    *   **SAM:** There are approximately 125,000 middle-market businesses in the U.S. Assuming an average annual software spend of $3,000 (a price point between Simple Escheat's entry and enterprise tiers), the SAM is **$375M**.
    *   **SOM (Year 3 Goal):** Capturing 0.2% of these companies (250 customers) at a $3,000 average price point yields **$750,000 ARR.**

*   **Growth and Fragmentation:** The market is growing as state enforcement becomes more sophisticated. The buyer base (mid-market companies) is fragmented, making it accessible through targeted digital marketing to CFOs and Controllers.

*   **Incumbents and Gaps:** The high end is dominated by Sovos and Ryan, which offer enterprise-grade software and extensive consulting services. The low end is served by tools like ReportMyUP (owned by Sovos) and HRS Pro, which target very small businesses with simple needs. The gap is for a product designed specifically for the mid-market: more robust and multi-entity capable than the low-end tools, but simpler, more affordable, and fully self-service compared to the enterprise behemoths.

## Unit economics and moat
The model is a standard B2B SaaS with high margins and strong retention driven by compliance needs and high switching costs.

*   **Pricing:** Tiered annual subscription: $3,000/year (Core), $6,000/year (Pro), $10,000+/year (Enterprise), based on number of entities (FEINs) and property records.
*   **Unit economics:**
    *   **Gross Margin:** ~90% after hosting and support costs.
    *   **CAC/LTV Proxy:** With a targeted content marketing and digital ad strategy aimed at financial controllers, a CAC of $3,500 is realistic. The mandatory, recurring nature of the compliance task should lead to very low churn (<5% annually), resulting in a 20-year customer lifetime. At $3,000/year, the **LTV is $60,000.** The LTV/CAC ratio is a very healthy ~17.
    *   **Payback Period:** $3,500 CAC / $250 monthly revenue = **14 months.**
*   **Moat thesis:** The primary moat is **high switching costs created by the customer's own data.** Once a company has years of historical unclaimed property data, due diligence records, and filing receipts in the system, it becomes the official system of record for audit defense. Migrating this data to a new provider is a high-risk, high-effort task that finance departments will avoid.

## Scope of work
The core challenge is not technology but maintaining an accurate, up-to-date database of rules for 50+ jurisdictions.

*   **MVP Scope (In):**
    *   Secure data import for property records (CSV).
    *   A rules engine that calculates dormancy periods based on property type and state.
    *   A dashboard to track upcoming due diligence and filing deadlines.
    *   Automated generation of due diligence letters.
    *   Generation of NAUPA II formatted export files for state reporting.
    *   Support for the 10-15 most common property types (e.g., payroll checks, vendor payments, AR credits).
*   **MVP Scope (Out):**
    *   Direct API integrations with ERPs/accounting systems.
    *   Automated e-filing directly to states.
    *   Support for complex property types (securities, dividends, etc.).
    *   Managed services or consulting.
*   **Milestones:**
    *   **Months 1-2:** Build and validate the state rules database. This is the critical path.
    *   **Months 3-5:** Develop core application: data import, dormancy calculation, dashboard, and reporting export.
    *   **Month 6:** Onboard 3-5 beta customers to validate workflow and accuracy.
*   **Resources:** One full-stack developer. One part-time compliance expert/paralegal to build and maintain the rules database. Initial capital for data sources and legal review.

## Validation plan
The biggest assumption is that a self-serve product can be trusted by a mid-market CFO for a high-stakes compliance task without a sales-led motion.

*   **Test:** Create a high-fidelity landing page and a detailed "State of Unclaimed Property Compliance for Mid-Market Companies" PDF report. Drive traffic via LinkedIn ads targeted at "Financial Controller" and "CFO" titles at companies with 200-2,000 employees.
*   **Action:** The call-to-action is to download the report in exchange for an email, followed by an offer to join the waitlist for the "first self-serve escheatment platform built for the mid-market."
*   **Thresholds:**
    *   **GO:** 5%+ conversion rate on the landing page and >20 qualified waitlist sign-ups within a $2,000 ad spend. This indicates the messaging resonates with the target buyer.
    *   **REFINE:** Lower conversion but high open/click rates on follow-up emails. The pain is real, but the product positioning or trust is weak. Conduct interviews with sign-ups.
    *   **PAUSE:** <1% conversion rate. The channel is wrong, or the self-serve message is not compelling enough to overcome the inertia of the status quo.

## Red-team and rebuttal
1.  **Attack:** The pain is a vitamin, not a painkiller. This is a low-frequency task that finance departments ignore until an audit notice arrives, at which point they'll hire an expensive law firm, not buy a SaaS tool.
    *   **Rebuttal:** This misreads the buyer. For a CFO or Controller, managing contingent liabilities and ensuring regulatory compliance is a core part of their job. The penalties are severe enough to make proactive management a fiduciary duty. This is not a vitamin; it's a mandatory government filing with significant financial and legal consequences, akin to tax compliance.
2.  **Attack:** The moat is weak. The rules are public. An incumbent like Sovos can easily launch a mid-market version and crush you with their brand and distribution.
    *   **Rebuttal:** Sovos already has. They launched ReportMyUP, but it's positioned for the low end of the market. Incumbents are optimized for enterprise sales motions and service-heavy contracts. The opportunity is to win with a product-led growth model focused on a superior, self-service user experience, which is culturally difficult for incumbents to replicate. The moat is not the rules, but the workflow and the data accumulated by the customer, which creates high switching costs.

## Risks and kill criteria
1.  **Compliance Complexity Risk:** The effort required to maintain the state-by-state rules database is underestimated, leading to inaccurate software and reputational damage. **Kill criteria:** If a compliance audit of the MVP by a third-party expert reveals material inaccuracies that require a fundamental architectural rebuild.
2.  **Sales Cycle Risk:** The assumption of a self-serve sales motion is wrong. Mid-market buyers require extensive, sales-led education and hand-holding, destroying the unit economics. **Kill criteria:** If after 6 months and 50+ sales conversations, >90% of deals require multiple demos and legal reviews, and the sales cycle averages over 90 days.
3.  **Incumbent Risk:** Sovos or another large player re-positions an existing product or acquires a competitor to aggressively target the mid-market, using their brand and budget to dominate the space. **Kill criteria:** If an incumbent launches a directly comparable product with a similar price point and backs it with a significant marketing budget, making customer acquisition costs prohibitive.

## What I learned today
- A non-discretionary compliance problem where the government itself is the primary demand driver (through audits and penalties) is an extremely powerful market dynamic.
- The gap between enterprise software (complex, service-heavy) and SMB software (simple, feature-light) can be a significant market opportunity in itself, particularly in B2B compliance.
- A product's moat can be built not just from proprietary technology, but from becoming the auditable "system of record" for a customer's high-stakes operational data, creating immense switching costs.
- The most effective "salespeople" for a compliance product are often the regulators themselves; increased government enforcement is a market-wide tailwind.

## Why this beats yesterday's idea
Yesterday's idea, the "SOC 2-Ready" Evidence Collector, is a strong, revenue-gating compliance problem. However, this Unclaimed Property system is arguably stronger for two reasons. First, the requirement is universal and mandatory for nearly all businesses, not just SaaS companies selling to the enterprise. The market is broader. Second, the enforcement mechanism is more direct and financially motivated; state governments actively hunt for non-compliance as a revenue source, whereas SOC 2 is enforced by commercial counterparties. The demand driver feels more acute and less discretionary.

- **Margin pool:** High software margins (~90%). Value is in mitigating catastrophic, non-discretionary compliance penalties, allowing for value-based pricing.
- **Distribution control:** Incumbents control enterprise distribution via established sales teams. The mid-market is open and accessible via content marketing and targeted digital ads aimed at financial controllers.
- **Incumbents and inertia:** Incumbents are focused on high-touch, high-ACV enterprise deals and services. Their business models create inertia against a low-touch, self-serve product for the mid-market, leaving a structural gap.
- **Regulatory / platform risk:** Low. The business exists to service regulation. The primary risk is the complexity of keeping the rules engine current across 50+ jurisdictions.
