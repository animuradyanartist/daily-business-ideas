# The US Importer's "ISF 10+2" Filing Preparation Hub

_2026-09-05 · conviction: high · score: 100/100_

## The idea
A SaaS tool for US importers to gather, validate, and prepare the data required for the Importer Security Filing (ISF or "10+2"). For **$49/month**, the software provides a guided, collaborative workflow to collect the 10 required data elements from suppliers and internal teams, validate them against common errors, and generate a clean, broker-ready packet. It replaces chaotic email chains and spreadsheets, de-risking every ocean freight shipment against a $5,000 penalty for a late or inaccurate filing.

## Who pays and why
The buyer is a small to medium-sized US business that imports goods via ocean freight, such as an Amazon FBA seller, a small retailer, or a niche manufacturer. The Importer of Record is legally 100% responsible for the timely and accurate submission of the ISF. They can be found searching for terms like "ISF filing requirements" and "how to avoid ISF penalties."

They pay to avoid a direct, government-mandated penalty of **$5,000 per violation** for failing to file the ISF with US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) at least 24 hours *before* the cargo is loaded onto a vessel at the foreign port. This penalty applies to late, inaccurate, or incomplete filings. The core pain is the operational chaos of collecting 10 specific data elements from multiple parties (foreign supplier, freight forwarder, internal teams) under a tight deadline, where simple communication errors can trigger the fine.

## Problem proof
The pain is a legally-mandated, high-stakes compliance requirement with severe financial consequences. The alternative to a dedicated tool is a manual, error-prone process of chasing data via email.

*   **Direct Buyer Quotes:**

(V. Alexander & Co., Inc. Trade Insights, Nov 2022)
    2.  "For any business importing goods into the U.S. by sea, a late Importer Security Filing (ISF) is a quick way to lose $5,000. (Great Lakes Customs Law, accessed Sep 2026)

*   **Prevalence:** The problem affects every single ocean freight shipment into the US. U.S. ports handle tens of millions of containers annually, each requiring an ISF. CBP enforcement has stiffened markedly, with ports no longer obligated to issue "three strikes" before levying penalties.

*   **Cost of Pain & Frequency:** The pain occurs with every ocean shipment. The direct cost of failure is a **$5,000 penalty per violation**, which can escalate to $10,000 for repeat offenses. Even if mitigated, first-time violations typically cost $1,000-$2,000, and subsequent offenses are not mitigated below $2,500. Beyond the fine, non-compliance can lead to cargo holds, increased inspections, and significant supply chain disruptions.

## Demand signals
Money is already flowing to solve this exact problem, primarily through service fees paid to customs brokers and, for larger companies, through expensive compliance software.

1.  **Spend on "Worse" Solutions:** Importers pay customs brokers **$30 to $80 per filing** specifically for the ISF service. Many brokers also charge a **"Late ISF Filing Fee" of around $100** on top of the base fee. This proves importers pay a per-shipment fee to offload this task.
2.  **Premium for Compliance:** Forgoing a broker is so risky that it's rarely done, as mistakes can bring fines of $5,000+ per violation. The entire customs brokerage industry, which charges $150-$400 per entry, is a proxy for the willingness to pay to avoid compliance penalties.
3.  **Incumbent Software Exists (for Brokers):** Large software vendors like Descartes and CrimsonLogic sell complex, expensive ISF filing and customs compliance suites directly to customs brokers and large forwarders. This confirms the problem is valuable enough to be solved by enterprise software, but they target the filer, not the importer.
4.  **Emergence of Self-Filing Tools:** Niche platforms like Strix ($30/entry) and eezyimport are emerging to help importers self-file, proving a desire to unbundle this service from brokers and take more control.

**Wallet verdict: PROVEN DEMAND**. Importers consistently pay service fees to brokers for every single shipment to handle this filing, and a market for both broker-centric and self-filing software already exists.

## Why now
CBP enforcement of ISF penalties has significantly increased. What was once a rule with a "three strikes" warning system is now strictly enforced at the port level, with liquidated damages issued within 90 days of the violation. This shift from discretionary to consistent enforcement transforms ISF compliance from a background task into an acute, recurring financial risk, creating a strong catalyst for adopting a specialized tool to prevent errors.

## Competitive landscape
1.  **Customs Brokers (The Status Quo):** They provide a manual service, not a product. Their strength is their license and expertise. The gap is that they are reactive; they file the data they are given. This tool makes their data better, reducing errors and liability for the importer.
2.  **Broker-centric Software (Descartes, CrimsonLogic, CustomsNow):** These are powerful, complex, and expensive platforms designed for the operational needs of a licensed broker or large forwarder. They are not sold to or designed for the SMB importer.
3.  **Self-Filing Tools (Strix, eezyimport):** These tools aim to replace the broker entirely for filing. This is a higher-friction sale requiring the importer to get a filer code and take on more liability. The proposed idea is a lower-friction "pre-compliance" tool that works *with* the importer's existing broker relationship.

## Scope of work
The MVP is a "pre-flight checklist" web app that helps an importer gather, validate, and format the 10 required ISF data elements before sending them to their customs broker.

*   **IN for v1:**
    *   A simple, guided web form to input the 10 required data elements for a new shipment, with clear help text for each field.
    *   A "profiles" feature to save and re-use Seller, Buyer, and Manufacturer details.
    *   Basic data validation (e.g., checking field formats, ensuring required fields are not empty).
    *   A final review screen showing all data points clearly.
    *   An "Export to PDF/CSV" function that generates a standardized document to email to a broker.
    *   User authentication and subscription management via Stripe.

*   **OUT for v1 (and why):**
    *   **Direct CBP Filing/API Integration:** Explicitly out. This adds massive complexity and changes the business into a licensed filer. The value is in *data prep*, not filing.
    *   **Supplier/Forwarder Portal:** This is a v2 feature. V1 empowers the importer who is ultimately responsible.
    *   **Integrations with other software:** Adds complexity. The pain of a $5,000 fine justifies manual data entry initially.

## Red-team and rebuttal
1.  **Attack:** The pain is overstated. The $5,000 penalty is often mitigated down to $1,000-$2,000 for first-time offenders.
    *   **Rebuttal:** Conceded, but the mitigation process itself costs time and legal/consulting fees. Furthermore, the penalty for subsequent violations is higher, and the operational costs of cargo holds and inspections can exceed the penalty itself. The goal is to avoid the entire process, not just minimize the fine.
2.  **Attack:** Customs brokers already solve this. They have the software and the process. An importer won't pay for another tool on top of their broker fees.
    *   **Rebuttal:** Brokers file what they are given. The core pain of data collection and coordination falls on the importer. This tool doesn't replace the broker; it makes the importer a better, lower-risk client. For $49/month, it offloads the internal chaos and anxiety that brokers don't solve.

## Risks and kill criteria
1.  **Risk: Importers are unwilling to pay for a tool on top of existing broker fees.** Kill if the validation plan fails to get 5 verbal commitments to pay.
2.  **Risk: The operational pain of data collection is not severe enough to warrant a dedicated product.** Kill if feedback from demo calls consistently indicates that a simple spreadsheet template is "good enough."
3.  **Risk: Customer acquisition cost via paid search is too high.** Kill if, after launch, a sustainable CAC below $350 cannot be achieved.

## What I learned today
- A non-discretionary compliance task tied to every single core transaction (like an ocean shipment) is a powerful driver for a B2B tool.
- The true pain is often not just the government penalty, but the secondary operational costs (delays, inspections, legal fees) that accompany a compliance failure.
- A valuable wedge can be to create a tool that helps a customer be a better client to their existing service provider (importer → customs broker), rather than trying to replace the provider directly.
- Enforcement trends are a critical "Why Now?" catalyst. A regulation that has existed for years can become a source of acute pain overnight when a government agency decides to increase enforcement.

## Why this beats yesterday's idea
This opportunity is stronger than the "NYC Building Owner's Local Law 97 Compliance Forecaster." While both are driven by high-penalty regulations, the ISF pain is more frequent (per shipment vs. annual), the deadline is more immediate (24 hours pre-loading vs. a 2030 cliff), and the buyer base (any US importer) is far larger and less geographically constrained than NYC building owners. The wallet proof is also more direct, with importers already paying a per-transaction fee for this specific task.
