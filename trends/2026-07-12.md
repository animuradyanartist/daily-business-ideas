## Trend 1: AI-Automated Architectural & Construction Plan Review

**What's happening** — Architecture, engineering, and construction (AEC) firms are adopting AI-powered software to automatically review building plans for compliance with building codes and to detect design errors before construction begins. These tools ingest 2D PDF drawings and run them against thousands of rules.

**Why now** — Manual plan review is a significant bottleneck in the permitting and construction process, taking hours and catching only 60-80% of errors. This leads to costly rework, with the US construction industry losing an estimated $31 billion annually. In response, a new category of AI tools like PlanCheckPro, Helonic, and InspectMind AI has emerged. These platforms can analyze a full set of construction documents in minutes, flagging code violations with specific citations and identifying cross-disciplinary conflicts (e.g., an HVAC duct clashing with a structural beam). Governments are also driving adoption; Canada's National Research Council is actively seeking AI solutions to accelerate building permit approvals.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will move from pre-submission "error checking" to "agentic" compliance management throughout the design lifecycle. AI agents will be embedded directly in design software, providing real-time feedback on code compliance as architects work. These systems will also translate natural language building codes into machine-readable rules, allowing for instant adaptation to the thousands of local code amendments across different jurisdictions.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Architect's AI Compliance Kit." This digital product would provide Notion templates for managing the permit submission process, paired with a guide on how to use an affordable AI plan review tool to de-risk submissions and reduce revision cycles with municipal reviewers.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from May, June, and July 2026 describe a competitive and maturing market for AI-powered plan review, with a clear ROI based on reducing costly rework and accelerating permit timelines.

## Trend 2: AI-Powered Employee Relations & HR Investigations

**What's happening** — HR and Employee Relations (ER) teams are adopting specialized AI platforms to manage workplace investigations and employee disputes. These tools assist with case documentation, analysis of unstructured data (like witness statements), and ensuring procedural consistency.

**Why now** — Hybrid work, increased regulatory scrutiny, and a rise in employee-generated legalistic complaints (often drafted with consumer AI) are creating an administrative crisis for HR teams. In response, platforms like HR Acuity, Case IQ, and AllVoices are embedding AI to automate the most time-consuming parts of an investigation. These tools can summarize witness interviews, draft incident reports, and analyze case data to identify systemic issues, allowing ER professionals to focus on human judgment rather than manual documentation. In 2025, 70% of organizations experimented with or deployed AI for ER work, signaling rapid adoption.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will move from "documentation assistance" to "agentic" risk management. AI agents will monitor internal communication channels for signals of potential conflict, provide managers with real-time coaching on how to handle sensitive conversations, and assemble a complete, defensible audit trail for every stage of an investigation. This creates a market for "The Manager's ER Playbook," a digital product teaching leaders how to use these tools to handle issues fairly and reduce legal exposure.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Small Business HR Investigation Kit." This product would provide Notion templates for managing a fair and consistent investigation process, paired with a guide on how to use AI tools to securely document interviews and generate legally defensible case summaries.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from June and July 2026 confirm a mature and growing market for AI-powered ER software, driven by clear needs for efficiency, compliance, and risk mitigation in a complex employment landscape.

## Trend 3: AI-Powered Litigation Support for Small Law Firms

**What's happening** — Small law firms and solo practitioners are adopting AI-powered tools to automate the document-intensive workflows of litigation, specifically eDiscovery, deposition analysis, and medical record review. This allows them to handle complex cases that were previously only manageable by larger firms with more staff.

**Why now** — The volume of digital evidence (eDiscovery) in even small cases has become unmanageable for manual review. AI tools like Everlaw and Logikcull use predictive coding to surface relevant documents, while platforms like Sonix and Steno's Transcript Genius automate the summarization and analysis of deposition transcripts. For personal injury firms, tools like Dodonai can turn thousands of pages of medical records into a structured chronology in minutes. This technology gives smaller firms a competitive advantage by leveling the playing field and reducing non-billable hours.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will move from automating discrete tasks to providing an "agentic" case strategy co-pilot. AI agents will analyze all case documents—from initial client intake to discovery and depositions—to identify key themes, flag inconsistencies in testimony, suggest follow-up questions for witnesses, and help build a narrative timeline for trial. The focus will shift from "finding the document" to "understanding the story."

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could develop "The Litigator's AI Playbook." This digital product would provide a Notion-based system for managing case files and trial preparation, combined with a guide on how to build a cost-effective AI stack for eDiscovery, deposition analysis, and document review.

**Confidence:** high — Numerous sources from April through July 2026 confirm a mature and highly competitive market for AI litigation support tools, with a clear value proposition for small firms focused on efficiency and leveling the playing field.

## Patterns I noticed today

- For licensed professionals, the most compelling value proposition is not just saving time, but mitigating the specific risks that could jeopardize their license and livelihood.
- A market dominated by a complex, expensive, all-in-one incumbent (like Autodesk) is often a prime environment for a simple, elegant tool that does one critical job perfectly.
- The price of a B2B SaaS tool can be effectively anchored against the cost of the "insurance" the buyer already pays for the same risk (e.g., professional liability premiums).
