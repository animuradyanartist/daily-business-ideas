## Trend 1: AI-Automated Home Health Compliance (EVV)

**What's happening** — Home health agencies are adopting AI-powered software to automate compliance with federally mandated Electronic Visit Verification (EVV) rules. These tools move beyond simple GPS clock-ins to proactively manage exceptions, create audit-ready documentation, and prevent revenue loss from non-compliant visits.

**Why now** — With the grace period for EVV enforcement now over, state Medicaid programs are aggressively auditing agencies and denying claims for non-compliant visits. This has created an urgent, revenue-gating pain point. In response, a new generation of AI-native home care platforms is emerging. These systems use AI agents to automatically contact caregivers about missed clock-ins, resolve documentation errors without human intervention, and ensure every visit record is complete before it becomes a billing problem. A recent industry survey found that ensuring compliance is the top business priority for agencies, and over a third are specifically deploying AI for this purpose.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "compliance automation" to "agentic" revenue cycle management. An AI agent will manage the entire visit-to-cash process. It will not only ensure EVV compliance at the point of care but will also automatically generate claims, scrub them for errors against specific payer rules, and manage the submission and reconciliation process. This will collapse the time between visit completion and payment, directly improving cash flow for agencies operating on thin margins.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Home Health Agency's EVV Audit Kit." This digital product would provide a guide for small agencies on how to implement AI-powered EVV tools to become audit-ready. It would include Notion templates for standardizing caregiver training on EVV procedures and checklists for conducting internal compliance audits to find and fix issues before they result in denied claims.

**Confidence:** high — A federally mandated compliance rule is creating a non-discretionary, revenue-gating problem for a large, fragmented market of SMBs. AI tools that directly prevent revenue loss have an exceptionally clear and powerful ROI.

## Trend 2: AI-Automated M&A Due Diligence

**What's happening** — Legal and corporate development teams are widely adopting AI to automate the document review process in M&A due diligence. These tools ingest the entire contents of a virtual data room (VDR), automatically extracting key terms, identifying risks, and allowing deal teams to query thousands of documents using natural language.

**Why now** — The volume of data in M&A deals makes manual review a competitive liability. AI is now a standard component of due diligence, with a 2025 survey showing 86% of dealmakers have integrated it into their workflows. AI platforms can now compress review timelines by 70-80% by automatically flagging high-risk clauses, change-of-control provisions, and financial liabilities that human reviewers might miss. This shifts the work of expensive legal teams from manual search to high-value strategic analysis.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "document review" to "agentic" deal intelligence. An AI agent will monitor a data room in real time, cross-referencing new documents with existing findings to build a dynamic risk model of the target company. It will provide deal teams with proactive alerts ("A new supply contract was uploaded that conflicts with the change-of-control clause in the master service agreement") and allow them to run simulations ("What is the combined financial risk if all contracts with non-standard indemnity clauses are triggered?").

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Founder's AI Data Room Prep Kit." This digital product would guide startup founders on how to prepare for a fundraising or M&A process. It would include Notion templates for organizing company documents into a "diligence-ready" structure and a library of prompts for using AI to pre-screen their own contracts for the types of red flags that investors and acquirers look for.

**Confidence:** high — A high-stakes, time-sensitive business process with a catastrophic cost of failure is being addressed by AI tools that provide a clear and measurable advantage. Adoption is already mainstream among professional firms.

## Trend 3: AI as a College Admissions Co-Pilot

**What's happening** — High school students are using a new generation of purpose-built AI tools to navigate the college application process. These platforms act as specialized coaches, providing essay feedback, application planning, and interview prep based on proprietary admissions data, moving beyond the capabilities of general-purpose chatbots.

**Why now** — While most universities prohibit students from submitting AI-written essays, they broadly permit the use of AI for brainstorming and feedback. This has created a market for "guardrail" AI tools that assist without ghostwriting. Platforms like Prepory's Rory are trained on thousands of real essays and coaching sessions to provide specific, actionable feedback that helps students improve their own writing. These tools level the playing field for students who lack access to expensive human admissions coaches.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "essay feedback" to "agentic" application management. An AI co-pilot will manage the entire application timeline for multiple universities. It will create a personalized roadmap, track deadlines, analyze a student's profile to suggest best-fit schools and extracurriculars, and provide interactive interview practice. On the university side, admissions offices will increasingly use their own AI to perform initial screenings of applications, making the quality and authenticity of the student's submission even more critical.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator with a background in English-learning content is perfectly positioned to build "The AI-Assisted College Essay Kit." This digital product would be a guide that teaches students how to use AI tools ethically and effectively as a writing coach. It would include a structured workflow for brainstorming and drafting, Notion templates for organizing personal stories and mapping them to specific essay prompts, and a library of advanced prompts for getting high-quality, nuanced feedback from AI assistants.

**Confidence:** high — A massive, high-anxiety consumer market is rapidly adopting AI. The clear "rules of engagement" set by universities are creating a specific need for tools and educational products that help students use AI as a coach, not a ghostwriter.

## Patterns I noticed today

- Federally mandated compliance workflows (like EVV) create the most durable and non-negotiable pain points for SMBs. The government forcing a change is a powerful market-creation event.
- In a market with established all-in-one incumbents, the most effective wedge is not to build a better all-in-one, but to build a best-in-class point solution for the single most expensive failure point in the incumbent's workflow.
- A government audit that quantifies the dollar amount of non-compliance (e.g., the NY Comptroller's $97.6M report) is the most powerful piece of marketing collateral possible for a compliance-driven product.
- Reimbursement pressure from a central payer (like CMS) can increase, not decrease, the demand for tools that prevent revenue leakage, as businesses can no longer afford operational sloppiness.
