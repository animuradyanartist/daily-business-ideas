## Trend 1: AI-Powered Performance Reviews for Managers

**What's happening** — Managers are adopting AI tools to automate the drafting of employee performance reviews. These platforms ingest data from various sources—such as meeting notes, goals, and feedback—to generate structured, data-driven evaluations, reducing the administrative burden of review cycles.

**Why now** — Writing performance reviews is a time-consuming and often biased process that managers universally dislike. New AI tools from HR platforms like Lattice and standalone generators like Junia and Vondy are productizing this workflow. They connect to performance data to create drafts grounded in an employee's actual work, helping managers overcome writer's block and the limitations of human memory. These tools provide structured templates and can even suggest SMART goals, ensuring reviews are consistent and actionable.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will move from draft generation to full-cycle performance intelligence. AI agents will proactively flag performance issues or achievements in real-time, providing managers with continuous insights instead of a year-end summary. The most advanced systems will act as coaching co-pilots, analyzing performance data to suggest specific, data-backed talking points for the manager to use in the live review conversation. This will create a market for "Performance Conversation Kits"—digital products that provide the frameworks and prompts to manage these AI-assisted discussions effectively.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator can build and sell "The Manager's Performance Toolkit." This could be a digital product that combines Notion templates for organizing performance notes with a library of prompts for using AI to generate fair, effective, and well-documented reviews for different roles and performance levels.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from March to May 2026 confirm the rapid emergence of AI tools specifically designed to automate the writing of performance reviews, addressing a significant and recurring pain point for managers in organizations of all sizes.

## Trend 2: AI-Generated Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)

**What's happening** — Businesses are using AI to automatically generate Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and other process documentation. A new class of tools can observe a user performing a task via screen recording and instantly convert the actions into a step-by-step guide with annotated screenshots.

**Why now** — Creating and maintaining SOPs is critical for scaling operations but is a tedious manual process that teams rarely prioritize. Tools like Tango, Scribe, and Vidocu automate this workflow entirely. A user simply records themselves performing a task, and the AI generates a structured, editable document. This "show, don't tell" approach dramatically reduces the friction of knowledge capture, turning a multi-hour writing task into a few minutes of recording.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from simple documentation to creating interactive, AI-powered training modules. Instead of a static PDF, the output will be a "live" SOP where new employees can be guided step-by-step within the actual software they are using. AI agents will monitor adherence to these procedures, flagging deviations and automatically updating the SOP when the underlying software or process changes. This creates an opportunity for "Business Playbook Kits"—digital products that teach solopreneurs and small teams how to use these tools to build a complete, automated operations manual.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator can build and sell "The SOP Automation Kit." This digital product could provide a Notion-based framework for organizing a company's processes and a guide with best practices for using AI tools to capture and maintain a full library of SOPs with minimal effort.

**Confidence:** high — Numerous sources from February, March, and April 2026 confirm the rise of AI-powered tools that automatically generate SOPs from screen recordings, solving a universal business problem of process documentation.

## Trend 3: AI-Generated Consulting Deliverables

**What's happening** — Consultants are using AI to automate the creation of core client deliverables like reports, presentations, and data analysis spreadsheets. These tools are moving beyond simple text generation to produce structured, client-ready artifacts from raw data and prompts.

**Why now** — A significant portion of a consultant's time is spent on the "production" of deliverables rather than on strategic thinking. AI tools are now being purpose-built for consulting workflows to accelerate this production. Platforms like auxi and Beautiful.ai can generate complex, brand-compliant slide decks from prompts, while other tools assist in structuring reports and analyzing data. This allows consultants to go from raw client notes to a polished first draft of a deliverable in minutes, not hours.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The market will shift from generating individual documents to creating interconnected "deliverable systems." A consultant will input notes from a client meeting, and an AI agent will generate a suite of related artifacts: a spreadsheet with the core analysis, a PowerPoint summarizing the findings for executives, and a Word document with the detailed report. The key differentiator will be the AI's ability to maintain a consistent narrative and data integrity across all formats. This creates a market for "Consulting Engagement Kits"—digital products that provide the templates and AI workflows for managing a full client project, from proposal to final report.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator with a design background can build and sell "The Independent Consultant's Kit." This product could bundle professionally designed Canva and PowerPoint templates for key deliverables (proposals, kickoff decks, final reports) with a Notion-based guide and prompt library for using AI to generate the content for each.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from March, April, and May 2026 confirm that AI tools are being rapidly adopted by consultants to automate the creation of client-facing reports, presentations, and other core deliverables, directly impacting their core workflow.

## Status update: AI-Assisted UX Research Synthesis

**Originally flagged:** 2026-04-30

**Current status:** 🚀 accelerating

**What changed** — The use of AI in UX research has become a baseline expectation in 2026, with adoption for tasks like transcription and synthesis growing significantly. The most impactful application is the automated analysis and thematic clustering of qualitative data from interviews and surveys. Platforms are moving beyond simple summarization to become research hubs that ingest video, audio, and text, allowing researchers to query the entire dataset with natural language. The human researcher's role is shifting from manual data processing to higher-level judgment, prompt design, and validating AI-surfaced insights.

## Patterns I noticed today

-   AI is automating the high-stakes, high-effort "deliverable" moments in professional workflows. Whether it's a performance review, an SOP, or a client report, AI is being used to produce the formal artifacts that document value and drive decisions.
-   The value proposition of many new AI tools is the conversion of unstructured, messy inputs (screen recordings, meeting notes, raw data) into structured, professional outputs (SOPs, performance reviews, slide decks).
-   For every professional workflow being automated by AI, a secondary market is emerging for "Scaffolding Kits"—digital products that provide the templates, prompts, and strategic frameworks needed to manage these AI systems effectively.
