## Trend 1: AI-Powered "Deposition Co-pilot" for Litigators

**What's happening** — Law firms are adopting AI platforms that act as a "co-pilot" for the entire deposition lifecycle. These tools assist attorneys by analyzing case files to generate deposition outlines, summarizing transcripts to identify key testimony, and even providing real-time support during the deposition itself.

**Why now** — Preparing for and analyzing depositions is one of the most time-consuming parts of litigation. A single transcript can be hundreds of pages long, and reviewing multiple transcripts for inconsistencies is a massive manual effort. AI platforms from legal tech companies like Clio, CoCounsel, and TrialBase are automating this workflow. These tools ingest case materials and transcripts to automatically generate summaries, create timelines, and flag contradictions between witnesses, reducing hours or days of work to minutes. Some platforms are even providing AI-powered deposition simulators for training and live assistance during proceedings.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from post-deposition analysis to "agentic" real-time intelligence. An AI agent will listen to live deposition testimony, cross-reference it with the entire case file in real-time, and surface relevant documents or prior inconsistent statements directly to the attorney's screen. The agent will act as a second brain for the litigator, suggesting follow-up questions and identifying opportunities for impeachment on the fly.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could develop "The Litigator's AI Deposition Prep Kit." This digital product would provide a toolkit for attorneys at small firms to structure their case files for optimal use with these AI platforms. It would include Notion templates for creating a "master case chronology" and a guide with prompts for using general AI tools to perform a preliminary analysis of key documents before uploading them to a specialized legal AI system.

**Confidence:** high — This trend automates a high-cost, high-friction, and recurring workflow for a professional class with a high willingness to pay. The ROI in terms of hours saved and strategic advantage gained is immediate and clear.

## Trend 2: AI-Assisted Divorce & Co-Parenting

**What's happening** — A new category of AI tools is emerging to help both legal professionals and individuals navigate the administrative and emotional complexities of divorce and co-parenting. These tools assist with drafting agreements, organizing financial disclosures, and de-escalating high-conflict communication.

**Why now** — Divorce is a high-friction, emotionally charged process. AI is being applied to streamline the logistical aspects and reduce conflict. For lawyers, AI tools are automating the drafting of parenting plans and the analysis of financial records. For individuals, AI is being used as a "neutral third party" to rewrite emotionally charged messages into calm, business-like communication for co-parenting apps. AI can also help organize schedules, generate ideas for parenting strategies, and prepare individuals for conversations with their attorneys.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "communication assistance" to "agentic" co-parenting coordination. An AI agent will connect to both parents' calendars and communication platforms. It will monitor for scheduling conflicts, propose solutions for holiday planning, and act as a neutral intermediary for negotiating minor changes to the parenting plan. By creating an objective, data-driven buffer between the parents, the agent will handle the high-frequency logistical work, allowing them to focus on higher-level decisions about their children's welfare.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could develop "The AI-Ready Co-Parenting Plan." This digital product would be a toolkit for divorcing parents. It would include a comprehensive set of Notion templates for outlining parenting goals, schedules, and financial responsibilities. The templates would be structured as a series of prompts and questions that parents can then feed into an AI to generate a detailed first draft of a comprehensive parenting plan, which they can then take to a mediator or lawyer.

**Confidence:** high — This trend addresses a universal, high-anxiety life event with significant financial and emotional costs. Tools that reduce conflict and administrative burden for both professionals and individuals have a very strong value proposition.

## Trend 3: AI-Automated Environmental Permitting & Compliance

**What's happening** — Government agencies and regulated industries are adopting AI platforms to automate the complex, document-intensive process of environmental permitting and compliance. These tools are used to draft permits, review applications for completeness, and monitor ongoing compliance data.

**Why now** — Environmental permitting is a major bottleneck for infrastructure and energy projects, often relying on manual, paper-heavy workflows. AI is now being used to streamline this process. For regulators, platforms like PermitPro are ingesting applications, checking them for completeness against jurisdictional rules, and generating complete, regulation-compliant draft permits. For companies, AI tools are helping to prepare higher-quality applications and conduct environmental impact assessments more efficiently. This reduces rework and accelerates review cycles for both applicants and agencies.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "document processing" to "agentic" compliance monitoring. An AI agent will be connected to a facility's real-time environmental monitoring data (e.g., water discharge sensors). The agent will continuously compare this data against the limits set in the environmental permit. If it detects an anomaly that could lead to a violation, it will automatically draft an incident report and alert the relevant personnel, turning compliance from a reactive reporting exercise into a proactive, real-time management system.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could develop "The Environmental Consultant's AI Permitting Toolkit." This digital product would provide a guide for consultants and project managers on how to prepare environmental data and project plans for submission to these AI-powered regulatory systems. It would include Notion templates for structuring environmental impact data and checklists for ensuring applications are complete before submission.

**Confidence:** high — This trend automates a mandatory, high-cost, and extremely slow government-mandated workflow. The value proposition of accelerating critical infrastructure projects while improving regulatory consistency is powerful for both public and private sectors.

## Patterns I noticed today

- The most potent business risks are those that threaten a company's core operational inputs (e.g., labor, raw materials, operating licenses). A solution that insures that input is a painkiller.
- A market's willingness to pay for software can be accurately benchmarked by what they currently pay for manual, service-based alternatives to solve the same problem.
- For compliance products, the most valuable initial wedge may not be the most complex part of the process (e.g., filing), but the most chronically painful and high-risk part (e.g., ongoing record-keeping).
