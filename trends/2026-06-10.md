## Trend 1: AI-Powered Accounting & Tax Automation

**What's happening** — Accounting and tax firms are deploying AI to automate core professional workflows, moving beyond simple data entry to handle complex tasks like document analysis, compliance checks, and drafting financial reports. These tools are being embedded directly into practice management and audit platforms.

**Why now** — A significant portion of accounting work is document-heavy and rules-based, making it ideal for automation. A 2026 report indicates a third of tax firms are already using generative AI, with 66% planning to integrate it. Platforms from companies like Fathom and DualEntry are using AI to automatically extract data from invoices and receipts, generate narrative commentary for financial reports, and cross-reference data for compliance checks, reducing tax preparation time by up to 65%. This allows professionals to shift from manual data processing to higher-value advisory work.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will accelerate toward "agentic" accounting, where AI systems autonomously manage entire workflows like accounts payable or monthly close processes. These AI agents will not only process transactions but also perform variance analysis, flag anomalies for human review, and generate complete, audit-ready workpapers. This creates a market for "AI-Ready Firm" toolkits that teach accountants how to implement, govern, and audit these semi-autonomous systems.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator can build "The Modern Accountant's AI Toolkit." This digital product could provide a Notion-based system for managing client workflows, paired with guides and checklists for using AI tools to automate document processing, accelerate financial reporting, and scale advisory services.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from March, May, and June 2026 confirm the rapid adoption of AI to automate end-to-end accounting and tax workflows, driven by clear ROI in efficiency and the need to shift professionals to advisory roles.

## Trend 2: AI-Powered Veterinary Practice Operations

**What's happening** — Veterinary clinics are adopting AI-powered software to automate administrative and clinical documentation workflows. These tools act as an "AI scribe" during appointments, listening to conversations and automatically generating structured medical records (SOAP notes).

**Why now** — Veterinarians are burdened by administrative work, reducing time available for patient care. A new class of purpose-built AI platforms from companies like Shepherd, Otto, and HappyDoc is emerging to solve this. These tools integrate with existing Practice Information Management Systems (PIMS) and use AI to transcribe and structure conversations into medical notes, summarize patient history, and even assist with differential diagnoses. This allows the veterinarian to focus on the animal and client rather than on typing.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from documentation assistance to a "virtual practice manager." AI will not only handle clinical notes but also automate client communications, manage appointment scheduling, and analyze practice-wide data to identify operational bottlenecks or revenue opportunities. The most advanced systems will use AI-powered analyzers for in-clinic diagnostics, providing near-instant results for bloodwork and cytology.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator can build "The Efficient Vet Clinic's AI Playbook." This digital product could provide a Notion-based system with templates for standardizing clinic workflows, paired with a guide for implementing an AI scribe and automation platform to reduce administrative overhead and improve patient throughput.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from March, April, and May 2026 confirm the launch and adoption of AI platforms specifically designed to automate the high-friction documentation and administrative workflows within veterinary practices.

## Trend 3: AI-Automated Mortgage Document Processing

**What's happening** — Mortgage lenders are deploying AI platforms to automate the labor-intensive process of document intake, classification, and data extraction. These systems can process a complete loan file in minutes, a task that previously took hours of manual work.

**Why now** — High operational costs and pressure to shorten closing times are driving lenders to adopt automation. AI platforms are now capable of handling the complexity of mortgage paperwork, using computer vision and natural language processing to classify hundreds of document types and extract key data with over 90% accuracy. This allows loan officers and underwriters to focus on exceptions and complex cases rather than manual data entry and verification.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will move from data extraction to "agentic" underwriting workflows. AI agents will not only process documents but also perform initial compliance checks, verify data against policy rules, and prepare a preliminary underwriting decision with a full audit trail. As this technology becomes table stakes, the risk focus will shift to validating the authenticity of AI-generated documents, creating a new need for "truth protocol" services that verify data integrity.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator can build "The Modern MLO's AI Workflow Kit." This digital product could provide a Notion-based system for managing a loan pipeline, paired with guides and checklists for using AI-powered document processing tools to accelerate closings and improve client communication.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from March, April, and May 2026 confirm that AI is being rapidly deployed to automate the entire front-end of the mortgage workflow, addressing the persistent, high-cost problems of manual document processing and slow cycle times.

## Patterns I noticed today

- AI is systematically productizing the "back office" for licensed professionals. Whether it's an accountant, a veterinarian, or a mortgage broker, AI platforms are emerging to automate the high-friction, non-billable administrative and documentation work that surrounds the core professional service.
- The value proposition of AI is moving from a "tool" that a human uses to an "agent" that a human governs. The opportunity is in building the scaffolding—the toolkits, playbooks, and training—that helps non-technical managers oversee these new digital workforces.
- For every professional workflow being automated, a secondary market is emerging for compliance and governance. As AI agents take on more work, the need for auditable, transparent, and explainable AI systems becomes a critical business requirement.
