## Trend 1: AI-Powered Employee Attrition Prediction

**What's happening** — Companies are deploying AI-powered HR platforms to predict which employees are at risk of leaving. These systems analyze workforce data—such as performance reviews, engagement scores, and communication patterns—to identify flight risks months in advance, allowing for proactive retention efforts.

**Why now** — The cost of replacing an employee is high, and organizations are shifting from reactive exit interviews to proactive, data-driven retention. AI platforms can now analyze vast amounts of data to find subtle patterns that indicate an employee might be disengaging. Recent analyses show these systems can predict turnover with up to 87% accuracy, giving managers a significant lead time to intervene. This is becoming a standard practice, with 80% of HR departments expected to use predictive analytics in their daily workflow by the end of 2026.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from risk identification to "agentic" retention. AI systems will not only flag at-risk employees but will also recommend and even initiate personalized interventions, such as suggesting a tailored learning path, flagging a need for a manager check-in, or identifying internal mobility opportunities. This creates a market for "Retention Playbook" toolkits that teach managers how to interpret these AI signals and execute the recommended retention strategies effectively.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The SMB Manager's AI Retention Kit." This digital product would provide a Notion-based system for tracking employee engagement signals and conducting effective retention conversations, paired with a guide on using affordable HR analytics tools to identify and support at-risk team members.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from March, April, May, and June 2026 confirm that businesses are rapidly adopting predictive AI to manage employee turnover proactively, driven by clear financial incentives and maturing technology.

## Trend 2: AI-Automated M&A Due Diligence

**What's happening** — Legal and financial teams are adopting AI platforms to automate the analysis of documents in mergers and acquisitions (M&A). These tools ingest entire virtual data rooms, extracting key clauses, identifying risks, and flagging anomalies from thousands of contracts in a fraction of the time required for manual review.

**Why now** — M&A timelines are compressed, while the volume of data required for diligence has exploded. AI platforms from companies like Luminance, Datasite, and Harvey are now standard practice for pre-signing diligence. They use natural language processing to analyze contracts for critical provisions like change-of-control triggers and liability caps, providing a consistent level of review across massive datasets that is impossible to achieve manually under pressure. This shifts the work of legal teams from repetitive document extraction to higher-value strategic analysis.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will move from AI as an "analysis tool" to an "orchestrated system" that manages the diligence workflow. AI agents will not only extract data but will also cross-reference findings between legal, financial, and operational documents, generate integrated risk reports, and even draft initial disclosure schedules. This creates a market for "AI-Powered Diligence" playbooks that teach deal teams how to structure their workflows around these tools to achieve faster, more accurate outcomes.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Small Business Acquirer's AI Diligence Kit." This digital product would provide a Notion-based system for managing a due diligence checklist and tracking findings, paired with a guide on using AI tools to analyze contracts and financial documents for small-scale acquisitions.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from March, April, and June 2026 confirm that AI is a standard, widely adopted tool for automating high-volume document analysis in the M&A diligence process, driven by the need for speed and accuracy.

## Trend 3: AI-Generated Architectural Visualization

**What's happening** — Architects and designers are using a new class of AI tools to rapidly generate high-quality conceptual visualizations from simple inputs like sketches, 3D massing models, or text prompts. This accelerates the early stages of design exploration and client communication.

**Why now** — The technology has moved from experimental text-to-image generators to purpose-built architectural platforms like Veras, Finch 3D, and TestFit that integrate directly into existing workflows in Revit, Rhino, and SketchUp. A recent survey shows 64% of architects are now experimenting with AI tools. These tools allow designers to test dozens of design variations in minutes, cutting down feasibility studies from weeks to hours and turning basic geometry into polished visuals almost instantly. This allows for faster iteration and clearer client communication much earlier in the design process.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from generating static images to creating "agentic" design systems. AI will not just visualize a single concept but will generate multiple, viable design options that adhere to specific constraints like local building codes, structural logic, and financial targets. This creates a market for "AI-Assisted Design" toolkits that teach architects how to use these generative tools as a creative partner, managing prompts and constraints to guide the AI toward buildable, code-compliant outcomes.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Freelance Architect's AI Presentation Kit." This digital product would provide Canva and Notion templates for client presentations, paired with a guide and prompt library for using AI visualization tools to quickly generate compelling concept imagery for residential and small commercial projects.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from March, April, May, and June 2026 confirm the rapid and widespread adoption of AI tools for conceptual visualization in architecture, driven by significant time savings and improved client communication.

## Status update: AI-Generated Technical Documentation

**Originally flagged:** 2026-05-04

**Current status:** 🚀 accelerating

**What changed** — The market has matured from AI as a "drafting assistant" to AI as a core part of the documentation "operating system." The focus is now on maintenance and AI-readiness. Leading platforms like Mintlify and HappySupport are differentiating by their ability to keep documentation automatically synchronized with product changes in the codebase. A new critical requirement is making documentation readable by other AI agents; platforms now automatically generate `llms.txt` files and support the Model Context Protocol (MCP) to feed accurate context to AI coding assistants. This reflects a major shift where nearly half of traffic to documentation sites now comes from AI agents, not humans.

## Patterns I noticed today

-   AI is systematically automating high-stakes, information-intensive "gate" decisions in business, such as predicting employee churn, assessing M&A risk, and validating architectural feasibility.
-   The value proposition for B2B AI is shifting from "faster" to "more comprehensive." The ability to analyze an entire dataset (every contract, every employee record) without sampling is creating a new standard of diligence and foresight.
-   For every professional workflow being automated, a secondary market is emerging for "governance toolkits"—digital products that teach non-technical managers how to implement, oversee, and interpret the outputs of these new AI systems.
