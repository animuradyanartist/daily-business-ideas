## Trend 1: AI-Automated Amazon FBA Reimbursement

**What's happening** — Amazon sellers are adopting specialized AI-powered tools to automate the process of identifying and claiming reimbursements for FBA inventory that is lost, damaged, or incorrectly handled by Amazon. These platforms audit complex Seller Central reports to surface eligible claims that sellers would otherwise miss.

**Why now** — Most FBA sellers leave 1-3% of revenue unclaimed due to the complexity of Amazon's reimbursement process, which requires cross-referencing multiple reports to find discrepancies. In response, a new class of AI tools has emerged to automate this audit. Platforms like Jarvio, SellerForge, and GlancePeak now use AI to scan up to 18 months of transaction data across multiple report types, automatically identifying discrepancies and preparing the necessary documentation for a seller to file a claim. This is a direct challenge to the incumbent model of "done-for-you" services that charge a 20-25% commission on recovered funds.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will move from "claim identification" to "agentic" financial operations for Amazon sellers. AI agents will not only find and prepare reimbursement claims but will manage the entire financial back office, from inventory reconciliation and fee audits to proactive cash flow forecasting. This creates a market for "The FBA Financial OS," a toolkit teaching sellers how to use these AI platforms to manage their business finances without hiring an agency.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Amazon Seller's Reimbursement Kit." This digital product would provide spreadsheet templates for manually tracking FBA inventory and fees, paired with a guide on how to use an affordable, flat-rate AI tool to automate the audit and claim preparation process, maximizing recovery without paying high commissions.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from May and June 2026 describe a clear market shift from high-commission manual services to flat-rate, AI-powered software that automates the specific, high-friction workflow of FBA reimbursement auditing.

## Trend 2: AI-Powered Plant Care & Disease Diagnosis

**What's happening** — A new generation of mobile apps is using AI, specifically computer vision, to provide home gardeners and plant owners with instant plant identification, disease diagnosis, and personalized care schedules.

**Why now** — The technology for mobile image recognition has matured, allowing apps to accurately identify thousands of plant species and common diseases from a single photo. Apps like Plantify, PlantSnapper, and PlantCare now act as an "AI botanist," diagnosing issues like root rot or pest infestations and providing step-by-step treatment plans. They also create personalized care schedules with smart reminders for watering and fertilizing based on the specific plant type, its location, and even the pot it's in. These tools are becoming comprehensive digital hubs for managing a plant collection.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from identification and reminders to "agentic" garden management. AI systems will integrate with local weather data and soil sensors to create dynamic, predictive care plans. These agents will not just remind users to water but will advise on optimal planting times, warn of impending pest outbreaks based on regional data, and provide proactive advice for maximizing harvests or blooms. This creates a market for "Smart Garden" toolkits that teach non-experts how to combine these AI apps with simple hardware for a more automated gardening experience.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The New Gardener's AI Toolkit." This digital product would provide a Notion-based planner for garden layouts and seasonal tasks, paired with a guide on using a top-rated AI plant care app to diagnose problems, create automated watering schedules, and manage plant health.

**Confidence:** high — Numerous app releases and updates in April, May, and June 2026 confirm a competitive market for AI-powered plant care apps that are moving beyond simple identification to offer sophisticated disease diagnosis and personalized care management.

## Trend 3: AI-Automated Influencer & Creator Deal Management

**What's happening** — Brands and agencies are adopting AI-powered platforms to automate the entire influencer marketing workflow, from creator discovery and outreach to contract negotiation and campaign management. These tools are moving beyond simple search to act as autonomous agents that manage deals end-to-end.

**Why now** — As brands increase spending on creator marketing, the manual process of finding, vetting, and negotiating with hundreds of influencers has become a major bottleneck. In response, platforms like GRIN, Creally, and Ainfluencer are deploying AI agents to handle these tasks at scale. These systems can analyze millions of creator profiles to find the best fit, automate personalized outreach and follow-ups, and manage contract negotiations and payments directly within the platform, turning a manual process into a repeatable system.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from workflow automation to "agentic" performance marketing. AI agents will not only manage the deal-making process but will also predict campaign success, autonomously allocate budgets to the highest-performing creators in real-time, and generate performance-based contracts. This creates a market for "The Solo Creator's AI Agent," a new class of tool that acts on behalf of the creator to find, negotiate, and manage brand deals.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Creator's Brand Deal OS." This digital product would provide Notion and Canva templates for creating a media kit and tracking brand deal opportunities, paired with a guide on how to use AI-powered marketplaces to find inbound deals and structure profitable, long-term partnerships.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from April, May, and June 2026 describe a mature and competitive market for AI platforms that automate the full lifecycle of influencer marketing, with a clear focus on solving the operational scaling challenges faced by brands and agencies.

## Status update: AI-Automated Meeting Workflows

**Originally flagged:** 2026-05-06

**Current status:** 🚀 accelerating

**What changed** — The market for AI meeting assistants has moved decisively beyond simple transcription and summarization. The core differentiator in 2026 is workflow automation—the ability to turn conversations into action. Leading tools now focus on extracting decisions and action items and automatically creating tasks in project management systems like Jira or Asana. The market is bifurcating between native tools included with platforms like Zoom and Microsoft Teams, and more advanced, third-party "agentic" platforms that automate the entire meeting lifecycle, from pre-call briefings to post-call CRM updates and follow-up emails.

## Patterns I noticed today

- A dominant, commission-based service model in a market (like FBA reimbursement agencies) is a strong signal for a SaaS opportunity. The wedge is a predictable, flat-rate price that offers a clear ROI.
- Platform-dependent businesses (Amazon sellers, creators on Instagram) carry immense risk, but also offer immense opportunity due to the standardized nature of the customer's problems and a single point of integration (the API).
- The value proposition of consumer AI is shifting from novelty identification ("what is this?") to ongoing, personalized management ("how do I care for this?").
- The most valuable B2B AI tools are moving from passive analysis (summarizing a meeting) to active execution (creating a task in Jira from the meeting).
