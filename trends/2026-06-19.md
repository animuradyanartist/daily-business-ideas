## Trend 1: AI-Powered Revenue Recovery for B2B

**What's happening** — Businesses, particularly in healthcare and consumer packaged goods (CPG), are deploying AI platforms to automate the identification and recovery of lost revenue from complex billing, invalid deductions, and underpaid claims. These tools move beyond simple automation to manage the entire dispute and recovery workflow.

**Why now** — Thin margins and complex billing processes with large enterprise partners (e.g., retailers, insurance payers) create significant "revenue leakage" that is too labor-intensive to recover manually. AI platforms from companies like Glimpse, Finortal, and Revecore automate the process by ingesting documents, identifying discrepancies, generating dispute packages, and tracking claims until the cash is recovered. One startup, Glimpse, which recently raised a $10M seed round, helps CPG brands recover up to 3% of top-line revenue lost to invalid retailer deductions.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "recovery" to "prevention." AI agents will analyze transaction data in real-time to flag and prevent revenue leakage before it occurs, such as by identifying a non-compliant purchase order before an invoice is sent. This creates a market for "Revenue Operations (RevOps) Governance" toolkits that teach finance teams how to manage these AI systems to protect cash flow proactively.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The CPG Brand's Deduction Recovery Kit." This digital product would provide Notion and spreadsheet templates for tracking retailer deductions, paired with a guide on how to use an AI-powered platform to automate the dispute and recovery process for invalid charges from major retailers like Walmart and Amazon.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from March, April, May, and June 2026, including funding announcements and platform launches, confirm the adoption of AI for automating the end-to-end revenue recovery workflow in B2B environments with complex billing.

## Trend 2: AI-Automated Commercial Real Estate (CRE) Operations

**What's happening** — Commercial real estate operators, investors, and developers are adopting AI platforms to automate high-friction administrative and operational workflows. The most immediate impact is in lease abstraction, where AI extracts key data from complex legal documents in minutes instead of hours.

**Why now** — The CRE industry is under pressure to improve operational efficiency, and manual data extraction is a significant bottleneck. A standard commercial lease can take 4-8 hours to abstract manually. AI-powered platforms from companies like VTS, Prophia, and LeaseWizard now use AI to pull critical data like renewal dates, rent schedules, and key clauses automatically. This allows teams to standardize data across large portfolios, ensure compliance, and make faster decisions. Adoption is widespread, with over 88% of CRE investors now piloting AI programs.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will move beyond document analysis to "agentic" asset management. AI agents will not only extract lease data but will also monitor it, automatically triggering workflows for critical dates, managing tenant communications, and coordinating maintenance based on lease obligations. This creates a market for "CRE AI Governance" toolkits that teach non-technical asset managers how to oversee these semi-autonomous operational systems.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The CRE Analyst's AI Toolkit." This digital product would offer a Notion-based system for managing due diligence and tracking lease portfolios, paired with a guide and prompt library for using AI tools to abstract lease documents and generate initial market research reports.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from April, May, and June 2026 confirm that AI is being rapidly adopted across the CRE industry, with lease abstraction being the most mature and impactful use case for improving operational efficiency.

## Trend 3: AI-Automated Food & Beverage Supply Chain Compliance

**What's happening** — Food and beverage manufacturers are adopting AI-powered platforms to automate the documentation and management of regulatory compliance. These tools are specifically designed to manage HACCP plans, supplier certifications, and traceability requirements mandated by regulations like FSMA 204.

**Why now** — The food industry faces immense pressure from complex, overlapping regulations (FDA, USDA, SQF, BRC) and the high cost of non-compliance. AI platforms from companies like Trustwell are purpose-built to automate the administrative burden. These systems continuously monitor regulatory databases for changes, track supplier documentation and expiration dates, and generate audit-ready records in real-time. This allows quality assurance teams to shift from manual paperwork to active risk management. The market for AI in food safety is projected to grow at over 30% annually.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will move from documentation automation to "agentic" quality assurance. AI agents will not just track compliance but will proactively manage it, automatically placing holds on ingredients from non-compliant suppliers or flagging production batches that deviate from safety parameters. This creates a market for "Food Safety AI" implementation guides that teach non-technical managers how to configure and oversee these systems.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Small Food Brand's FSMA Compliance Kit." This digital product would provide a Notion-based system for managing supplier documents and building a HACCP plan, paired with a guide on implementing an affordable AI platform to automate traceability records and prepare for audits.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from April, May, and June 2026 confirm that food manufacturers are adopting specialized AI platforms to automate the management of complex regulatory and supply chain compliance, driven by new rules like FSMA 204.

## Patterns I noticed today

- The most powerful B2B SaaS value propositions for SMBs are often found in workflows where they are structurally disadvantaged against large enterprise partners (e.g., recovering revenue, managing complex leases).
- A "revenue recovery" product has a clearer, more immediate ROI than a "cost savings" or "risk mitigation" product, making the sales process easier.
- The "worse substitute" for many modern B2B SaaS tools is not a direct competitor, but a complex, manual spreadsheet-and-email process run by an over-burdened employee. The product's primary job is to defeat that manual process.
