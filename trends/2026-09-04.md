## Trend 1: AI-Powered NYC Local Law 97 Compliance

**What's happening** — Building owners in New York City are adopting AI-powered platforms to manage compliance with Local Law 97, a regulation imposing escalating carbon emission caps. These tools automate emissions tracking, calculate potential penalties, and model the ROI of decarbonization projects.

**Why now** — The financial penalties for non-compliance are now an active reality. The law, which affects buildings over 25,000 square feet, imposes a recurring annual penalty of $268 per metric ton of CO2 equivalent over a building's limit. With the second round of compliance reports for 2025 emissions filed in mid-2026, building owners are shifting from awareness to action. AI platforms from companies like Bright Power AI and Guardia are emerging to provide a clear, actionable path to compliance by continuously monitoring energy data and simulating the impact of specific upgrades, such as HVAC improvements or building envelope work.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "penalty calculation" to "agentic" capital planning. An AI agent will not just report on a building's current emissions exposure but will generate a fully-costed, multi-year decarbonization roadmap. It will analyze the building's systems, identify the highest-ROI capital improvements, and model the long-term financial impact of different investment scenarios against the tightening 2030 and 2035 emissions caps, acting as an automated energy consultant.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could develop "The Small Landlord's LL97 Action Plan." This digital product would provide owners of a single building with a toolkit to prepare for using an AI compliance platform. It would include Notion templates for organizing utility data and maintenance records, and a guide for evaluating the ROI of common retrofits before engaging a high-cost engineering firm.

**Confidence:** high — A geographically-concentrated market faces a non-discretionary, high-penalty, deadline-driven mandate. AI tools that provide clarity and a path to financial savings have an exceptionally strong value proposition.

## Trend 2: Generative AI for Synthetic Datasets

**What's happening** — Companies are using generative AI platforms to create high-fidelity, privacy-preserving synthetic datasets for training machine learning models and testing software. These tools generate artificial data that statistically mirrors real-world production data without exposing sensitive customer information.

**Why now** — Access to realistic data is a primary bottleneck in AI development and software testing. Real data is locked behind privacy rules (GDPR, HIPAA) and security concerns. Gartner predicts that 75% of businesses will use generative AI to create synthetic data by 2026, up from less than 5% in 2023. Platforms from vendors like Tonic.ai, MOSTLY AI, and Gretel are meeting this demand by using advanced AI models to generate structured tabular data that preserves complex relationships and statistical distributions, enabling teams to build and test more robustly.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "data generation" to "agentic" data operations. An AI agent will not just create a static dataset but will manage the entire data provisioning workflow. A developer will request a specific type of data in natural language (e.g., "I need a test database with 10,000 users from Germany who have placed orders in the last 30 days"). The agent will then generate the data, provision a sandboxed database, and provide the developer with secure credentials, creating a self-service, on-demand data infrastructure.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Data Scientist's Synthetic Data Starter Kit." This digital product would provide a guide for data professionals on how to use open-source libraries like Synthetic Data Vault (SDV) to create their first tabular models. It would include Notion templates for documenting data schemas and a guide with prompts for evaluating the statistical quality of the generated data against the real-world source.

**Confidence:** high — A fundamental bottleneck in the rapidly growing AI development market is being solved by a new class of tools with a clear value proposition around privacy, compliance, and development velocity.

## Trend 3: AI-Augmented Platform Engineering

**What's happening** — Platform engineering teams are adopting AI to design, build, and operate the Internal Developer Platforms (IDPs) that their developers use to ship software. AI is being used to automate the creation of infrastructure-as-code, manage resources, and provide a natural language interface for developers to interact with complex cloud infrastructure.

**Why now** — Platform engineering has become a standard practice for improving developer productivity, but building and maintaining an IDP is a significant effort. The rise of generative AI is now being applied to the platform itself. AI engineering platforms are emerging that allow developers to request infrastructure or services in plain language. This extends the self-service "golden path" concept by allowing AI agents, not just humans, to safely consume infrastructure, governed by centrally-managed policies and guardrails.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "AI-assisted platform creation" to "agentic" platform management. The IDP will become a self-optimizing system. An AI agent will monitor application performance, infrastructure costs, and security posture. It will then autonomously suggest and, with approval, execute changes to the underlying platform—like optimizing a Kubernetes configuration for cost or patching a newly discovered vulnerability—acting as a proactive, AI-powered Site Reliability Engineer (SRE).

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Platform Engineer's AI Toolkit." This digital product would provide engineers with a guide to integrating AI into their existing IDP. It would include a curated set of prompts for generating Infrastructure-as-Code (IaC) using tools like GitHub Copilot, and Notion templates for creating a "golden path" documentation site for their internal developer customers.

**Confidence:** high — A well-established engineering discipline (Platform Engineering) is being augmented by AI to solve its core challenges of complexity and cognitive load. The trend is backed by major industry players and addresses a clear need for increased developer productivity.

**Originally flagged:** 2026-05-10

The focus has shifted from generic text generation to end-to-end, compliance-driven workflows, especially for government and enterprise bids. ## Patterns I noticed today

- A geographically-constrained market (e.g., one city) can be far more attractive than a national one if the regulation is specific, the penalties are severe, and the buyer density is high.
- A future, date-certain regulatory cliff (like the LL97 2030 deadline) is a powerful catalyst for creating a market for planning and forecasting tools *today*.
- The most valuable B2B AI tools provide a "governance layer" on top of a complex process, whether it's software development, data access, or regulatory compliance.
