## Trend 1: AI-Powered Operations for Marine Businesses

**What's happening** — Operators of marinas, boatyards, and commercial fleets are adopting AI-powered platforms to automate core business functions, including procurement, fleet performance management, and customer lead qualification.

**Why now** — The marine industry, traditionally reliant on manual processes, is facing pressure to digitize to improve efficiency and meet compliance standards. New AI-native platforms are productizing complex operational workflows. Tools like Cradle are using an AI concierge to provide instant quotes for marina slips, while SpecTec's AMOS Procure Smart uses AI to automate purchasing and inventory management for fleets. Veson Nautical has integrated its "CoCaptain" AI across its entire platform to assist with voyage and fuel optimization.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from task-specific automation to "digital twin" simulation for entire marine operations. An AI-powered virtual model of a marina or fleet will ingest real-time data on asset location, weather, and market demand. Operators will run "what-if" scenarios to optimize slip allocation for maximum revenue, predict maintenance needs for vessels before they fail, and dynamically adjust routing to minimize fuel consumption, de-risking major operational and financial decisions.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Small Marina's AI Operations Kit." This digital product would guide non-technical marina owners on how to implement low-cost AI tools for quoting, scheduling, and customer communication. It would include Notion templates for standardizing service offerings so they can be fed to an AI estimator and a guide to using AI for local SEO to attract new berth holders.

**Confidence:** high — A large, fragmented market of non-technical SMBs has clear, revenue-gating pain points (inefficient quoting, complex inventory, high fuel costs) that are being addressed by a new class of vertical-specific AI tools.

## Trend 2: AI-Automated IT Offboarding & Deprovisioning

**What's happening** — IT and security teams are adopting AI-powered tools to automate the deprovisioning of employee access to SaaS applications. This ensures that when an employee departs, their access to sensitive data is revoked completely and immediately, reducing the risk of insider threats and compliance failures.

**Why now** — Manual offboarding is slow and error-prone, with one study showing that only 34% of organizations revoke access on an employee's last day. This creates a significant security gap. AI-powered identity and SaaS management platforms like Josys and BetterCloud now integrate with HR systems (like Workday) to automatically trigger deprovisioning workflows the moment a termination is processed. These tools map all of a user's permissions and systematically revoke access, preventing "orphaned accounts" that pose a long-term risk.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "reactive deprovisioning" to "agentic" identity lifecycle management. An AI agent will continuously monitor an employee's role and behavior, not just their employment status. It will detect role changes or anomalous activity and proactively adjust permissions in real-time based on the principle of least privilege. When an employee is terminated, the AI will not only revoke access but will also automatically transfer file ownership, archive data according to retention policies, and generate a complete, audit-ready log of the entire offboarding process.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Startup's Secure Offboarding Kit." This digital product would provide a guide for small companies on how to set up a low-cost, automated deprovisioning workflow. It would include Notion templates for creating a SaaS application inventory and a checklist for conducting quarterly access reviews, helping founders implement security best practices without a dedicated IT team.

**Confidence:** high — A universal, high-risk business process (employee offboarding) with clear compliance and security drivers is being addressed by a new generation of automation tools that provide a direct and measurable reduction in risk.

## Trend 3: AI-Powered Clinical Trial Site Selection

**What's happening** — Pharmaceutical sponsors and contract research organizations (CROs) are increasingly using AI to select the best-performing research sites for clinical trials. These platforms analyze vast datasets to predict which sites will enroll patients fastest and produce high-quality data, addressing a major bottleneck in drug development.

**Why now** — Feasibility and site selection are primary drivers of trial delays and budget overruns, with 80% of trials failing to meet enrollment targets. AI is productizing this high-stakes decision process. Platforms like Ryght AI and Salesforce Life Sciences Cloud analyze historical trial performance, real-world patient data, and investigator experience to generate a targeted shortlist of high-potential sites. This data-driven approach is replacing manual, assumption-based methods and reducing study startup times from months to weeks.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "site selection" to "agentic" study startup. An AI agent will manage the entire site activation workflow. After identifying the optimal sites, it will automatically initiate contact, distribute feasibility questionnaires, manage the exchange of startup documents, and track progress against activation timelines. This will create a more predictive and efficient model for launching new trials, allowing sponsors to bring therapies to patients faster.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Emerging Biotech's AI Trial-Ready Kit." This digital product would guide small pharma and biotech companies on how to structure their clinical trial protocols so they can be easily analyzed by these AI site selection platforms. It would include Notion templates for defining protocol requirements and a guide to using AI for pre-screening to ensure the trial design is feasible before committing resources.

**Confidence:** high — A high-stakes B2B process with a massive cost of failure (trial delays cost ~$1M/day) is being addressed by AI tools that provide a clear, measurable ROI in speed and efficiency.

## Patterns I noticed today

- A change in a regulation's *enforcement* (e.g., removing a grace period) can be a more powerful market-creation event than the regulation itself.
- The best wedge against a bundled feature in a large platform (like an HRIS) is to be the best-in-class, most up-to-date solution for the single highest-risk component of that bundle.
- A product's value can be directly anchored to the specific dollar amount of the fine or loss it helps customers avoid.
