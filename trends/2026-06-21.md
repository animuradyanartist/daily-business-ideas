## Trend 1: AI-Automated Electronic Visit Verification (EVV) Compliance

**What's happening** — Home healthcare agencies are adopting AI-powered platforms to automate compliance with federal and state Electronic Visit Verification (EVV) mandates. These systems move beyond simple data capture to automate the entire workflow of matching verified visits to claims, identifying exceptions, and generating audit-ready documentation.

**Why now** — Stricter enforcement of the 21st Century Cures Act is now tying Medicaid reimbursements directly to EVV compliance, making it a critical cash flow issue for agencies. Platforms like HHAeXchange are introducing AI-powered "intelligent billing" that automatically flags and helps resolve EVV-related discrepancies before claims are submitted, reducing denial rates. This addresses a major pain point, as agencies can lose significant revenue due to mismatched visit records and billing codes.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from exception management to "agentic" revenue cycle management. AI agents will autonomously manage the EVV-to-billing pipeline, proactively identifying scheduling issues that could lead to compliance failures, automatically adjusting claims based on verified visit data, and submitting documentation for reimbursement with no human intervention required for standard visits. This creates a market for "EVV Compliance-in-a-Box" toolkits that teach agency owners how to implement and oversee these systems to ensure financial stability.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Home Health Agency's EVV Cash Flow Kit." This digital product would provide Notion and spreadsheet templates for tracking EVV compliance and managing claim exceptions, paired with a guide on how to use an affordable AI-powered platform to automate the billing and reconciliation process.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple recent sources confirm that state-level enforcement of EVV mandates is creating a non-discretionary need for AI tools that automate the complex and financially critical workflow of matching verified visits to billing claims.

## Trend 2: AI-Powered EV Charging Network Operations

**What's happening** — Electric vehicle (EV) charge point operators (CPOs) and fleet managers are deploying AI platforms to automate the operational management of charging infrastructure. These systems handle predictive maintenance, dynamic pricing, and energy load balancing to maximize uptime and profitability.

**Why now** — As the EV charging network scales, reliability has become a critical issue, with studies showing high failure rates for public chargers. In response, companies like EV-NRG and PIONIX are launching AI-powered management platforms that predict hardware failures before they happen, analyze usage patterns to set dynamic pricing, and integrate with energy grids to optimize charging times. This allows operators to improve charger availability and manage the complex interplay between vehicle demand and energy costs.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will move from predictive maintenance to "agentic" network orchestration. AI agents will autonomously manage entire charging networks, making real-time decisions on pricing based on demand, routing vehicles to available chargers, and even trading energy back to the grid during peak hours. This will create a market for "Charge Point Operator (CPO) Toolkits" that teach small business owners how to use these AI platforms to run profitable, reliable charging stations.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Aspiring CPO's AI Operations Guide." This digital product would provide financial modeling spreadsheets for a small charging business, paired with a guide on selecting and implementing an AI-powered management platform to optimize uptime and pricing strategy.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from April, May, and June 2026 confirm that AI is being deployed to solve the core operational challenges of reliability and profitability in the rapidly scaling EV charging industry.

## Trend 3: AI-Automated Grant Compliance & Post-Award Reporting

**What's happening** — Non-profits and research institutions are adopting AI platforms to automate the administrative workflows of post-award grant management. These tools focus on tracking expenditures, monitoring project milestones, and generating compliance reports for funders.

**Why now** — The administrative burden of managing grant funds and reporting on outcomes is a significant drain on non-profit resources. A new generation of AI-powered tools from companies like Grantable and Streamlyne are specifically designed to automate this "post-award" phase. They connect to financial systems to track spending against grant budgets and use generative AI to draft the narrative progress reports required by foundations and government agencies, turning a multi-day writing process into a matter of hours.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from report generation to "agentic" grant management. AI agents will continuously monitor project data and financial systems, proactively flagging potential compliance issues, forecasting budget shortfalls, and automatically generating and submitting quarterly reports for human review. This creates a market for "Grant Management OS" toolkits that teach non-profit managers how to implement these systems to ensure compliance and free up resources for program delivery.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Non-Profit's Post-Award Toolkit." This digital product would provide a Notion-based system for tracking grant deliverables and budgets, paired with a guide and prompt library for using AI tools to automate the creation of funder-ready progress and financial reports.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple recent sources confirm a growing focus on AI tools that automate the high-friction, post-award phase of the grant lifecycle, moving beyond proposal writing to solve the persistent pain of compliance and reporting.

## Patterns I noticed today

-   The most powerful business case for a vertical SaaS tool is when it directly gates revenue based on a non-discretionary, technical compliance requirement.
-   As government mandates (like EVV) move from initial rollout to strict, automated enforcement, the nature of the market's pain shifts from "annoying paperwork" to "critical cash flow threat," creating new opportunities for focused software.
-   The "worse substitute" for compliance software is often not a direct competitor, but a human administrator. However, when the compliance failure becomes programmatic (i.e., an automatic claim denial), the human substitute becomes ineffective, creating a powerful wedge for a software solution.
