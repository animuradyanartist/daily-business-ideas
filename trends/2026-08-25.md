## Trend 1: AI-Automated SOC 2 Compliance

**What's happening** — B2B startups are adopting AI-powered platforms to automate the evidence collection and continuous monitoring required for SOC 2 compliance. These tools integrate with a company's cloud infrastructure and SaaS applications to automatically gather proof of security controls, replacing a manual, time-consuming process.

**Why now** — Achieving a SOC 2 report is a critical sales enabler for any B2B company handling customer data, yet the process is a major resource drain. In response, a new generation of "agentic" compliance platforms from companies like Drata and Vanta are using AI to autonomously collect evidence, monitor controls, and maintain an audit-ready posture. This shift is turning a point-in-time audit scramble into a continuous, automated background process.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "evidence collection" to "agentic" remediation. An AI agent will not only detect a compliance failure (e.g., a misconfigured S3 bucket) but will also analyze the risk, propose a specific fix, and, with human approval, apply the remediation directly. This will collapse the time between detection and resolution, creating a self-healing compliance posture and significantly reducing the workload on small engineering teams.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Founder's SOC 2 Readiness Kit." This digital product would provide a guide for non-technical founders on how to prepare their company for using an AI compliance platform. It would include Notion templates for defining security policies and a step-by-step guide to conducting a manual readiness assessment before engaging with an auditor or automation vendor.

**Confidence:** high — SOC 2 is a revenue-gating, non-negotiable requirement for B2B SaaS. AI tools that dramatically reduce the cost and complexity of achieving this have an exceptionally clear ROI.

## Trend 2: AI-Automated IT Offboarding & Deprovisioning

**What's happening** — IT and security teams are using AI-powered automation to manage employee offboarding, specifically the critical task of revoking access to dozens of SaaS applications. These platforms connect to HR and identity systems to trigger automated deprovisioning workflows the moment an employee's status changes.

**Why now** — Departing employees with persistent SaaS access is a major security and compliance risk. Manually deprovisioning accounts across a sprawling SaaS stack is slow and error-prone. In response, SaaS Management Platforms like BetterCloud and workflow automation tools like Zapier are using AI and API integrations to automate this process. When an employee is terminated in the HR system, the workflow automatically revokes active sessions, reclaims software licenses, and closes accounts, preventing security gaps without a manual checklist.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "workflow automation" to "agentic" identity governance. An AI agent will continuously monitor the entire SaaS environment, correlating user activity with HR data. It will not just deprovision users at offboarding but will also proactively identify and flag "identity drift"—such as a user accumulating excessive permissions or an account showing anomalous activity—and recommend rightsizing or suspension in real-time, moving from a reactive offboarding trigger to a continuous, proactive security model.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Startup's Secure Offboarding Kit." This digital product would provide a guide for early-stage companies on how to establish a secure offboarding process before they can afford an expensive automation platform. It would include Notion templates for conducting a SaaS access audit and a step-by-step checklist for manual deprovisioning.

**Confidence:** high — A universal business process (employee offboarding) has a direct and severe security and compliance risk. AI tools that provide a reliable, auditable, and automated solution have a clear value proposition.

## Trend 3: AI-Powered Clinical Trial Site Selection

**What's happening** — Pharmaceutical companies and contract research organizations (CROs) are using AI platforms to select the best-performing research sites for their clinical trials. These tools analyze vast datasets to predict a site's enrollment speed, data quality, and operational capacity, replacing a slow, relationship-based process with a data-driven one.

**Why now** — Failing to meet enrollment timelines is a primary cause of clinical trial delays, costing millions. Traditional site selection relies on manual, time-consuming feasibility assessments. In response, AI platforms from companies like Ryght AI and Salesforce are being used to analyze historical trial performance data, investigator expertise, and patient populations to identify the optimal sites for a specific study protocol. This data-first approach accelerates study startup and helps ensure trials are placed at sites with a high probability of success.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "site selection" to "agentic" site management. An AI agent will continuously monitor a site's performance in real-time throughout a trial. It will analyze data from various sources to detect early warning signs of underperformance, such as slow data entry or a dip in patient screening rates. The agent will then proactively alert the clinical research associate (CRA) and suggest specific interventions, turning site monitoring from a reactive, visit-based activity into a proactive, continuous process.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Research Coordinator's AI Readiness Kit." This digital product would guide staff at clinical research sites on how to structure and present their site's operational data to be more attractive to the AI-powered selection tools used by sponsors. It would include Notion templates for creating a "Site Performance Resume" and a guide to best practices for data collection and reporting.

**Confidence:** high — Clinical trials are a multi-billion dollar industry where delays have massive financial consequences. AI tools that can demonstrably reduce these delays by solving the critical bottleneck of site selection and performance have a clear and compelling ROI.

## Patterns I noticed today

- The price point of a market's dominant incumbents creates a "price umbrella" that can define the opportunity for a new, unbundled entrant. The $15k+ price for Vanta/Drata makes a $3.6k product seem inexpensive.
- A problem that is a direct blocker to revenue (like needing SOC 2 to close deals) is an order of magnitude more painful than a problem that merely impacts profitability or efficiency.
- The most labor-intensive part of a complex process is often the best piece to unbundle into a focused software product.
- A product's moat can be built from the customer's own operational data, which they create through the act of using the product, leading to powerful switching costs.
