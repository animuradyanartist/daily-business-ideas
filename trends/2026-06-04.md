## Trend 1: AI-Powered Qualitative Data Analysis

**What's happening** — Academic and corporate researchers are adopting a new class of AI tools to automate the analysis of qualitative data like interview transcripts and open-ended survey responses. These platforms automate the time-consuming process of thematic analysis, which involves identifying, coding, and reporting on patterns in text.

**Why now** — Manually coding large volumes of qualitative data is a major bottleneck in research. A new category of purpose-built AI tools is emerging to accelerate this workflow. Platforms like Marvin, Thematic, and Conveo ingest raw text and automatically suggest codes and themes, reducing analysis time from weeks to minutes. Legacy academic software like NVivo and MAXQDA are also integrating AI assistants to support, but not replace, manual coding workflows, indicating a broad industry shift. This allows researchers to handle larger datasets and focus on interpretation rather than manual organization.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will move from simple theme detection to AI-driven insight generation. These platforms will connect qualitative themes to quantitative business metrics (like NPS or churn) and generate first-draft narrative reports with supporting quotes. The most advanced tools will offer AI-moderated interviews that flow directly into an automated analysis pipeline, creating a market for "Research Scaffolding Kits"—digital products that teach teams how to manage this entire AI-driven research lifecycle.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator can build and sell "The UX Researcher's AI Analysis Toolkit." This digital product could provide a Notion-based system for organizing research data, paired with a library of prompts and guides for using AI tools to analyze interview transcripts, identify user pain points, and generate presentation-ready insights.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple product comparisons, guides, and platform updates from March, April, and May 2026 confirm the rapid development and adoption of AI tools specifically for thematic analysis of qualitative data.

## Trend 2: AI-Automated Grant Lifecycle Management

**What's happening** — Non-profits are adopting AI-powered platforms that manage the entire grant lifecycle, moving beyond just proposal writing to include post-award reporting and compliance. These tools are helping organizations spend less time on administration and more time on their mission.

**Why now** — Grant management is a high-friction, manual process. After winning a grant, non-profits face significant reporting requirements to prove impact, which consumes staff time. A new class of AI tools is automating this end-to-end workflow. Platforms from Benevity, Instrumentl, and HYS Grants now use AI not only to find and write grant proposals but also to summarize applications for reviewers and track post-award compliance. This addresses the full operational challenge, with some nonprofits reporting up to 60% less time spent on reporting.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from workflow automation to predictive fundraising. AI agents will analyze a non-profit's program data and past successful grants to proactively identify new, high-probability funding opportunities. These systems will then automatically generate tailored proposals and impact reports in the preferred format of each specific funder, creating a market for "Non-Profit Ops Toolkits" that teach small teams how to build a semi-automated fundraising and reporting engine.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator can build and sell "The Small Non-Profit's AI Grant Reporting Kit." This digital product could provide a Notion-based system for tracking grant deadlines and impact metrics, paired with guides and templates for using AI tools to generate compliant, data-driven reports for funders.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple product launches and industry articles from March, May, and June 2026 confirm the emergence of AI platforms that manage the full grant lifecycle, including the critical post-award reporting and compliance phases.

## Trend 3: AI-Automated Film & Video Pre-Production

**What's happening** — Independent filmmakers and video production teams are using AI-powered platforms to automate the entire pre-production workflow. These tools ingest a script and automatically generate essential planning documents like breakdowns, schedules, and storyboards.

**Why now** — Pre-production is a manual, time-consuming process that involves breaking down a script element by element (characters, props, locations). A new class of AI tools like Filmustage, FilmPro.ai, and Ciaro Pro is automating this entire sequence. They parse a screenplay and generate a full production breakdown, shooting schedule, and even AI-generated storyboards and concept art in minutes, compressing what used to take days of work. This dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for small crews, allowing them to achieve a level of planning that was previously only possible for large studios.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from generating static documents to creating a "living" pre-production environment. AI agents will act as virtual line producers, automatically re-calculating schedules and budgets as script changes are made. These systems will connect pre-production planning directly to on-set tools, allowing for real-time updates and decision-making. This will create a market for "Solo Filmmaker Production Kits"—digital products that teach creators how to use these AI platforms to manage an entire production from script to final cut.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator can build and sell "The Video Creator's AI Pre-Production OS." This digital product could provide a Notion-based system for managing a video project, combined with a guide and checklists for using AI tools to go from a simple script to a complete, professional shot list, schedule, and storyboard.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from March, April, and May 2026 confirm the launch and adoption of integrated AI platforms that automate the entire film pre-production workflow, from script breakdown to scheduling and storyboarding.

## Status update: The AI-Augmented Second Brain

**Originally flagged:** 2026-04-29

**Current status:** 🚀 accelerating

**What changed** — The market is maturing and bifurcating. Notion is doubling down on cloud-based, collaborative AI agents, releasing a new Developer Platform in May 2026 that allows external AI agents to run code and build directly within a workspace. In contrast, the Obsidian ecosystem is focusing on local-first, privacy-centric AI, with a thriving marketplace of community plugins that support local LLMs and keep all data on the user's device. This creates a clear choice for users: collaborative intelligence in the cloud (Notion) versus private, user-controlled intelligence on-device (Obsidian).

## Patterns I noticed today

- The most valuable AI tools are those that automate an entire professional *workflow*, not just a single task. Whether it's grant management, qualitative research, or film production, the trend is toward integrated platforms that manage a process from end to end.
- For every complex, high-friction professional workflow, a market is emerging for "Scaffolding Kits"—digital products that teach non-experts how to use the new generation of AI platforms to achieve a specific outcome.
- The value proposition of AI is shifting from generating novel content to imposing structure on complex, unstructured information. The AI's primary job is to act as an organizational and analytical layer on top of existing data.
