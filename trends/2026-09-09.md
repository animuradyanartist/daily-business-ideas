## Trend 1: AI-Powered Veterinary Practice Operations

**What's happening** — Veterinary practices are adopting AI platforms to automate their entire operational workflow, from clinical documentation to client communication and practice management. These tools are specifically trained on veterinary terminology and workflows to reduce the heavy administrative burden on clinicians.

**Why now** — Veterinarians spend 25-35% of their working time on documentation, a primary driver of burnout in a profession facing staffing shortages and rising demand. In response, a new class of AI-powered scribes and practice management systems from companies like HappyDoc, CoVet, and VetGenius is gaining traction. These tools use ambient listening to automatically generate structured SOAP notes, integrate directly with Practice Information Management Systems (PIMS), and automate client communication, saving hours of manual work per day. The adoption of AI scribes alone has reportedly increased fivefold in 14 months.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "documentation automation" to "agentic" practice intelligence. An AI agent will not just write notes but will act as a clinical and operational co-pilot. It will analyze a patient's complete history to surface key information before an exam, suggest differential diagnoses based on recorded symptoms, and flag missed revenue opportunities by comparing services rendered against best-practice protocols. This will transform the AI from a time-saving tool into a proactive system for improving both clinical outcomes and practice profitability.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could develop "The Vet Practice AI Implementation Kit." This digital product would provide practice managers with a toolkit for evaluating and adopting AI scribes and operational software. It would include Notion templates for comparing vendor features, a guide for structuring clinical templates to optimize AI note-taking, and checklists for training staff on the new workflows.

**Confidence:** high — A large, fragmented market of licensed professionals faces a clear, expensive administrative bottleneck. AI platforms that directly reduce non-billable work have a powerful and easily quantifiable ROI.

## Trend 2: AI-Powered "Team Brain" Across Multiple Apps

**What's happening** — Companies are deploying AI-powered enterprise search platforms to create a single, conversational "team brain" that unifies scattered knowledge from disparate applications like Slack, Google Drive, Notion, and Jira.

**Why now** — Employees waste hours each week searching for internal information, costing a 1,000-person company over $5 million annually. Traditional knowledge bases are difficult to maintain. In response, AI platforms from companies like Glean, GoSearch, and Notion AI connect to a company's existing apps and use generative AI to provide direct, source-cited answers to natural language questions. This allows an employee to ask "What did we decide about pricing?" and get a synthesized answer with links to the specific Slack messages, meeting notes, and documents where the decision was made.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "question answering" to "agentic" workflow execution. The team brain will not just find information but will use it to take action across applications. A user will give a multi-step command like, "Find the latest sales deck, create a new version in Google Slides for the Q4 prospect list from Salesforce, and share it in the #sales channel on Slack." The AI agent will then execute this entire cross-app workflow autonomously, acting as an operational assistant that understands the company's full context.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could develop "The Remote Team's AI Brain Setup Guide." This digital product would provide a toolkit for operations managers at small companies to structure their internal knowledge for optimal use by these AI platforms. It would include Notion templates for creating a "single source of truth" documentation style and a guide for establishing data governance rules before connecting all company apps to an AI.

**Confidence:** high — This trend solves a universal, expensive, and high-frequency problem for businesses of all sizes. The ROI is directly tied to employee productivity and faster decision-making.

## Trend 3: AI-Automated SOC 2 Compliance

**What's happening** — Companies are adopting AI-native platforms to automate the high-friction workflows required for SOC 2 compliance. These tools continuously collect evidence from a company's tech stack, map it to specific SOC 2 controls, and use AI agents to review evidence and draft compliance documentation.

**Why now** — Achieving SOC 2 compliance is a mandatory sales gate for B2B SaaS companies, but the manual process is slow and expensive, costing upwards of $50,000 for a first audit. While first-generation compliance automation tools from vendors like Vanta and Drata automated evidence collection, a new wave of AI-native platforms from companies like Strac, Scytale, and Delve is emerging. These tools use AI agents to not only collect evidence but also to review it, flag gaps, and write policy drafts, further reducing the manual work required from engineering and compliance teams.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "evidence automation" to "agentic" remediation. An AI agent will not just flag a compliance failure (e.g., an S3 bucket made public) but will autonomously remediate it based on pre-approved policies. The agent will document the detection, the fix, and generate the evidence of remediation, creating a closed-loop compliance system. The human operator's role will shift from manually fixing issues to defining and governing the policies that the AI agent executes.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could develop "The Founder's Guide to AI-Powered SOC 2." This digital product would provide a toolkit for early-stage startups to prepare for their first AI-assisted SOC 2 audit. It would include Notion templates for a lightweight risk assessment and pre-written policy starters that can be fed into an AI platform to generate a full set of compliant company policies.

**Confidence:** high — A non-discretionary, expensive, and recurring business requirement is being made faster and cheaper with AI. The value proposition is directly tied to unblocking enterprise sales deals.

## Patterns I noticed today

- The most powerful B2B SaaS ideas often live at the intersection of a high-frequency, annoying task and a low-probability, catastrophic-risk event. The solution sells on both daily efficiency and as a form of business-ending-risk insurance.
- A fragmented market of small, independent professionals (like vets) is a feature for a product-led SaaS, as they are underserved by enterprise sales models and more likely to adopt a self-serve tool that solves a key pain point.
- The price of a "worse substitute" (like a $50,000 manual audit or hours of unpaid documentation time) provides a powerful anchor for SaaS pricing. A product that is 10x better and 1/10th the price of the alternative is an easy value proposition.
