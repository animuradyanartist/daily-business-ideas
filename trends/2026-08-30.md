## Trend 1: AI-Automated ISO 27001 Compliance

**What's happening** — Companies are adopting AI-powered platforms to automate the evidence collection, control monitoring, and risk management required for ISO 27001, the international standard for information security. These tools connect directly to a company's cloud infrastructure and SaaS stack to maintain a continuously audit-ready posture.

**Why now** — ISO 27001 is shifting from a nice-to-have to a mandatory requirement for B2B sales, especially in the EU. The manual process of gathering evidence and managing documentation for the 93 controls in the ISO 27001:2022 standard is a major resource drain. In response, compliance automation platforms from vendors like Vanta, Drata, and Scytale are using AI agents to automatically collect evidence, map it to controls, and even draft auditor-ready narratives, reducing audit preparation time by up to 80%.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "evidence collection" to "agentic" security governance. An AI agent will not just flag a compliance gap (e.g., a user with excessive permissions) but will also analyze the associated risk, propose a specific remediation in line with the company's policies, and, with human approval, execute the fix via API. This will create a self-healing security posture where compliance drift is corrected in near real-time.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Founder's ISO 27001 Readiness Kit." This digital product would provide a guide for non-technical founders on how to prepare their company for using an AI compliance platform. It would include Notion templates for defining security policies and a step-by-step guide to conducting a manual readiness assessment before engaging an automation vendor.

**Confidence:** high — ISO 27001 is a revenue-gating, non-negotiable requirement for global B2B SaaS. AI tools that dramatically reduce the cost and complexity of achieving this have an exceptionally clear ROI.

## Trend 2: AI-Automated Commercial Real Estate (CRE) Underwriting

**What's happening** — Commercial real estate investors and lenders are using AI platforms to automate the analysis of potential deals. These tools ingest unstructured documents like offering memorandums and rent rolls, extract key financial data, and populate underwriting models in minutes, not days.

**Why now** — The CRE investment workflow is notoriously manual, with analysts spending most of their time on low-value data entry. With over 88% of investors initiating AI programs in 2025, the industry has reached a tipping point. AI platforms from companies like Blooma and Aloan are now being used to automate the financial "spreading" process, allowing underwriters to analyze four times as many deals and focus their time on higher-judgment tasks like sponsor evaluation and market assessment.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "document extraction" to "agentic" deal sourcing and screening. An investment firm will provide an AI agent with its specific buy-box criteria (e.g., "Class B multifamily in the Southeast with value-add potential"). The agent will then autonomously scan deal flow from multiple sources, perform a preliminary underwriting on promising deals, and present the human team with a ranked shortlist of opportunities that meet their exact mandate.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The CRE Analyst's AI Toolkit." This digital product would teach junior analysts and brokers how to use these new AI platforms to accelerate their work. It would include Notion templates for structuring deal memos and a guide with prompts for using AI to identify risks and opportunities in a property's financial statements.

**Confidence:** high — A massive industry has a clear, time-consuming bottleneck (manual data extraction for underwriting). AI tools offer a direct and measurable ROI by increasing deal throughput.

## Trend 3: AI-Automated Medical Device Quality Management Systems (QMS)

**What's happening** — Medical device companies are adopting AI-powered platforms to automate the creation and maintenance of their Quality Management System (QMS) documentation, which is required for regulatory approval from bodies like the FDA.

**Why now** — A major regulatory shift is the primary driver. As of February 2, 2026, the FDA's Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR) now incorporates the international standard ISO 13485 by reference. This harmonization forces companies to update their entire QMS. AI-powered QMS platforms are emerging to automate this transition, using AI to perform gap analyses, draft procedures, and maintain traceability between design controls, risk analysis, and testing evidence, ensuring the system is audit-ready.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from "document generation" to "agentic" quality assurance. An AI agent will continuously monitor the entire product lifecycle. It will analyze post-market surveillance data from sources like complaints and social media to detect early signals of product performance issues. The agent will then automatically initiate a Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) workflow within the QMS, assigning tasks and tracking the investigation, acting as a proactive, autonomous quality analyst.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The MedTech Founder's QMSR Transition Kit." This digital product would provide a guide for early-stage medical device startups on how to prepare for an AI-powered QMS. It would include Notion templates for mapping existing procedures to the new ISO 13485 framework and a checklist for ensuring all documentation is in place before implementation.

**Confidence:** high — A mandatory, deadline-driven regulatory change is forcing an entire high-value industry to re-tool its core compliance infrastructure. The cost of failure is being barred from the U.S. market.
