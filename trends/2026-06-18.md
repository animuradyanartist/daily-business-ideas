## Trend 1: AI Co-pilots for Executive Assistants

**What's happening** — Executive Assistants (EAs) are adopting AI-powered tools that automate core administrative workflows, moving beyond single-task assistance to integrated operational support. These co-pilots handle email triage, calendar management, meeting preparation, and document summarization.

**Why now** — The EA role is evolving from administrative support to a strategic partnership, and AI is automating the routine tasks to enable this shift. Platforms like Microsoft's Copilot Cowork and dedicated tools like Motion and Sai by Simular are being deployed to manage complex scheduling, draft morning briefings from emails and calendars, and prepare meeting agendas. These tools can reduce the manual workload for tasks like meeting prep from hours to minutes, allowing EAs to focus on higher-value activities like project management and stakeholder relations.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will move from AI as a "co-pilot" an EA uses to an "AI employee" the EA manages. These AI agents will autonomously handle multi-step workflows, such as processing an inbound contract from email, summarizing key terms, adding a review deadline to the calendar, and assigning a follow-up task to the legal team, with the EA providing oversight and approval. This creates a market for "AI-Powered EA" playbooks that teach assistants how to build, manage, and govern these digital team members.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The AI-Powered Executive Assistant's OS," a digital product with Notion templates for managing executive workflows (e.g., meeting pipelines, daily briefings) and a guide on how to configure and chain together affordable AI tools to automate them.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from April, May, and June 2026 confirm the rapid adoption of AI tools specifically designed for EA workflows, with a clear focus on automating multi-step administrative processes to free up EAs for more strategic work.

## Trend 2: AI-Automated Client Communications for Financial Advisors

**What's happening** — Registered Investment Advisors (RIAs) are adopting AI platforms to automate and scale client communications while managing strict regulatory compliance. These tools draft personalized emails, prepare meeting notes, and ensure adherence to SEC and FINRA rules.

**Why now** — Advisors face dual pressures: clients expect highly personalized, timely communication, while regulators are increasing scrutiny of all client interactions. AI tools from companies like Altitude CRM, Savvy Wealth, and Jump AI are being deployed to automate the administrative work around communication, such as generating meeting summaries, drafting follow-up emails, and updating CRM records. This addresses a key industry pain point, as 75% of clients want personalized updates. Simultaneously, the SEC has explicitly named AI as a 2026 examination focus, forcing firms to adopt governable AI solutions for compliance.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from AI as a "drafting assistant" to an "agentic" communication manager. AI agents will monitor client portfolios and market data to proactively suggest and draft communications for key events, like a market downturn or a significant portfolio milestone. This creates a market for "AI-Powered RIA" toolkits that provide frameworks and prompt libraries for using these AI systems to deliver personalized advice at scale without breaching compliance.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Independent Advisor's AI Communication Kit." This digital product would provide a Notion-based system with email templates and communication workflows, paired with a guide on how to use AI tools to generate personalized client updates that are both effective and compliant.

**Confidence:** high — Numerous sources from April, May, and June 2026 confirm that RIAs are adopting AI to solve the dual problem of scaling personalized client communication while managing increased regulatory oversight.

## Trend 3: AI-Automated IT Service Management (ITSM)

**What's happening** — Enterprise IT departments are deploying "AI-native" service management platforms to automate the entire lifecycle of internal support tickets. These systems use AI to interpret requests, resolve common issues autonomously, and route complex problems without human intervention.

**Why now** — IT support teams are overwhelmed by a high volume of repetitive requests (e.g., password resets, access provisioning), leading them to adopt AI as a "force multiplier." A new generation of "AI-native" ITSM platforms from companies like Serval and Konverso AI are moving beyond simple chatbots to provide end-to-end resolution. These systems can reportedly achieve auto-resolution rates of up to 84% for support requests, handling ticket creation, categorization, and remediation automatically. This shifts the ITSM model from a reactive, manual queue to a proactive, "self-healing" system.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will move from automating *known* issues to "agentic" problem management. AI agents will continuously analyze operational data to predict and prevent incidents before they occur, automatically creating and deploying fixes for novel issues. This creates a market for "ITSM Governance Toolkits"—digital products that teach IT managers how to configure, oversee, and audit these increasingly autonomous support systems.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The SMB's Guide to AI-Powered IT Support." This digital product would offer a Notion-based system for creating an internal knowledge base and managing IT tickets, paired with a guide on how to implement a low-cost AI ITSM tool to automate common employee support requests.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from April, May, and June 2026 confirm a clear market shift from traditional ITSM to AI-native platforms that provide end-to-end, autonomous resolution of IT support issues.

## Patterns I noticed today

- The most valuable B2B AI tools automate an entire, high-friction professional workflow (executive support, client communication, IT helpdesk), not just a single, isolated task.
- For every professional workflow being automated, a secondary market is emerging for "governance toolkits"—digital products that teach non-technical owners how to implement, manage, and oversee these new semi-autonomous AI systems.
- A compliance mandate (from the SEC, FINRA, etc.) is a powerful catalyst for AI adoption, creating a clear, non-discretionary business case for tools that can ensure auditable adherence to rules.
