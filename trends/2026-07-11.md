## Trend 1: AI-Automated Operations for Shopify B2B

**What's happening** — A new class of AI tools is emerging to automate the specific operational workflows for Shopify merchants using the platform's recently expanded B2B capabilities. These tools manage wholesale pricing, customer-specific catalogs, and B2B order processing.

**Why now** — In April 2026, Shopify made its native B2B feature set, previously exclusive to high-cost Shopify Plus plans, available to all paid plans. This instantly created a massive new market of small and mid-sized merchants who can now run wholesale operations but lack the sophisticated tools to manage them. In response, AI-powered apps are launching to automate B2B-specific tasks like dynamic pricing, wholesale customer onboarding, and managing net payment terms.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will move from single-function automation (e.g., pricing) to "agentic" wholesale management. AI agents will run the entire B2B channel for a Shopify store, from identifying and approving wholesale applicants to proactively generating re-stocking orders for existing partners based on their sales velocity. This creates a market for "The Shopify B2B Launch Kit," a digital product teaching merchants how to set up and automate their new wholesale channel using these AI tools.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator with e-commerce knowledge can build a digital toolkit that includes Canva templates for wholesale line sheets and a Notion-based guide for implementing an AI-powered B2B automation stack on a standard Shopify plan.

**Confidence:** high — A significant platform shift by Shopify in April 2026 created a new, well-defined customer segment with an immediate need for operational tooling. Multiple sources confirm that AI app developers are actively building solutions for this new B2B ecosystem.

## Trend 2: AI-Automated Local SEO Management

**What's happening** — Small, local businesses are adopting AI-powered platforms to automate the high-friction, continuous work of local search engine optimization. These tools manage Google Business Profile (GBP) optimization, review responses, and local citation consistency.

**Why now** — Google Business Profile is no longer a static listing; it's a dynamic data feed for AI systems that prioritize freshness and engagement. AI Overviews now appear in over 40% of search queries, and AI platforms have become the third most popular source for local business recommendations. This shift requires constant activity—responding to reviews, posting updates, and ensuring data consistency—which is a major operational burden for small business owners. In response, AI tools are emerging to automate these tasks, from generating localized content to managing review responses and auditing NAP (Name, Address, Phone) consistency across directories.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will evolve from task automation to "agentic" local marketing. AI agents will not only optimize a business's existing online presence but will proactively identify and execute local marketing opportunities. This includes generating hyperlocal content based on community events, managing automated ad campaigns on local platforms, and providing predictive insights on customer foot traffic and seasonal demand.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Local Business AI Visibility Kit." This digital product would provide a Notion-based system for managing local marketing activities, paired with a guide and prompt library for using AI tools to automate Google Business Profile updates, review responses, and social media content.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from May, June, and July 2026 confirm that the rise of AI-driven search is forcing a fundamental change in local SEO tactics, creating a clear and urgent need for automation tools that manage the required increase in activity.

## Trend 3: AI-Powered "Team Brain" Across Multiple Apps

**What's happening** — A new category of AI tools is emerging to create a unified, searchable "team brain" by connecting to a company's fragmented knowledge sources like Slack, Notion, Google Drive, and Jira. These platforms provide a single interface to ask questions and receive synthesized, cited answers from across all connected apps.

**Why now** — Knowledge fragmentation is a major drag on productivity, with the average support team searching across 10-15 systems to find a single answer. Traditional knowledge bases require manual curation. In response, AI-native platforms are using retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to search live data across multiple sources simultaneously. Tools like Slite, Nairi, and Kollab allow a user to ask a question in Slack (e.g., "What's the status of the mobile onboarding project?") and receive a single answer synthesized from the relevant Notion docs, Jira tickets, and Slack conversations, complete with links to the source material.

**Forecast (3–6 months)** — The trend will move from reactive Q&A to proactive, "agentic" knowledge work. AI agents will monitor project activity across all connected apps and proactively assemble and distribute briefings, flag conflicting information between documents, and automatically update outdated wikis based on recent decisions made in Slack or Jira. The "team brain" will become a system that not only answers questions but anticipates them.

**Who rides this** — A solo creator could build "The Remote Team's AI OS." This digital product would provide a Notion-based framework for knowledge management hygiene (e.g., how to structure documents for better AI retrieval), paired with a guide on selecting and implementing an AI-powered team brain tool to reduce repetitive questions and accelerate onboarding.

**Confidence:** high — Multiple sources from June and July 2026 describe a competitive and rapidly maturing market for AI tools that solve the specific, high-pain problem of cross-platform knowledge retrieval for teams.

## Status update: AI-Generated Business Presentations

**Originally flagged:** 2026-05-03

**Current status:** 🚀 accelerating

**What changed** — The market for AI presentation generators has matured from novelty prompt-to-deck tools to a segmented ecosystem of specialized platforms. The conversation is no longer about whether AI can make slides, but which tool fits a specific workflow. Key differentiators now include native integration (Plus AI for Google Slides, Microsoft Copilot for PowerPoint), narrative and argument structuring (Storyflow, Perceptis AI), and speed of first draft (Gamma). The primary limitation remains the gap between the AI's first draft and a client-ready deliverable, which still requires significant human editing.

## Patterns I noticed today

- A platform's expansion of native features (like Shopify adding B2B to all plans) can be a massive opportunity, not a threat. It educates the market, expands the TAM, and creates demand for more powerful, third-party "pro" versions.
- For platform-based businesses, distribution is a solved problem. The challenge shifts entirely to product quality and user experience. The winner is the one who can out-execute on product, not the one who can out-market.
- A "revenue-enabling" tool will almost always have a higher willingness-to-pay and lower churn than a "cost-saving" or "efficiency" tool.
