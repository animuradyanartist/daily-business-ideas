# Market: US Small Law Firm Client Intake Software
slug: us-small-law-firm-intake-software

## 2026-08-28 revision — idea: The Small Law Firm's "Never-Drop-a-Lead" Intake Funnel (score: 99)
- TAM/SAM/SOM: TAM: $32B+ US legal tech market. SAM: 100,000 small firms x $3k ACV = $300M. SOM (Y2): 250 firms = $750k ARR. Method: # of target firms x plausible ACV. Sources: Grand View Research, Fortune Business Insights.
- Growth: Market growing at 9-12% CAGR. Driven by increasing adoption of technology to improve efficiency and client service. Source: Grand View Research.
- Fragmentation: Buyer side is extremely fragmented. The majority of US lawyers work in firms of 10 or fewer, making them ideal for a self-serve SaaS model.
- Margin pool: Margin sits with the software provider that can solve the highest-value problem. Intake is a direct revenue-leakage problem, justifying premium software pricing relative to development cost.
- Distribution control: Incumbents like Clio have strong distribution through their existing user base. New entrants must rely on content marketing, paid acquisition, and partnerships with adjacent service providers (e.g., marketing agencies).
- Incumbents and inertia: Incumbents (Clio, MyCase) offer intake as a feature in a complex suite. Their weakness is that the complexity leads to poor adoption, which is proven by the widespread industry data on dropped leads. This creates an opening for a simple, best-in-class point solution.
- Regulatory / platform risk: Minimal. No specific regulatory hurdles. No dependency on a single platform.
