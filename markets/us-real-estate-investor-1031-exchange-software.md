## 2026-08-13 revision — idea: The Real Estate Investor's "1031 Exchange" Deadline Tracker (score: 98)
- TAM/SAM/SOM: TAM: ~$200B+ in annual transaction value. SAM: 100,000 annual exchanges × $299 fee = $29.9M. SOM (Y2): 1,000 investors x $299 = $299,000.
- Growth: Transaction volume fluctuates with the real estate market, but the underlying tax law and pain are constant. Trend is toward more professionalized, tech-enabled services.
- Fragmentation: The end buyers (investors) are highly fragmented. The distribution channel (Qualified Intermediaries) is more concentrated and is the key access point.
- Margin pool: Margin sits with the Qualified Intermediaries who charge a mandatory fee ($750-$1500) and legal/tax advisors. This tool captures a small piece by providing high-value risk mitigation.
- Distribution control: Qualified Intermediaries control customer access at the exact moment of need. Partnering with them is the most efficient path to market.
- Incumbents and inertia: Incumbent software (Appsparc, Exchange Manager Pro℠) serves the QI's back office, not the investor. Inertia (manual tracking) is the main competitor, but is weak due to the high cost of failure.
- Regulatory / platform risk: The entire market is dependent on Section 1031 of the Internal Revenue Code remaining in effect. A change in tax law is the primary systemic risk.
